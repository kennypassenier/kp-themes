kyu on kp-themes 11.0.0: the parts that cross pages

How kyu’s dashboard would look in the parts that belong to no single page: the command palette (Ctrl K), the keys and the help sheet, the first-visit tour, links that point at a heading or a row, and two ways to see who reads what. Everything here is mock data in kyu’s own shape (topics notify.kenny, ha.events, almanac.sync, jobtracker.mail, newsflash.items); nothing talks to a real kyu.

Judge in the review dialog, formal only. Every section says under Expected what to see and what each control does. The last two sections are the same question answered twice (a line diagram and a hub-and-ring picture): look at both.

Command palette: jump anywhere, publish, prune

Ctrl K opens one box on every kyu page. Type a topic or an app to go there, or a verb to do something: Publish a test message, Prune dead letters. It is shown open here, with its list and every state.

Expected:

  • Nothing typed: a short start, not the whole list: the two featured actions (Publish a test message, Prune dead letters) and, because this page is about notify.kenny, that topic’s own commands first. The status line says how many are shown.
  • Type for me types into the box: publish, dead prune (words in any order), desk notify (finds the subscription notify.kenny / desktop), runner, zzz (nothing matches: a plate that fills the list and names what you can search). The best answer comes first, the matched words are ruled under, Enter runs the first row and the line under the palette says what it would do.
  • State: Loading lays the busy card over the list; Failed shows the failed plate with a normal-size Retry while the always-available commands (Publish a test message) stay usable; Retry loads again and then shows the list.
  • Open as a modal (or Ctrl K) opens it as the package does on a page; Esc closes it and the sample comes back. The four choices in the dialog (matching, marks, one letter off, what an empty result shows) all change the palette at once.

Formal’s palette is the card with a strong boundary; the matched words are ruled under in navy and the active row is the soft wash of the ink. Look at the group labels (Do, Topics, Subscriptions) staying clear of the rows, the keys (p) at the end of a row, the plate and Retry filling the list’s height, and no text touching the card’s edge at 390 px.

Keys: g t, g a, /, p, the hint line and the ? sheet

Two letters take you to a page (g t topics, g a apps), / searches, p opens the publish box, and ? lists every key. The one list that handles the keys also fills the sheet and the hint line, so none of them can promise a key that does nothing.

Expected:

  • Press for me presses keys on the page for you: g t and g a (the readout says where kyu would go), g x (no such chord: the cap says so, or says nothing, per the choice), / (the search field gets focus) and p (the publish box gets focus). Typing a letter in a field never fires a shortcut.
  • Click a subscription in the list (or Tab into it) and press j / k to move, x to select. Shift R (requeue its dead letters) works only while something is selected; in the sheet it is dimmed with the reason Select a subscription first (x).
  • Sheet switches the open sheet between the page’s own keys (the list’s keys first, then Anywhere) and a page that registered nothing (the sheet says so). The ? key opens it as a modal.
  • The hint line under the list shows the same keys as small caps; Ctrl would be ⌘ on a Mac. The three choices (cap place, the unknown-chord word, hint line start or end) apply at once.

The sheet is formal’s own: ruled rows, keys in dark caps in one column on the left, meanings muted on the right, the dimmed row keeping its meaning readable and adding one small line saying why. The hint line caps are the same caps; the words never touch a cap.

Keyboard shortcuts

First-visit tour of the topic page

The first time a person opens a topic, a short tour walks over the page: the commands, the subscriptions, the dead letters, the publish box. It is remembered once taken, and the Help button (or ?tour in the address) starts it again.

Expected:

  • Running: a card stands beside the part it talks about (never over it), the rest of the page is dimmed, the part has a ring. The card says 2 of 5 and has Back, Skip and Next (Done on the last step). The left and right arrows and Esc work on the card; the page stays usable.
  • Visit as: First visit forgets the tour and starts it, as kyu would on a person’s first visit; Returning visit ends it and remembers it, so nothing shows; Opened with ?tour starts it whatever was remembered. The line under the page says whether it was taken before and whether it would start by itself.
  • Help (the button in the page’s bar) opens the help drawer, which has Take the 1-minute tour. A script-driven browser never starts the tour by itself, so a person sees it and a test does not.
  • Phone: at 390 px the card keeps 16 px from the window’s edges and follows its target when the page scrolls.

The card is formal’s plate with a strong boundary; the ringed part keeps its own colours while the rest is dimmed. Look at the count (2 of 5) and the three buttons sharing one row without a short strip, and the card never touching the ring.

Anchors on headings

A small # beside each section heading of a topic page. A press copies a link to exactly that heading; opening such a link scrolls there and marks it. A link to a heading that is gone says so.

Expected:

  • Point at a heading (or Tab onto its mark): a small # appears in the link colour. The page title and the small heading have none: only levels 2 and 3. Press the #: the mark itself says Copied for 1.5 seconds without changing its width; the readout shows what was copied. Shift + press copies a Markdown link.
  • Open a link: the article scrolls to the heading and marks it for two seconds. A link to a heading that is gone shows the notice That item is no longer here. with a button Show the whole list.
  • The long heading wraps inside its width and the mark keeps its own room at the end; the four choices in the dialog change the marks at once.

The mark is formal’s link ink in its mono face; the landing mark is the info colour as an outline with a faint wash. The mark never touches the heading’s last letter and the notice has air round its words.

Anchors on dead-letter rows: landing and “no longer here”

One failed message can be pointed at in a chat. Its link scrolls to the row and flashes it; when the message was requeued in the meantime, the page says it is no longer here instead of showing nothing.

Expected:

  • Open a link to a row: the table scrolls to it and the whole row is outlined in the info colour with a wash for two seconds. The # stands at the start of the first cell with air before the id; sorting by Reason keeps each mark on its message.
  • Requeue 3b71 removes that row from the table at once and the count updates (3 dead letters). A link to 3b71 then shows That item is no longer here. with Show the whole list (after a short wait: the page keeps a link for up to 10 seconds in case the rows are still loading). Reset brings the row back.
  • Rows: Loading lays the busy card over the table at its height, Empty shows the full-size plate (Nothing is dead), Failed a plate with a normal-size Retry. The mock counts the whole topic: 142 messages, never abbreviated.

The # is the same link-coloured mark as on headings; the landing row is outlined in the info colour with a faint wash across the whole row. In the narrow layout the mark stays visible and the row stays on one grid with its neighbours.

Who reads what, as a line diagram

kyu already knows every topic and subscription, so this needs no new backend work. Topics on one side, the apps that read them on the other, a line for each subscription, coloured by its state.

Expected:

  • Map: five topics, five apps, and a line for every subscription: notify.kenny reaches desktop (the line is in the danger colour: it has a dead letter) and phone (green); one amber line is an expired lease; the archive lines are muted. Each topic says its messages and how many subscriptions read it (142 messages, never 99+); newsflash.items has only an archive reader.
  • Point at a node: its lines light and thicken, the nodes it touches stay clear, everything else dims. Press to pin; press again or Esc to let go. Keys: ↑ ↓ move down a column, ← → jump to the other column, Enter opens the node’s link.
  • View switches to the grid (the same data as a table of ticks) and back; the pin stays. State: Loading lays the busy card over the map, Empty and Failed fill its area with a plate (Failed has a normal-size Retry that loads again). Under 640 px each topic lists its apps as chips instead of lines. The four choices in the dialog change the map at once.

White cards with a hairline and formal’s navy for a lit line; the danger line is readable beside the muted ones. No text touches a card’s edge, the count sits under the name, and a line meets its card at the middle.

Who reads what, as a hub and ring

The same answer drawn the other way, from the same data kyu already has. kyu is the hub, every subscription a node on the ring, and the link says the subscription’s state: active, flagged (a dead letter or an expired lease) or archived.

Expected:

  • The picture: kyu in the middle, ten subscriptions around it, each named topic / app. A solid line is active, a dashed one flagged (five of them: a dead letter on notify.kenny / desktop, ha.events / kyu-runner, almanac.sync / almanac and jobtracker.mail / desktop, an expired lease on newsflash.items / phone), a dotted one archived. The kinds are listed above the picture with a switch each; Show all clears a pick.
  • Point at a node: its links stay, the rest dim; press (or Enter) to pick it, the arrows walk the ring, Esc shows all again. Switch a kind off in the list and its links leave the picture. The readout under it says what is picked.
  • State: Loading draws the graph as waiting nodes and links; Empty and Failed show the package’s note in the picture’s area (this component has no plate or Retry button of its own yet: see the report). Live update changes one subscription’s state and its backlog while you watch; ids that stay keep their selection and the focus.

The hub is the strongest node; the ring nodes are white cards with a hairline; each link kind has its own dash and colour and the list above says which. Labels never touch a node’s edge or a line, and all ten names are fully readable.
