Homelab dashboard: operations — backups, restore, firewall, host and console

The operations half of the homelab admin dashboard on kp-themes 11.0.0, in 25 sections that each ask one decision: Backups with the coverage grid, the grouped repositories, the Month view, one night in detail and Restore (5); the Firewall with totals that filter, a hop-by-hop path trace, the rules table, lines against grid, live against files and a guard before editing (6); the Host and System with the disk and thin pool, the checks with their remedy, one refresh control, the containers, the landing tiles, versions and the line to the host (7); and the Console with its history, safety, session tools, pane or bar, the host log and the keys (7).

Mock on this page: all the data. The stacks and containers (traefik CT 104, jellyfin CT 106, kyu CT 109, almanac CT 112, metrics CT 113, inbox CT 118, admin CT 120, host pve), sizes, times, rules and log lines are made up in the shapes the dashboard reads (/data/backups/<stack>, /data/firewall, the doctor report, shellTargets()), and the page works the answers out from them. The clock stands at Monday 19/10/2026 14:05; the nightly round starts at 03:00. Nothing restores, applies or runs anything. Needs the host to change before it is real: a bounded history read for the 7, 30 and 90 night ranges (backend item C8), a job that reports bytes for the restore progress (C5), ping history for 24 hours and 90 days (C2) and a job cancel (C11); each is shown here with sample data. The Firewall page's topology figure and the Map's network drawing keep their own look and are not on this page. Judge in the review dialog; nothing moves until you press a button.

The coverage grid: every stack against every night

Shows, for each stack, whether a backup landed on each of the last 30 nights, so a missed night stands out at a glance and one press pins it.

Expected:

  • Thirteen stack rows (a small colour square before each name) and a Whole fleet row on top whose cells fill from the bottom by the share of stacks backed up. Thirty night columns, the last one 19/10. syncthing is named in a soft ink and its cells say keeps no data by design. grafana and admin start later: the nights before are before any history, not missed.
  • Missed nights are red and come with a reason on hover: loki on 14/10 (disk full), inbox on 17/10 and 18/10, almanac on 12/10, vaultwarden on 29/09. The fleet row for those nights is amber (some stacks missed).
  • Press a cell: its night is pinned (the line under the grid names it and who missed it); press again or Esc to unpin. Press a stack name: only that stack stays lit and the line says so (the page filters, the grid reports). Arrow keys move, Page Up and Page Down page, and so do the Earlier and Later buttons; Today goes back.
  • Loading lays the busy card over the whole grid; Empty and Failed fill the same height with a plate and a normal-size action (Reload). The legend and the paging buttons are the page's own (the package gets its legend helper later); they keep their place in every state.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): whether weekend nights read quiet. Both are real options, the buttons flip them, and nothing is required.

White grid on a hairline, navy for the pin, green and red cells with an amber fleet row where stacks differ. Look at: the red cells being distinguishable without colour (the hover card names them), the names of the stacks never touching the cells, the night numbers keeping a gap, and that the grid keeps its height with the legend and the buttons below it in every state.

The repositories: one grouped, expandable table

Lists every backup repository under its stack, shows which stack takes the space on the backup disk, and lets you open one to see its snapshots and restore from them.

Expected:

  • Above the table a bar splits 16 repositories, 165.7 GB of 500 GB by stack; press a part (or its legend row) and the table and the stack list on the left show only that stack. Press it again to show all. The list under the bar is the same filter with a swatch and pattern per stack, the number of repositories, and an only button.
  • Under each stack heading (mark, name, count, Restore… for the whole stack) its repositories stand in columns: Last 7 nights (one cell per night with its share and incidents above, hover a night for the time or why it is missing), Snapshots, Size (a bar relative to the biggest, sorted by the real number, so 9 GB sorts before 10.2 GB), Restore drill (failed, never drilled, passed) and Restore…, which stands in the same column on every row.
  • Press a row's arrow: its snapshots open under it (id, taken, age, size, Restore… and Show a file… per snapshot, in two fixed columns). The search, the filters (Stack, Restore drill) and the sort, with Shift for a second sort key, are remembered. Sort Restore drill: failed first, then never drilled, then passed.
  • Tick rows: a bar says 3 repositories selected with their names and Verify restore and Back up now. Pressing one shows the work (spinner and words), then the result; with Some refused (which ticks three rows and runs Back up now for you) it ends 2 done, 1 refused with the reason on the refused row.
  • Loading lays the busy card over the table, Empty and Failed fill the same height with a plate and Try again; the bar and the list follow. On a 390 px phone the rows become cards and each heading stays above its own cards.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): whether the stack groups start open or folded. Both are real; the buttons flip them.

Heading rows read as headings (a light ground, the stack mark, a count badge), the bar parts match the swatches in the list, and the buttons of every row share one column. Look at: the 7-night cells keeping a gap to their neighbours, the size bar not touching the number, the badges never breaking a word, and the table keeping its height in every state.
