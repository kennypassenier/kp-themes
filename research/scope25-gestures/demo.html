The scope-25 gestures beside what ships

At scope-25 (2026-09-11) Kenny approved one gesture for sepia and one for the shade pair; none was built. At scope-100 (2026-09-16) he asked to see them next to what ships before choosing. Each block below puts CURRENT, the shipped register with nothing added (package), beside PROPOSED, the same markup inside a .sg-proposed wrapper whose rules sit in this page's <style> and its last script (mock). Every proposal rule starts with the theme's own selector and moves into css/<theme>-register.css by dropping .sg-proposed. The buttons, cards, surfaces, dividers and the dossier stamp are package classes; the panel captions, the switcher and the "held" copies are mock. Blocks 1 and 2 are read in sepia, blocks 3 and 4 in shade-light and again in shade-dark; in any other theme CURRENT and PROPOSED are identical, which is the proof that nothing leaks. Loaded registers: sepia, shade-light, shade-dark. The line counts in the cost notes are read live from this page's source between each proposal's BEGIN and END markers: non-blank lines, comments included.

1 · sepia · the ink spreads on a press mock

This block is about sepia: switch the theme to sepia to judge it.

Look at: press and hold each PROPOSED button, then let go. A brown stain grows from the exact point you pressed, unevenly and with a feathered, fibrous edge, over the button's fill and out about 12px into the paper around it; it never covers the label, which stays where it was (no button moves or changes size). On release the stain sinks back into the paper over about a second. A quick click still leaves a visible stain that fades. With reduced motion the stain appears and disappears at once, at its full size. Compare CURRENT, where a press only darkens the primary fill and adds a faint inset shadow. The marginal bracket on hover (scope-12) stays in both.
"the ink spreading into the paper on a press" (scope-25, sepia)

2 · sepia · the rule thickest in the middle mock

This block is about sepia: switch the theme to sepia to judge it.

Look at: the heading rule under "Handover, pump house 4" and the two section dividers. CURRENT draws two even 1px lines (the double warm-brown rule) and a straight 2px heading rule. PROPOSED draws each as one stroke of sienna ink that swells to 3px in the middle and tapers to a hairline at both ends, like a printer's swelled rule; the second divider keeps its diamond, which now sits on the thickest part. Zoom in (or view at 225%): the stroke must be one continuous shape with no break, notch or lighter column anywhere along it, and the ends must stay visible. Static: the heading rule still draws once on scroll, as today.
"the rule that is thickest in the middle" (scope-25, sepia)

3 · shade-light and shade-dark · the pointer is the light mock

This block is about the shade pair: switch the theme to shade-light, then to shade-dark.

mock Read it in:
Look at: move the mouse slowly around and across the PROPOSED panel. In shade-light the plain button, the two cards and the headline throw their shade away from the pointer: pointer to the left of a card, shade on its right; pointer below, shade above; pointer right over it, the shade shrinks under it. In shade-dark the same elements are lifted toward the pointer: the card or button nearest to it takes a pale wash on the side the pointer is, and the dark shadow under it lengthens, away from the pointer; far away they settle back. In CURRENT nothing follows the pointer: the light stays at the top left. Then take the mouse off the page, or press Tab: PROPOSED falls back to that same top-left light and matches CURRENT's buttons exactly. With reduced motion PROPOSED never follows the pointer. The coloured buttons stay flat in both, as they ship.
"the pointer is the light, and the light half throws its shade away from it while the dark half is lifted out of shade by it" (scope-25, shade-light and shade-dark)

4 · The light, held in three places mock

This block is about the shade pair: switch the theme to shade-light, then to shade-dark.

Look at: the same card three times with the light fixed where the pointer would be, so the states can be compared without moving the mouse: to the left of the card, to its right, and right over its middle. In shade-light the shade falls on the right, then on the left, then almost none. In shade-dark the pale wash sits on the left edge, then on the right edge, then in the middle at its strongest, with the shadow under it on the far side. The text must read equally well in all three; the contrast measured in the brightest spot is below.
