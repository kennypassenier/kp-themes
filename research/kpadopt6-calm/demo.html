Calm screens: six parts for a day that is allowed to be quiet

Six parts for a daily check-in and a today screen, built in the package: a picker with the choices you use most on top, one question per screen, a quiet card for what is open, done or paused, a card that quotes your own earlier words, a calendar that shows a day with nothing logged as quiet instead of failed, and a star rating. Four extend a component that already exists (the combobox, the one-step view, the status list, the calendar with its heat grid and uptime strip) with options that are off until asked for; two are new. This page asks formal only; the other themes follow once formal is approved.

Judge in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top): 6 topics, one step each in formal. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does. Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample content (symptoms, check-in questions, the day’s tasks, the diary sentences, recipes), the fixed clock (Saturday 17/10/2026), the frames marked “phone width” (390 px boxes standing in for a phone), the event readouts and the buttons that drive a state are this page’s. Nothing here is saved anywhere.

Quote card

Quote card: your own words, back to you

<figure class="kp-quote" data-kp-quote> (js/quote.js): a card that shows what you wrote earlier, word for word, with who wrote it and when. It only shows; what to show, and never a negative sentence on a rough day, is decided by the app that owns the notes.

Expected:

  • First card: a navy rule along the start edge, a large quote mark, the sentence Wandelen na het eten hielp (Dutch, marked lang="nl"), the small line What helped last time over it, You, Wednesday 14/10/2026 under it, and a Do not show this again button. Hover the date: the tooltip is the exact moment, 14/10/2026 19:40.
  • Hide: press Do not show this again (or press h while the focus is in the card): the card goes at once, a toast says Hidden with Undo and a ring that counts down 6 seconds; Undo brings it back where it was. When the six seconds pass, the grey box under the first card says the page was told (kp-quote-hide).
  • Long entry: the second card shows four lines and ends in an ellipsis, with Show all under it; press it and the whole entry shows (Show less folds it again). The card keeps its caption under the words either way.
  • Several quotes (the list): three quotes and Show all 7 (the exact number), then all seven and Show fewer. Hero: the same card at a large size with a link, Open that day, and a Copy button.

Formal’s card is paper with a hairline; the rule along the start edge is the navy that draws every rule in formal, and the quote mark is a quiet navy at half strength. Look at the margin between the mark and the words, at the words never touching the card’s edge, and at the buttons under the caption.

Star rating: give one, show one

                    

                        <div class="kp-rating" data-kp-rating> (js/rating.js): five whole stars to give something a score, and the
                        same row read only to show one. An empty row says it is empty; it never looks like a zero.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Lasagne (first case) starts with five outlined stars and the words Tap a star under them. Press the fourth
                            star: four are filled in the accent colour (navy in formal, never red or green), the words say Great, a small
                            Clear button appears under the row (on a mouse only), a toast says Lasagne: 4 stars with Undo, and
                            the grey box under the case shows the event. Press the fourth star again: the rating goes (rating removed).
                            Nothing under the row moves when the words change.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Keys: Tab reaches the row once (one tab stop); the arrows move and choose, Home and End go to the
                            first and last star, Backspace or Delete removes the rating. No digit keys (they need Shift on your
                            keyboard). With a mouse, the stars up to the one under the pointer show lighter before you press (the second row of the
                            first case has this switched off, to compare).
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Show one: a read-only row of small stars with the number beside it (4.5 (12), the count exact), and the row
                            of nobody’s rating reads Not rated yet with outlined stars. A screen reader gets one name for the row
                            (“Rated 4.5 of 5 by 12 people”).
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Half stars (an option, not the default): the same row answers to a press on the left or right half of a star;
                            the keys step by one half. Save rating case: a tap only picks, the two buttons appear, and the page keeps the old
                            value until Save.
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Formal’s stars are drawn with a thin rounded outline, filled solid in the primary navy. Look at the filled stars against
                        the empty ones at the three sizes (small 16 px, medium 28 px, large 36 px), at the focus ring on a star, and at the touch area
                        around each star (44 px).
