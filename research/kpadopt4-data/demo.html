Identity marks, share bars, bullet rows, key-value grids, form rows, code blocks and live numbers

Seven things Kenny’s apps still draw themselves because kp-themes 10.1.1 has no component for them: the homelab dashboard’s stackMark, hk-stack-bar, st-set and hl-kv; JobTracker’s Mark, follow-up clock, Row, PageHeader and TickNumber; kyu’s payload pre.snippet; the chassis status dl. Each is built here on the package: the rules are in css/components.css, the behaviour in js/idmark.js, js/stackbar.js, js/bullets.js, js/code.js and js/tick.js. They take every colour, corner and face from the theme’s tokens; the registers do not dress them yet, so this round asks formal only.

Everything on this page is the package except the sample data (a homelab and a job hunt), the buttons that switch a section between its states, and the frames marked “phone width”, which are 390 px wide boxes standing in for a phone. Judge in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top): formal, five topics, eight sections. Each section says under Expected what you should see and what each button does.

1 · Identity mark

A small plate made from a name, so a company or a stack is found in a list before its name is read. The same name gives the same mark on every page. It is decoration: the name always stands beside it.

Letters: two capitals on a pale wash of one of formal’s five chart inks, a hairline edge in that ink, formal’s 0.375 rem corner. Pattern: the ink as ground with a mirrored 5 by 5 pattern in paper. Check the letters read at every size, the corner glyph never touches a neighbour, and the empty plate reads as “no name”, not as a colour.

Expected:

  • The table: one row per look, one column per size (xs 20 px, sm 24, md 32, lg 44, xl 56). Extra small shows one letter; every other size two. The glyph row has a small circle, square, triangle or diamond on the top corner, picked by the name; it is not drawn at xs.
  • Free hue: the same lightness and strength as formal’s first chart ink, the hue from the name (the homelab dashboard’s one colour per stack).
  • States: an empty name is a dashed quiet plate with “?”; the current mark in a list has a navy ring kept off its neighbours by a paper halo; loading is the plate’s own shape pulsing (still with reduced motion).
  • A list read together (the eight stacks): no two marks share a colour while one of the five is free; the sixth name onward shares.
  • A group: marks overlap with a paper edge, and the rest is an exact “+7”, never “9+”. In a table cell the long name is cut with an ellipsis and never runs under the mark.
  • Rename (button): the first mark’s name changes; its colour, letters and pattern follow.

2 · Share bar

One bar split into the parts of a whole (what fills a disk, where the money went), with a legend that gives every part its exact value and the free rest.

Formal’s five chart inks in order, a 2 px paper seam between the parts, the free rest as the muted track, the legend in aligned columns with the figures to the right. Check no text touches the swatch or the figure, and the bar keeps its height in every state.

Expected:

  • Ready: “Root volume” with “81.5 GB / 120 GB” at its end; five parts in the chart inks and the free rest as the track; a tick at 80 %. The legend lists each part with its exact size and “Free 38.5 GB”.
  • Point at a part (or Tab to the bar, then the arrow keys): the hover card says the name, the size and the share; the part gets a ring and its legend row is shaded, and the other way round when you point at a legend row. Home and End jump to the first and last part. A click writes the part in the readout.
  • Loading: the bar pulses at its own height and the legend keeps its rows as waiting lines; nothing under it moves. Not read and Failed: one sentence where the legend was, the bar still there.
  • Over the whole: the parts add up to 131 GB of 120 GB; the bar is their sum, a red ▸ stands at its end and the last row says “Over by 11 GB” in strong ink. Nine parts: the sixth to eighth take the inks again hatched, and parts under 2 % are merged into “Other”.
  • Sizes: small (8 px) and large (20 px) under the live one. Phone width: the legend goes to one column.

3 · Bullet rows: a value against a threshold

One row per item with a bar whose tick is the threshold, worst first: days since the last contact against 14 days, a backup’s age against its limit, days to a certificate’s expiry.

Each row: the company’s mark, the role and a detail line, a thin navy bar with a dark tick at the threshold, and the exact figure. Past the threshold the bar turns warning and the figure stands on the warning plate; far past it, red. Check every bar begins and ends in the same column, and no figure touches its bar.

Expected:

  • Ready: six applications, worst first. 31 d and 22 d are past the 14-day threshold (21 d is “bad” here, so 31 d and 22 d are red, 18 d is warning); the bars run to 28 days and 31 d shows the meter’s ▸ at the end. “Northwind” has no status record: a dash and no bar. The tick sits at the same place in every row.
  • The rows are links: point at one and the whole row shades and the role is underlined; Tab reaches each role, Enter follows it (here it only writes the readout).
  • Loading: the rows keep their places as waiting rows and the table’s own loading card (formal’s seal and “Reading…”) stands in the middle; when it has gone the rows are drawn. Nothing due: the reason in one line. Failed: the sentence and a Retry button (it loads again).
  • Phone width: the bar takes a line of its own under the role and the figure; nothing is cut but the long role, with an ellipsis.

4 · Key-value grid

A read-only list of facts about one thing (a host, a release, a message), the pattern every app wrote by hand. The values reuse the package: ids in the mono face, a copy button, badges, the freshness line.

Labels in the quiet ink in a column as wide as the longest label, values in the page’s ink beside them; with lines, a hairline between the rows. Check every value starts on one edge, a long URL breaks instead of widening the card, and nothing touches.

Expected:

  • Default: seven rows; the label column is as wide as “Last successful backup” and every value starts on one edge. The long URL wraps inside the card; the id is mono with a Copy button beside it (press it: it says “Copied” in the same width); “Image digest” shows the dash for “not known”.
  • With lines (--lines): a hairline between rows, the last row without one. The row marked changed has a navy dot after its label; the failed row has a red rule on its start edge, in the gap, so nothing moved.
  • Stacked (--stack): each label over its value, for a narrow side panel. Two columns (--columns): two lists of rows side by side where the box is wider than 44 rem, one list on a phone.
  • Loading (button): every value becomes a waiting line at the value’s place (pulsing); the labels stay. Press again for the values.

5 · Form rows: label, control, what is said about it

A settings page as rows: the label (with its key under it), the package’s own control with its help or error under it, and on the right where the value comes from, a badge, or an Edit button.

Formal’s fields and switch in a column that starts on one edge for every row, the label level with the middle of a one-line control; the aside quiet at the right edge. Check the changed row’s navy rule and the failed row’s red rule sit in the gap left of the label and nothing moved.

Expected:

  • The label column is as wide as the longest label (“Keep snapshots for”), up to a third of the form; every control starts on one edge. A one-line label sits level with its control; the description row (--top) aligns to the top; the notes row (--wide) gives its text area the aside’s column too.
  • States: “Retention” is changed and not saved (navy rule on its start edge, “Not saved” badge); “Mail server” is invalid (red rule, the field’s own red edge and its error sentence under it); “Data folder” is locked (quiet label, a padlock and why, the field disabled); the switch row has its own control.
  • Type into “Retention”: the row shows its changed rule at the first keystroke; Save (button) clears it and the aside says “Saved 16/10/2026 14:02”.
  • Phone width: each row reads top to bottom, the aside beside the label, the control under both at the full width.

6 · Code and payload block

A message payload, a request or a snippet: its title and what it is in the head, the tools beside them, the text in the mono face under them.

A paper card with a hairline, the head on the card and the text on the muted paper below; JSON keys in navy, the brackets and commas quiet. The tools are formal’s own small buttons and segmented control. Check the head never wraps to a second line on the desk width, and “Copy” to “Copied” moves nothing.

Expected:

  • Ready: “Payload” with “JSON · 20 lines · 612 B”. Pretty / Raw switches the layout (Raw is the one line as it came, scrolling sideways in its own box; the line count follows). Wrap (a toggle) wraps the long lines instead. Copy copies what is shown and says “Copied” for 1.5 s in the same width. Line numbers sit in a gutter and are never copied or selected.
  • Folded: the long request stops at 8 lines with a fade and “Show all 31 lines” under it; pressed, it opens and says “Show less”. The foot of the truncated one says “Showing the first 64 KB of 210 KB.”
  • Loading: the body keeps its height under the table’s own loading card; the tools stay in place, disabled. Empty, Binary and Failed: one sentence in the body’s place at the same height.
  • Plain text (the shell snippet): no Pretty / Raw, only Wrap and Copy. Phone width: the tools go under the title on a line of their own, never wrapping inside.

8 · A live number that ticks to its new value

data-kp-count already counts a number up once when it arrives. A live counter changes after that; marked data-kp-tick, it counts from the number shown to the new one whenever the page writes a new number, and lands on exactly that text.

Formal’s key-figure tiles with their figures. A change counts quickly (0.6 s) and settles; digits keep their width (tabular), so only a new digit place moves anything. Check the last frame is exactly the number in the readout.

Expected:

  • +3 open items: “12” counts to “15” in about 0.6 s. +250 requests: “1,204” counts to “1,454”, the comma kept. −5 counts down. Jump +1,000,000: a jump too far to follow counts only its last 400 steps, then lands exactly.
  • The Dutch tile (lang="nl") keeps “1.118.204,75”: the dot groups and the comma is the decimal point, and the count keeps both decimals.
  • Press twice fast: the second change starts from wherever the first had got to, never from the old number. With reduced motion the new number is written at once.
