A packing list where each item shows who brings it, and an item nobody brings is loud. Several people may bring one item (a pot for the stove, two people for the tent). It is the package's checklist with data-kp-checklist-claim added; the page says who is looking and answers each press.

Expected:

  • Each row ends in a column of its own: the people who bring it (their coloured mark and name) and one button. Rows nobody brings have a red edge, a ! and the words Nobody yet; the bar says how many.
  • Press I’ll bring it on Gas stove: the button shows the spinner for a moment (the row does not move), then your name joins and the button says I’m bringing it. Press it again to let go: a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds.
  • Press I’ll bring it on Tent: the pretend server answers that Tom took it first. The row gets a red outline for 3 seconds, says so in words and goes back to what Tom’s claim makes of it. Nothing you typed or ticked is lost.
  • The chips All, Nobody yet, Mine carry exact counts. Nobody yet shows only the rows nobody brings; when none is left the list says Everything is taken and offers all items back. The key c on a focused row takes or lets go; Shift C toggles the gaps (press ? for the list).
  • Not signed in, the buttons are off and say why on hover or focus. The state buttons show loading (the busy card over the list), empty and failed (with Retry), each at the list’s full height. Another person acts lets Anna take the first free item and the list follows. At 390 px the claim cell moves under the label and the button fills the line, 44 px high.

Console: states

A prompt and, for every command, a block that shows whether it still runs, worked or failed. The package draws the prompt, the recall of earlier lines and the blocks; what a command may do stays with the app. Your earlier lines are kept only while this tab is open (a line can hold a secret).

Expected:

  • Type uptime and press Enter (or Run): a block appears with the command in the mono face, Sending…, then Running for 1 s with a spinner, then exit 0 · Done with the output under it. Up and Down walk your earlier lines; Esc clears the line; Ctrl+L clears the screen and a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds. The key : jumps to the prompt from anywhere on the page (press ? for the list).
  • The sample buttons run the other cases: a long output (folded to 12 lines with Show all), one that prints as it goes with Cancel, a failing one (exit 1 · Failed), a line with no output ((no output)), one the app refuses (what and what to do now, your line stays in the prompt), a dropped connection (Not sure it finished with Check again) and one over the 4 KB limit (Only the first … shown). rm -rf / needs a second press: Run it anyway?
  • Each block has Copy command, Copy output (each says Copied in the same width), Run again and Collapse. When you scroll up in a long list, a pill says how many commands arrived out of sight and jumps back.
  • Empty, loading, failed and a console with running switched off all keep the body’s height; the empty one invites you to run a command, the failed one has Retry. At 390 px the select goes above the prompt, Run takes its own full line and the bar stays at the foot.

Formal keeps it plain: a card with a hairline, the output blocks on white over a pale ground, the prompt glyph and the focus ring in navy, the exit codes in the success green and the destructive red with their glyphs.
