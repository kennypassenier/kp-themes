The page frame of a dashboard

.kp-ops, CSS only: the header, the key figures, the attention band, the toolbar and a grid of cards in one fixed order, with the spacing and the 12-column spans written once.

Expected:

  • One whole page in a frame: the header (the title Pump houses, the freshness line “updated … ago” right after it, a one-sentence description, the buttons at the right), a strip of four key figures of one height, one warning in the attention band, the toolbar (search and filter chips) and a grid of cards: 8 + 4, 6 + 6, 4 + 4 + 4 and 3 + 3 + 3 + 3. Cards in a row share one height; the gaps are the same everywhere.
  • Scroll the window past the page: the toolbar stays under the top edge (the default). Sticky part moves that to the header or turns it off. Attention band shows the warning or takes it away: with none, the band takes no room at all, no “all well” line.
  • Loading shows the whole page waiting at its final size from the first frame: the key figures as ruled lines, every card with formal’s busy card in its middle. Page failed puts one red entry in the attention band with Retry and the cards keep their place and their data. Ready brings it all back.
  • Page width switches the rule between 80 % of the window (never under 64 rem, the default), the whole width and a reading measure (64 rem). The frame is narrower than every one of them, so the readout beside the buttons says the widest the page may be at this window. Density tightens the gaps.
  • The phone frame (390 px) is the same page folded: every card on its own row, the key figures two to a row, only the primary button in the header and the rest behind More.

Ledger paper: cards ruled with hairlines, the toolbar a ruled band that stays put, the loading as ledger lines ruled across the key figures and the busy card in each card, the failed page one red-ink entry along its start edge.

.kp-split--panes with js/master-detail.js: a list, a detail and an optional side panel next to each other; on a narrow screen one pane at a time, with a Back button.

Expected:

  • Three panes side by side in a ruled frame: the list of secrets (17 rem), the detail (the rest) and, when the page is wide enough, a History panel (18 rem). Nothing is picked yet, so the detail is an empty plate that says Pick an item on the left large, in the middle of the whole pane, with the list still usable.
  • Press an item, or move with the up and down arrow keys (the focus stays in the list, Home and End jump): the detail fills for the item you are on after a short load. The picked row has a navy bar along its start. A long name wraps, it is never cut.
  • Loading lays formal's busy card over the detail pane only; the list stays usable. Failed fills the pane with the red-ink plate, the sentence large, and a normal-size Retry that loads the item again. Nothing picked brings the empty plate back. The pane keeps its size in every state.

Back, the browser's Back, or Esc returns to the list at the same scroll with the same row picked and the focus on it. What a phone shows switches between one pane (the default), a sheet that slides over the list and everything stacked.
  • The log shows each select, open, close and retry event.

        The automatic refresh chooser

        .kp-refresh with js/auto-refresh.js: “Every 30 s”, a freshness line and Refresh now. The app does the reading; the control only says when.

        Expected:

        • The first control opens Paused so the page stays still: a button that says Paused ▾, “updated … ago” (here the moment the page opened), Refresh now, and under them an empty rule. The second one is the same control as segments. Start at the default (30 s) sets the first one to the default.
        • Open the menu: Paused, Every 15 s, 30 s, 1 min, 5 min and Other…, which opens a field for any number of seconds (try 3, then Apply; a 0 or a word is refused in words). Pick an interval: the thin navy rule under the control shrinks from full to nothing at an even pace towards the next refresh; at the end “updating…” takes the place of “updated … ago” (the line keeps its width), the readout below counts one more read, and the line starts again at “0 s”.
        • Fail the next refresh, then wait for a tick or press Refresh now: the data stays, the line keeps counting (it turns stale, on a warning plate, once refreshes have stopped arriving for three intervals or a minute) and a red-ink entry says “Last refresh failed, retrying in …” with Retry now. The retries back off (the interval, twice that, four times that) and a success clears the entry.
        • Click in the text field under the controls: no refresh happens while it has the focus (the readout keeps its number); leave the field and it refreshes. The R key refreshes now anywhere outside a field. A tab that is hidden does not refresh and refreshes once when you return. With data-kp-remember the choice is kept per page on the device (this demo does not store it, so it always opens still).

        The countdown is a navy rule ruled off from its end at an even pace, square-ended; the failed entry is a red-ink entry along its start edge; the buttons and the menu are formal's own.

        A transfer with its speed and the time left

        .kp-progress-group--transfer with js/transfer.js: a bar with the file name, how much is done, the speed and the time left. The app reports bytes and moments; the package works out the rest.

        Expected: three transfers, and the first two behave differently on purpose. The difference is whether the app knows how big the file is.

        • First bar (known size, 600 MB): the app knows the total, so it can say how much is done as a share. The bar fills from 0 to 100 %, and the row reads “240 MB of 600 MB · 40 %”, the speed and “about 30 s left”.
        • Second bar (size unknown): the app is still sending but has no total, so there is nothing to divide by: no percentage exists and the bar cannot fill. Instead the bar is a full band of stripes that drifts calmly, which means “busy, size unknown”; it is not stuck. The words say the same: “size unknown” in the last column, and the bytes that arrived so far count up live (“240 MB so far”) next to the speed. That counting is its progress.
        • Press Second one learns its size while it runs: the app now reports a total (1.6 GB). The stripes settle into a normal bar that shrinks smoothly to the real share and then fills like the first, without a restart or a jump.
        • Third bar: stalls after a few seconds (“stalled for 5 s”) and then fails with Retry.
        • Press Play once (about 12 s, the clock runs at five times speed). Reset clears all three.

        The bar is formal's navy rule ruled to the share (the second rule only at 100 %); with no known size it is navy hatching that drifts along the rule; numbers are Instrument Sans tabular; the stalled speed is a warning plate, the finished time a green plate, the failure a red-ink entry along its start edge.

        A question that waits for your answer

        .kp-question with js/question-card.js: the host asks before it goes on, says what each answer sets in motion and how long it waits. The card shows the sending, the outcome and a refusal.

        Expected:

        • All the states, frozen: the page opens with the clock stopped so nothing moves. Waiting is a warning plate with the question, “Waits 50 s more”, “If nobody answers: Stop.” and two buttons (Allow, Stop) in one column with what each does beside them. Urgent (20 s left) is the stronger red-ink plate with the time in bold. Sending has the pressed button busy. Answered is green with “Allowed. The operation goes on.” and Dismiss. Refused says “Not sent: …” and the buttons work again. Expired is a plain plate saying nobody answered and what the default did; it stays (Kenny: the card stays with its outcome). The three ways of drawing the time are side by side.
        • Ask a question adds a live card (90 s) that counts down for real: the rule shrinks from its end at an even pace; under 30 s the card turns urgent (and a screen reader hears it once). Press a button (or the key 1 or 2 with the card focused): it is sent; with The app accepts it turns to its outcome, with The app refuses it says why and you may answer again. Several questions stack, the oldest first. The tab title carries “(1) waiting” while one is open.
        • Ask one with 12 s left shows the whole end: urgent at once, then at zero the card is the expired plate with the default’s outcome. Ask one that leaves at zero is the other choice: it is taken away the theme’s way.
        • The phone frame (390 px): the buttons full width, each consequence under its button.

        The warning is a red-ink entry on paper (a rule along the start edge); the countdown is a navy rule ruled off from its end at an even pace (linear, no ease); the answered card is the green plate; the buttons are formal's own.
