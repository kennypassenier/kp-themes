An editable sequencer for a recipe’s steps

Kitchen wants to lay out a recipe’s steps like clips in a video editor, so the package got a new part: lanes as rows, every step a bar that is moved, resized and linked by pointer or keyboard. It looks like the run timeline on purpose (same bars, same theme answers). The data is one real recipe in Kitchen’s own shape (track, minutes, start, runs alongside). Formal only; 9 sections, one per capability, 9 pairs.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does.

What is asked (Kitchen): Kenny wants to lay out a recipe’s steps like clips in a video editor: drag a bar along the time axis, drag either edge to resize it, everything snapping to a step the app sets (1 minute by default) and to the edges of other steps.

Spaghetti bolognese, steps in time

Kitchen’s recipe in two lanes. Drag a step, drag its left or right edge. A chip follows the drag and a line shows the edge it snaps to. Under the sequencer: what Kitchen would save.

Expected:

  • Drag a step sideways: it moves in whole steps (1 minute); drag near another step’s edge and a thin line appears where it snaps. Drag an edge: the step gets shorter or longer. Escape during a drag puts it back. The Total in the toolbar follows the end of the last step.
  • Snap sets the step (1, 5 or 15 minutes). Snap to edges off leaves only the grid. Unit counts the same plan in seconds, minutes or hours: the axis, the chip and the total speak that unit, and the numbers Kitchen would save stay minutes.

The bars are the run timeline’s own: solid, a colour per lane, the label inside when it fits and beside the bar when it does not. The grips at the two edges show on hover. Nothing under the pointer is redrawn while dragging.

What is asked (Kitchen): A step can be dragged from one lane (track) to another, and the lanes are the app’s: a recipe has Sauce and Pasta, a bigger one an Oven too.

Sauce, pasta and the oven

Drag a step up or down onto another lane; it keeps its place in time unless you move it sideways too. Where a lane is taken, the step goes back.

Expected:

  • Items change lane (the default): drag “Boil the water” up to Sauce, where there is room, and it lands there. Drop it on a step that is in the way and it goes back, with a chip saying so.
  • Items stay in their lane pins every step to its row: only sideways moves work. Three lanes adds the Oven with “Warm the plates”, so a third row exists to drag to.

Lanes keep their height and their name column while a bar crosses them; the bar being dragged lies over the lanes, raised, and the lane it would land in is the one under the pointer.

What is asked (Kitchen): Keyboard for all of it: arrows move by the step, Shift and arrows resize, Alt and Up or Down change lane, Enter drops, Escape puts back; announced to screen readers.

Pick a step up, move it, drop it

Tab into the sequencer, use the arrows to reach a step, press Enter to pick it up. The buttons press the keys for you; the line under the sequencer is what a screen reader hears.

Expected:

  • Pick up a step (Enter): its bar turns dashed. Earlier and Later (← →) move it by the step, Shorter and Longer (Shift and ← →) resize its end, Lane up and Lane down (Alt and ↑ ↓) change lane; the line says where it is after every press.
  • Pushing into a neighbour says why it cannot (“cannot go there: it would overlap …”), Drop (Enter) keeps the change and says so, Put back (Escape) restores it. Overlap push lets the keys push neighbours later instead of refusing.

The picked-up bar is dashed (not a colour change), the focus ring is clear of its edge, and the line under the sequencer holds the announcement in a fixed place that does not move the page.

A screen reader hears: Press Enter on a step to pick it up.

What is asked (Kitchen): “Runs alongside”: a step can be linked to start with another (a visible join), and moving one moves the linked.

Boil the water while the mince browns

“Boil the water” runs alongside “Brown the mince”: a dotted line joins them. Move one and the other goes with it. This section pushes neighbours later, so the pair has room to move.

Expected:

  • Browning 5 min later (or 10) moves the boiling too, and pushes “Simmer” and “Season” later in the Sauce lane. The join follows both bars; Reset the plan puts the steps back.
  • Cook alongside the simmer links “Cook the spaghetti” to “Simmer with the tomatoes”: it jumps to the simmer’s start and a second join appears. Cook on its own cuts the link and leaves it where it is. Select a step and the buttons under the sequencer do the same (L and U on the keyboard).

The join is a dotted line with a dot at each end, drawn at the shared start from one bar’s middle to the other’s; it is never the only sign (the selection line says “runs alongside …” too).

What is asked (Kitchen): Items in one lane may overlap only where the app allows it; otherwise they push or refuse.

Cook the spaghetti into the plating

Moving “Cook the spaghetti” 5 minutes later would put it on “Drain and plate”. What happens is the app’s rule, one attribute.

Expected:

  • Refuse (the default): the move does not happen and the line says what is in the way. Push: “Drain and plate” moves later by what it needs, and so does everything behind it. Allow: the two overlap and the lane grows a second row for the one underneath.
  • Move 5 minutes later and earlier try it; Reset the plan puts Kitchen’s steps back as they were. The rule can change between moves.

Under Allow the lane gets exactly the rows it needs and the other lanes keep theirs; under Push the pushed bars glide to their new place and the total grows.

What is asked (Kitchen): A live total (the end of the last item) and a “now” line like the gantt.

How long is dinner, and where are we

The total is the end of the last step and follows every edit; the line for now is where the cook is in the plan, set by the page.

Expected:

  • Now at 15 minutes or at 40 puts the red line there; Advance 5 minutes moves it on; Off removes it. The plan does not move; only the line does.
  • Add “Let the sauce rest” appends a 10-minute step: the total grows from 61 to 71 minutes and a dashed line marks where the plan ends. Remove it brings it back to 61.

The now line is the gantt’s: a 2 px line through every lane, with its time as its accessible name. The end of the plan is a dashed line; the total reads in the toolbar at the right.

What is asked (Kitchen): Events kp-sequencer-change with, per item, {id, lane, start, duration, with} in minutes, and a programmatic set and get.

What Kitchen is told

Every edit that is dropped fires kp-sequencer-change with the whole plan; a refused one fires kp-sequencer-refuse; selecting fires kp-sequencer-select. The log is what a page receives.

Expected:

  • Move a step (a change with changed naming it), Try an overlap (a refusal naming what is in the way) and Select a step each add a line; dragging in the sequencer does too. A drag that is put back with Escape adds nothing.
  • Read it back calls getSequencerItems and lists what the page would save; Clear the log empties the list.

The log keeps a fixed height and its lines line up in the same columns; the component above it is as wide as the column.

    What is asked (Kitchen): Phone width: horizontal scroll inside its own box, pinch or a zoom control.

    The plan in a narrow box

    A box you can narrow. The plot keeps a readable width and the frame scrolls sideways inside itself, the lane names held at the start. Zoom with the buttons, Ctrl and the wheel, or a pinch.

    Expected:

    • Tablet and Phone narrow the box: the sequencer scrolls sideways inside its frame, no page scroll appears, the lane names stay in view. Column is the full width.
    • Zoom 200% and 400% widen the plot (the frame scrolls, the axis refines its labels); Fit returns; 50% shows a whole long plan. A long afternoon triples the plan so there is something to scroll.

    The axis stays on top, the name column does not scroll away, and a bar’s grips stay easy to hit (larger on a touch screen); nothing makes the page itself scroll sideways.

    What is asked (Kitchen): The package’s loading, empty and failed states, filling the footprint.

    The sequencer while the recipe is read

    Loading is the theme’s busy card over the lanes, empty and failed fill the same box with the package’s plate. The box keeps its height.

    Expected:

    • Loading lays the spinner and its words over the lanes, centred; Empty says there is nothing yet and Failed says why with Retry, which brings the plan back.
    • Each state is as tall as the loaded sequencer: switch between them and the page under it does not move.

    The state is centred in the whole frame, at heading size, with a normal-size Retry; the toolbar and the selection line keep their places.
