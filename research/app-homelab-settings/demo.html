Homelab dashboard: settings, passkeys, secrets and the app frame

Three parts of the homelab admin dashboard rebuilt on kp-themes 11.0.0, 24 sections in formal: the Settings page (staged changes with a Save bar, editors that fit each kind of key, links to one setting, a "This browser" card), the sign-in and secrets pages (passkeys, machine tokens, a QR to register a phone, reveal with an audit line, the secrets list and its change drawer, restart order, which file feeds which app) and the frame around every page (the connection banner, the folding bar, the slim rail, keys that work on a Belgian keyboard, problems, page states, effects).

Mock on this page: all the data. The keys, groups and defaults are the host's real host.toml keys (backup_hour, restic_base, no_touch, logs_window), the stacks are the real fleet (jellyfin, immich, paperless, vaultwarden, traefik, prometheus, grafana, loki and kyu on CT 109, almanac on CT 112, syncthing on CT 108, inbox on CT 118, metrics on CT 113, admin on CT 120, on the host pve), and the secret values are made up. A few things need the host or the chassis kit to answer something it does not answer today (renaming a passkey, which passkey signed in, the date a passkey was last used, a Retry that tries one stack's read again); those sections say so and show a sample answer. Dates are dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm in the host's clock. Nothing moves until you press a button.

Staged changes: a Save bar that stays in view and reverts one key

Settings are staged, not written: the bar counts what you changed, lists it as "key, was, now", reverts one key or all of them, and says per key why the host refused one.

Expected:

  • Two keys are already changed (Backups at once and Log level). The bar in the header says 2 changes, with Discard and Save 2 changes; both changed rows are marked. Press the 2 changes chip: the list shows each key with what it was and what it is now, a Revert per key, and a link that takes you to the row. Scroll the box down until the header is gone: the same bar appears at the foot of the box.
  • Save puts the spinner in the button and answers after a moment. Host answers decides the answer: it takes every key, or it refuses one key with a reason, which stays marked on that row while the other change goes through ("1 saved, 1 refused"). Discard puts every value back and offers Undo for 6 s. Ctrl+S saves.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): the bar in the header and at the foot once the header scrolls away (the default), or in the header only. Both are options of the bar; the buttons switch them by drawing the bar again, because the package reads where the bar stands when it attaches (see the note at the end of the page).
  • Real keys of the host's host.toml. The yes/no key is edited with a switch, the numbers with a stepper that shows its unit, the nightly hour with a 24-hour time field; the bar sees them through a hidden text copy of each value (the bar cannot read a stepper or a time field itself, a package gap).

The bar is a quiet strip with a navy Save; a changed row has a hairline mark and "was" in its aside. Look at the gap between the chip and Discard, at the list's columns (key, was, now, Revert in one column each), and that Save at the foot is the same size as in the header.

Leaving a page with staged changes: ask, name the keys, and keep them

Following a link with staged keys asks first and names what would be lost; a reload keeps the staged keys for the session and says so.

Expected:

  • The mini page has two links on its left (Backups, Metrics) and two staged keys. Press a link: a dialog asks Leave without saving? with Stay focused first. Leave question chooses the words: the package's ("2 changes are not saved yet") or the page's own, which names each key and what it would lose.
  • Reload this part draws the page again. With Kept for the session (the default) the staged keys come back and a toast says "2 changes kept from before you left"; with Not kept the page opens clean. A secret is never kept.
  • Today a staged edit silently survives a navigation and is silently lost on a reload; neither is said. Following the link here goes nowhere (the log says where it would have gone).

The dialog is the package's confirmation plate: Stay is the focused, primary button, Leave the quiet one. Look at the list of keys in the page's own words (one key per line, aligned) and at the toast after a reload.

An editor that fits the kind of key

Each host.toml kind gets the control that cannot be filled in wrongly: a switch, a stepper with its unit, a 24-hour time field, tags for lists.

Expected:

  • Remote exec (yes or no) is a switch; Backups at once and Log window are steppers that show their unit and stay inside the host's limits (1 to 16, 1 to 168); Nightly hour is the 24-hour time field (type 0230, press Up or Down, or open the list), and No-touch list and Data mount roots are tag inputs where typing a VM number and pressing Enter adds a tag.
  • Yes or no and Lists decide the default editor; the other is an option of the same row (a select, a plain text field), switched here at runtime. The right column shows the values the page would send, as text.
  • The host keeps Remote exec, No-touch list and Data mount roots ssh-only today (a safety policy the token must not loosen); they are shown editable only so the editors can be judged.

Formal draws the switch as a navy track, the stepper as one box with minus and plus, tags as small pills. Look at the equal row height, at the unit never breaking, and at the tag input's list opening on the plate of the menu.
