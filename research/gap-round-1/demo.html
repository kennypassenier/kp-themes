New components for the homelab dashboard, round 1

The homelab admin dashboard drew five things by hand because the package had none of them. Each is now a part of the package, shown here in formal only: the live log viewer, a diff of several files, the activity feed, two options of the entity card (a trend line and the app tile with a favourite star), and three small things around tabs, tiles and the heat grid (a count on a tab, arrow keys over a board of tiles, the legend under a heat grid). 21 sections, 21 pairs. All data is mock (a seeded homelab with jellyfin, sonarr and traefik); nothing is read from a host.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does.

Lines keep arriving and the log keeps only the newest

A host log that grows while you read it. The viewer keeps a fixed number of lines (here a small number, so you can see it), drops the oldest first and says how many it dropped. Following the newest line, Pause and the “N new lines” pill are the package’s own.

Expected:

  • It opens with 46 lines and a cap of 60. Add 10 lines adds them at the foot and the view follows; the second press crosses the cap and the foot says N earlier lines dropped, the newest 60 are kept, with the exact number.
  • Scroll up a little, then Play once: the view stays where you are and a pill says N new lines with the exact count; pressing it (or End) goes back to the newest line and follows again. Pause in the bar holds the view and becomes Resume.
  • Lines kept 30, 60 or 200 changes the cap at once: at 30 the oldest lines leave the log and the dropped count grows, at 200 nothing is dropped.
  • The level is a word first (WARNING, ERROR) and a tint second; each stack keeps its own hue; Copy says Copied without changing its width, Download saves a text file.

The viewer is the log of a homelab host: stacks such as jellyfin, sonarr and traefik write into one list. The panel is an opaque paper plate in a strong hairline with a 3 px navy rule on its start edge; things are ruled in from that edge in a straight line, nothing fades. Check that the text keeps clear of every edge, that the level colours still read as words first, and that nothing moves when a state changes.

Wrap, the time column and the level

Three settings of how the lines look. Wrap decides whether a long line breaks or scrolls sideways, the time column can show the time, the date and time, or nothing, and the level hides the quiet lines.

Expected:

  • Wrap On: the two long lines (a stack trace and a database message) break and every line starts at the same column.

Warnings carry a yellow plate on the word and errors a red one, with the same tone faint behind the whole line. Unchanged lines fold away

A long run of lines nobody touched becomes one row, “42 unchanged lines”, that opens where it stands, so the change is what you see.

Expected:

  • In the static config three runs fold with the default of 3 kept lines: the start of the file, the stretch between the two changes and the end. Each fold row says its exact number and is a button; Enter or a click opens it in place, and the same row now says Hide 42 unchanged lines.
  • Context lines: with 1 more is hidden, with All shown nothing folds. A run that would hide fewer than 3 lines is never folded.
  • A new file has no unchanged lines, so nothing folds and every line is shown with a plus sign.
  • Opening a fold keeps the focus on its row, and the line numbers stay true: the new file’s number for kept and added lines, the old one for removed lines.

Formal: the fold row is a quiet dashed rule across the three columns of the diff, its words standing where the code stands. Check that the words are clear of the edge, that the three dots turn into a single rule when the fold is open, and that opening a fold moves nothing above it.

The changed words inside a line

When a line is replaced by a nearly identical one, only the words that differ are marked, so a version bump reads as the version and not as a whole line.

Expected:

  • Image tags: in jellyfin/jellyfin:10.11.11 becoming jellyfin/jellyfin:12.1.0 only the version is marked. Added words are bold and underlined, removed words bold and struck through, so the two sides differ without colour.
  • Settings marks the port, the log level and the memory limit alone; Log lines marks the dates, the times and the version in a startup log, and a line that has no partner stays plain.
  • A rewritten line is not marked word by word: when more than 60% of a line changed, the whole line is the change and marking every word would say nothing.
  • Words: Not marked shows the same lines with whole-line tints only.

Formal: a deeper tint and a heavier weight inside the line, nothing flashing. Check that the marked words stay inside their line without moving the text beside them, that the sign column and the numbers stay quiet, and that a marked word is readable on both the green and the red line.

Who did what, day by day

One feed of a homelab: every row has its outcome, what happened, who started it and when, and the rows sit under a heading per day with that day’s count.

Expected:

  • Each day has a heading: Today, Yesterday, then the weekday, then the date as dd/mm/yyyy, with that day’s exact count and N failed (or all ok) at the end.
  • A row: a round mark (tick, cross, exclamation, spinner or i), the title in bold, a muted detail line, a chip with the actor, and the time. The chips and the times stand in one column each, whichever row is longer.
  • The time says 12 min ago for the first 24 hours and ticks by itself; after that it is the moment, dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm. Pointing at it gives the full moment.
  • Loading keeps the feed’s height with the theme’s busy card over a skeleton of rows; Empty and Failed fill the same height with a large centred plate (Failed has a Retry that reads the log again); Stale keeps every row and says in the head line why the feed was not refreshed, with a button to read it again.
  • Nothing moves sideways or jumps in height between the states. Labels are whole, never cut.

Formal is a paper plate: quiet ink, square-ish corners, one border weight. Check that the marks are legible by shape alone, that a failed row stands out without shouting, that text keeps 8 px from every edge, and that the chip column lines up from the first day to the last.

What is asked: Each of these is a setting of the component with a default (time auto, actor chip, rule on), switchable at run time. Press them and say which default is right, and whether the others are worth keeping as options. The rows reach back three days so the time options differ.

The time, the actor and the rule

Three things a page may want different: how the time reads, whether the actor is a chip, a mark or left out, and whether the timeline’s rule joins the marks.

Expected:

  • Auto (default): 12 min ago for 24 hours, then dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm. Ago line: always 1 day 20 h ago and so on. Clock: the ago line, then only HH:mm under its day heading. Moment: always dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm.
  • Chip (default): mark and name. Mark only: the round or square mark with the name as its title (a screen reader still hears it). No actor: the column is gone and the title gets the room.
  • Rule: a thin line joins one mark to the next inside a day and stops at the day heading; off, the marks stand alone.
  • Every press changes the feed at once, and the columns stay aligned.

Compare Auto with Clock: the older rows lose their date and keep only the hour. Compare Chip with Mark only: the name leaves but the mark stays recognisable by its colour and pattern. Check that the title uses the room when the actor is off.

What is asked: In the dashboard a row opens an inline detail. Here the title is the button (a chevron says so), the lines come in the mono face on the muted plate, and a row that has both lines and a page shows Open inside. Opening is a setting: several rows at once (default), one at a time, or not at all.

Arrow keys over a board of tiles

A board of app tiles is one place in the tab order: Tab enters it once, the arrow keys move between the tiles by where they are drawn, and Tab leaves it. Click a tile (or use Put the focus on the board) and press the arrow keys.

Expected:

  • Keys on: the readings under the board say one tab stop for all the tiles. Right and Left walk a row and go on to the next row; Up and Down go to the tile above or below in the same column, and from the last tile of a short row to the last tile of the next row, never skipping a row. Home and End go to the first and last tile. Up on the first row lands in the search box.
  • Keys off gives the ordinary tab order back: every tile is a tab stop (the readings say so) and the arrow keys do nothing.
  • Board width changes how many tiles stand in a row (four, three, two, one); the keys follow whatever is drawn. Folded and Only apps with a problem take tiles out; the keys skip them.
  • Press S on a tile to star it; the star is in the key line because it is not a tab stop of its own.

Formal is ruled paper. Check that the focused tile shows the package’s focus ring with clear space round the tile, that the ring moves with the key at once, and that the tiles keep one height in every width.
