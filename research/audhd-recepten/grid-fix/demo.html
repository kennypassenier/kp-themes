Recipe app: every column on one line

Twelve sections of the recipe app's approved screens that did not stand on a grid: a column that started lower than the one beside it, a button off its row's line, chips whose words did not line up on a phone. Each is rebuilt so its columns share one line and its rows share one set of tracks; the settings and the wheel's filters are now one form grid. Only these sections are here; everything else stays approved on its own page. 12 sections, 12 pairs, formal only.

Every section says under What changed what was wrong and what is different now, and under Expected what you should see.

The zero-prep shelf

Food that is eaten without cooking, sorted by how much you can manage today. Say how much that is, and the list shrinks to what fits.

What changed: Was: each row’s badge sat on the title line and “This one” in the middle of the row, 21 px lower. Now the badge, the title’s first line and the button share one line, and every button stands in one column. On a phone the three levels went under the title and scroll inside their own bar; before, they pushed the page 80 px sideways.

Expected:

  • Nine foods in rows, each with a lead (minutes or a word), a sentence, what it needs and a This one button in one column.
  • The three choices above the rows filter them: Just open it 4, One easy step 7, Anything here 9. The numbers are exact and never move the rows.
  • Press This one: a line under the list says it is chosen. Loading, Nothing on the shelf and Could not open fill the same area.

Formal: the three choices as one segmented control, the rows ruled, every title starting at one edge and every button in one column.

    Recipe page: read it, scale it, begin

    What changed: Was: the title stood in the left column only, so “Portions” on the right started 9 px off it. Now the title row spans both columns and the two columns under it start on one line.

    Expected:

    • The card is Fetching the recipe under the theme’s busy card from the first frame, then fills: title with an Effort: Low badge, the summary folded to two sentences with More, Total time, Hands-on and Effort in three columns, the big Begin cooking button with its one line under it, and Last time (04/10/2026 18:40) with what you wrote then.
    • Portions stands at 4. Press + or −, hold either, or type a number: every amount changes at once and lights for a moment (not in calm mode). Chicory 8 becomes 12 at 6; onion 1 becomes ½ at 2; milk 500 ml becomes 1.5 l at 12; the pinch of nutmeg never changes.

    • Buttons above: Summary folded or whole, Notes on a hover card or written under the name, Parts of a whole as fractions or decimals. Each redraws the card at once and keeps the portions you set.

    Formal’s paper and navy: the big button is the one filled navy plate on the card, the amounts are bold and right-aligned so the numbers read down in one column, the dotted underline marks the names that carry a note, and nothing else competes with Begin cooking.     Recipe page: loading, not found, could not load

    The same card when the recipe is being fetched, when it does not exist any more, and when the server did not answer. Each state keeps the card’s size, so nothing around it moves.

    What changed: The same change as above: the title row spans both columns.

    Expected:

    • Loading: the theme’s busy card centred over the whole card with Fetching the recipe; the recipe under it is dimmed and cannot be pressed. It keeps moving.
    • Not found: the card’s empty plate fills the card, centred, at heading size: We cannot find this recipe, It may have been renamed or removed. Nothing you saved is lost. and one button under it. It is calm: no red, no exclamation mark, no “error”.

    Formal’s empty plate and its destructive alert, in the middle of the card and large; the busy card is formal’s navy ring. Look at the text centre against the card centre, and at the card’s height in the four states.

    Expected:

    Cooking log: a quiet week is only quiet

    The weeks you cooked, as a grid: a day you logged is filled, a day you did not is a pale plate with no mark, no number and no red. It never counts days, never says missed, and a whole week with nothing in it looks like a week of rest.

    What changed: Was: on a phone the three-letter days stood 3 px apart. Now the phone shows two letters (Mo, Tu, We…), never cut; a wide screen is unchanged.

    Expected:

    • Four rows of seven days, named 3 weeks ago, 2 weeks ago, Last week and This week (the weeks starting 21/09, 28/09, 05/10 and 12/10). The first week is all quiet: seven pale plates and nothing else, no warning, no count. The second has three logged days; the third has three paused days (hatched: you were ill) and two logged; the fourth ends with two days that have not come yet (dashed).
    • Point at a logged day: Witloof in the oven · felt easy. Point at a quiet one: 14/10/2026, nothing logged. Press a day: it is marked, and the line above the grid says Log dinner for Wednesday 14/10/2026: adding a day afterwards is always allowed. Press it again to go back to today.
    • Log today’s dinner: choose how it felt (Easy, Fine, Heavy, or nothing at all) and press Add to the log: today fills in, the button says Added, and Remove today’s entry takes it back. There is no streak and no total.
    • A quiet week (button above): every day of every week is quiet and the note under the grid says Nothing logged. That is fine. Nothing yet: the empty plate fills the card, centred: Nothing logged yet, That is fine: the log starts whenever you do. Loading: the busy card over the grid. Could not read: the alert says the log could not be read and that the entries are safe, with Try again.
    • Buttons above: State, the Colour of a logged day (accent or success), how a week is named (This week, Last week, or its first day) and how many Weeks show (four or two).

    Formal’s navy is a logged day; a quiet day is a tone barely darker than the paper with no border; the paused hatch is the one the heat grid already uses. Look at the quiet week: it must draw no eye, and nothing in it may look like a fault.

    Filters: want, not, or either way

    Each chip is pressed once to require something, twice to leave it out and a third time to let it go, so “dinner, quick, without cheese” is three presses and the wheel only holds what fits.

    What changed: Was: on a phone the chips wrapped to different widths, so their words and counts did not line up. Now the filters are one form grid, and on a phone the chips stand two per row in equal columns (the time ranges one per row, never cut).

    Expected:

    • Three rows with their names in one column: Meal, Time and Ingredients. Each chip shows the exact number of dishes behind it. Press a chip: it is filled with a tick (required); press again: struck through with a cross (left out); a third time: neutral. The line under each row says what is chosen in words.
    • Required meals and times mean any of them, required ingredients mean all of them; anything left out removes the dish. The card below lists the dishes that fit and the badge says “N of 13” (the real number).
    • Clear all filters lets every chip go. A set that leaves nothing (try Nothing fits) fills the card with a large centred note and Loosen a filter, which lets go of the chip that frees the most dishes and says which.
    • Loading puts the busy card over the list and a placeholder where the chip counts are; Failed a large alert with Try again. The chips themselves stay usable in every state.

    Formal: a pressed chip turns its face round (navy, light words) with a tick; a left-out chip keeps its face but strikes its word through in the destructive ink. Look at the three label columns lining up and at the chips never changing width when they change state.

    Cook mode: one step at a time

    Nine steps on the counter, one at a time and large, with what the step needs and a timer button; started timers keep running in the list beside it while you go on to the next step.

    What changed: Was: the steps column began with its toolbar, 2 px off the heading “Timers running” beside it. Now the steps column has its own heading, “Steps”, on that line.

    Expected:

    • Step 1 shows Step 1 of 9, the progress bar, the screen line (Screen stays on where the browser can), Read aloud, the step, what it needs, a timer button and a note saying which track it belongs to (Pasta or Sauce).
    • Press Start a timer · 10 min on step 1: a Water timer appears in the list and keeps counting. Go on with Next: steps 3, 4 and 5 start Onion, Sauce and Pasta, so Water, Sauce and Pasta run side by side while you stir and taste.
    • When a timer ends its card turns to the done plate with a chime, and the band above the step says which one and offers Go to step N for what comes next: nothing has to be remembered.
    • Arrow keys, Space, Home and End move between steps; a swipe does the same on a touch screen. The buttons never move, whatever the step’s length.
    • Why it helps: one thing on screen at a time (no scanning a recipe), the running timers are always visible (time blindness), the screen stays on with messy hands, and the end of a timer asks for attention once, kindly.
    • The parallel tracks are shown with what the package has today: the track is named in each step’s note and the timers carry the track names. The real track option is under Component gaps in the README.

    Formal: the step is large dark text on the white card, the two navigation buttons sit at the foot in fixed places, and the timer cards on the right show the time left in figures that do not jump as they count.
