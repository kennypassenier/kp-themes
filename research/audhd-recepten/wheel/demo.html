What do we eat?

The screen for the moment you freeze on “what do we eat tonight”. The wheel chooses and you only say yes or spin again; filters keep the wheel to what fits; a No energy today switch cuts it down to dishes that ask almost nothing of you. The dishes are sample data (Belgian home cooking), the clock stands at Sunday 18/10/2026 17:10 and dinner is at 18:30. Judge it in the review dialog, in formal.

The wheel and its answer

Press Spin and the wheel picks tonight’s dinner; dishes you have not cooked for a long time get a bigger slice, and all you do is say yes or spin again.

Expected:

  • Thirteen real dishes on one round wheel. Witloof in ham and cheese (40 days), waterzooi (31) and stoofvlees (24) have the largest slices; Croque monsieur (2 days) and yoghurt (1 day) the smallest.
  • Spin (the wheel’s hub, or Space on it, or Spin again beside the wheel): the wheel turns and slows to a stop on one dish. At the right the card then names it, says when it was last cooked and why its slice was that size. Nothing on the page moves while it turns.
  • Yes, cook this is dimmed, with a reason, until the wheel has chosen. Pressed, it fixes tonight’s dinner and counts back from 18:30 to when to start (and says so plainly when that moment is already past).
  • Not this one, not again this week takes the dish off the wheel (the slices glide to their new size) and lists it under Resting with a Bring back button.
  • The state buttons above the card: Loading lays the busy card over the whole card, No dish fits shows a large centred note with Loosen a filter, Failed a large centred alert with Try again.

Formal: the wheel is the one large shape, its slices soft washes of the chart colours with navy ink, the hub a navy disc; the answer at its right is a plain ruled column. Look at the room round every word on the card and at the three buttons standing in one column with their one-line descriptions beside them.

Filters: want, not, or either way

Each chip is pressed once to require something, twice to leave it out and a third time to let it go, so “dinner, quick, without cheese” is three presses and the wheel only holds what fits.

Expected:

  • Three rows with their names in one column: Meal, Time and Ingredients. Each chip shows the exact number of dishes behind it. Press a chip: it is filled with a tick (required); press again: struck through with a cross (left out); a third time: neutral. The line under each row says what is chosen in words.
  • Required meals and times mean any of them, required ingredients mean all of them; anything left out removes the dish. The card below lists the dishes that fit and the badge says “N of 13” (the real number).
  • Clear all filters lets every chip go. A set that leaves nothing (try Nothing fits) fills the card with a large centred note and Loosen a filter, which lets go of the chip that frees the most dishes and says which.
  • Loading puts the busy card over the list and a placeholder where the chip counts are; Failed a large alert with Try again. The chips themselves stay usable in every state.

Formal: a pressed chip turns its face round (navy, light words) with a tick; a left-out chip keeps its face but strikes its word through in the destructive ink. Look at the three label columns lining up and at the chips never changing width when they change state.

No energy today: only what is possible

One switch for the days with nothing in the tank: the wheel only holds safe favourites, portions from the freezer and food that needs no preparing at all.

Expected:

  • Off: the wheel holds all 13 dishes. Press the No energy today switch: the slices that ask real effort shrink away and eight dishes are left (the badge says “8 dishes”); the wheel keeps its size and place.
  • What counts as easy are three toggle chips with their exact counts (Safe favourites 6, Freezer portions 3, No prep 4). Turn one off and the dishes only it allowed leave the wheel; turn all three off and the card says nothing is left and offers Loosen a filter. While the switch is off the chips are dimmed with a reason.
  • Spin, and the answer card says in its facts why the dish is easy (“Safe”, “Freezer, 4 portions”, “No prep”); the start time counts back from dinner as before.

Formal: the switch is the package’s own, its track navy when on. Look at the wheel shrinking to fewer, larger slices without moving, and at the sentence beside the switch saying what it does.

Loading, nothing fits and failed

What the card shows while the dishes load, when the filters leave nothing and when the dishes could not be read; it keeps its size in every state so the page does not jump.

Expected:

  • Loading: the theme’s busy card centred over the whole card (the wheel under it dimmed), animated from the first frame. The card is as tall as when it is ready.
  • No dish fits: a large centred note (heading size) filling the card, with the drawing, one sentence about what blocks and a normal-size Loosen a filter. Press it: the card says which filter it let go of and the wheel is back.
  • Failed: a large centred alert, “The dishes could not be read”, and Try again; pressing it shows the busy card for a moment and then the wheel.

Formal: the empty note is formal’s dashed plate with the ledger drawing, the failure its destructive alert; the words sit in the middle of the card with air on every side, never in a corner. Compare the three heights with the ready card.
