HTTPSwitchboard: profile settings and recheck

HTTPSwitchboard accepts a message in one shape and delivers it in another. Each profile says where a message comes from and where it goes. This page shows how one profile’s settings are read, whether its retries fit inside its lease, and how a profile is put back to “starting” to be checked again. Judge it in the review dialog; formal only. The profile alertmanager is real (it carries Alertmanager alerts from the kyu topic alerts.raw to Home Assistant). uptime-kuma is a mock second profile. The destination’s webhook path is a credential and is never shown. Every request here is faked on the page; nothing is sent.

A profile’s settings and its retry budget

Lists what a profile does in plain rows, and shows with one bar how long its retries can take compared to the time the service holds a message for it (the lease).

Expected:

  • Two profile cards. Each lists Source, Destination host, Content type, Timeout, Retries, Max attempts and Lease, then Retry budget: a bar with the words “27 s of 60 s lease (45 %)”. The labels share one column as wide as the longest label; the values line up.
  • The budget is the timeout for every try plus the pauses between tries (1 s, 2 s, 4 s, doubling). For alertmanager: 4 tries of 5 s is 20 s, plus pauses of 1 s, 2 s and 4 s is 7 s: 27 s. A tick sits at the end of the bar: that is 100 % of the lease.
  • uptime-kuma (mock) is over budget: 5 tries of 10 s is 50 s, plus pauses of 1 s, 2 s, 4 s and 8 s is 15 s: 65 s against 60 s, 108 %. Its bar is full, carries the sign you chose below, and its words say “5 s over the lease” with the Over budget badge. The destination shows the host and port only, never the path.
  • Retries (alertmanager): 0 to 5. The Retries and Max attempts rows, the words and the bar follow at once; at 5 retries alertmanager is 61 s of 60 s and goes over too. Past the end flips the sign live. Settings list flips the ruling. Loading pulses the bars and shows the waiting line in every value at the same row height.
  • The settings list and the sign are options of the components; the dialog asks only which is the default (the first is preset).

Formal draws the bar as a thin ink rule. Look at the tick at the end of the bar, at the over-budget sign not touching the words beside it, at the label column keeping one width in both cards, and at the line between rows in the theme’s own hairline.

Recheck profiles, all at once or one by one

Puts every profile, or just one, back to “starting” so the service checks its destination again, and says what happened in a toast instead of a block of raw text.

Expected:

  • A small table of the two profiles with their state (Working), their retry budget (a small bar and the percentage) and an actions column that keeps its place and width. Above it, the Recheck profiles button.
  • Press Recheck profiles: the button shows the theme’s spinner and Rechecking… without changing width, a second press does nothing; after about 1.5 s it says Done for a moment, both states turn to Starting and a green toast says 2 profiles back to starting (with a close button; it waits while you point at it).
  • Open Actions on a row and choose Recheck this profile: that row shows Rechecking… and then Starting, with a toast alertmanager back to starting. A row that is already starting keeps the entry in the menu, greyed, with the reason “It is already starting.” under it. The menu is shown opened below the table.
  • Set Request to Fails and press either: the button shows Refused and a red toast stays until closed: “Could not reach HTTPSwitchboard. Check that the service is running, then try again.” with a Try again button. Rows stay as they were. Reset profiles puts both back to Working.
  • Error shows and Row action are options: the toast or a line under the button; the menu or a button in each row. The dialog asks only which is the default.

Look at the button keeping its width through rest, busy and done, at the toast not covering the table’s actions, at the menu’s opaque paper plate and its greyed entry with its reason, and at the Actions column staying put in both row looks.
