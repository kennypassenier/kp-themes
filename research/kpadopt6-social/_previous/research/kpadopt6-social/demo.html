The sixth adoption round, social: five parts for people who plan and talk together

Second round: only the two parts not approved yet are asked (who can come, the console); the packing list, the QR code and the time field were approved. Formal only, as every new part starts. One topic per part, five in all: who brings what on a packing list, who can come on which day, a QR code for a link, a field for a time of day and a console with a prompt. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does, and, for the few real choices, sets the default and lists the other options to switch to in the review dialog.

Everything here is the package running on its own stylesheets. Only the data is made up: the trip, the people, the dates, the links and the commands are samples, and a pretend server answers after a short wait.

Who can come

Who can come

People in the rows, days or proposed periods in the columns. Each person sets their own row; the column everybody can is ringed and starred. It is the package's heat grid, made editable. An organiser can switch a trip between periods and days.

Expected:

  • Your own row (Kenny, on top, with a blue edge round its cells) answers a press: empty, can, maybe, cannot, each a glyph (check, tilde, cross) and a tone. The other rows do not react. The summary row fills from the bottom with the number who can; under the grid the legend explains the three glyphs and the ring.
  • Press a cell: only that cell shows the spinner for a moment, then the answer. Press Make the server refuse first and the cell goes back, shows a ! on red for 3 seconds and a line says which cell was not saved.
  • Drag across your own row (mouse): every cell you cross gets the same answer, and one Undo (6 seconds) takes the whole stroke back. With a cell focused, y m n set can, maybe and cannot, Backspace clears; in someone else's row they do nothing.
  • Choose these dates appears once the best column is known. The switch Periods / Days swaps the columns (the answers are kept per set). A column header press picks the column; a summary cell's hover card names who can, maybe and cannot.
  • Loading keeps the grid's shape with the busy card over it and the names beside it; No dates yet and Failed are plates at the grid's full height, the failed one with Retry; Dates chosen makes every cell inert and says why. At 390 px the name column stays and the columns scroll; the second phone frame shows the list per column.
  • The groups marked (live option) change one setting of the main component while it runs and it redraws at once, nothing reloads: every choice you are asked about is a real option with a default.

Formal's plain ground: the tones are the package's own quiet green, amber and red, the best column a 2 px navy ring, and your row's cells carry a thin navy edge so it is the one that looks pressable.

Console

Console

A prompt and, for every command, a block that shows whether it still runs, worked or failed. The package draws the prompt, the recall of earlier lines and the blocks; what a command may do stays with the app. Your earlier lines are kept only while this tab is open (a line can hold a secret).

Expected:

  • Type uptime and press Enter (or Run): a block appears with the command in the mono face, Sending…, then Running for 1 s with a spinner, then exit 0 · Done with the output under it. Up and Down walk your earlier lines; Esc clears the line; Ctrl+L clears the screen and a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds. The key : jumps to the prompt from anywhere on the page (press ? for the list).
  • The sample buttons run the other cases: a long output (folded to 12 lines with Show all), one that prints as it goes with Cancel, a failing one (exit 1 · Failed), a line with no output ((no output)), one the app refuses (what and what to do now, your line stays in the prompt), a dropped connection (Not sure it finished with Check again) and one over the 4 KB limit (Only the first … shown). rm -rf / needs a second press: Run it anyway?
  • Each block has Copy command, Copy output (each says Copied in the same width), Run again and Collapse. When you scroll up in a long list, a pill says how many commands arrived out of sight and jumps back.
  • Empty, loading, failed and a console with running switched off all keep the body's height; the empty one invites you to run a command, the failed one has Retry. At 390 px the select goes above the prompt, Run takes its own full line and the bar stays at the foot.
  • The groups marked (live option) change one setting of the main component while it runs and it redraws at once, nothing reloads: every choice you are asked about is a real option with a default.

Formal keeps it plain: a card with a hairline, the output blocks on white over a pale ground, the prompt glyph and the focus ring in navy, the exit codes in the success green and the destructive red with their glyphs.
