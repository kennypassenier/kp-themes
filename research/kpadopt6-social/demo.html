The sixth adoption round, social: five parts for people who plan and talk together

Formal only, as every new part starts. One topic per part, five in all: who brings what on a packing list, who can come on which day, a QR code for a link, a field for a time of day and a console with a prompt. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does, and offers the few real choices as boxes to tick in the review dialog.

Everything here is the package running on its own stylesheets. Only the data is made up: the trip, the people, the dates, the links and the commands are samples, and a pretend server answers after a short wait.

Who brings it

Who brings it

A packing list where each item shows who brings it, and an item nobody brings is loud. Several people may bring one item (a pot for the stove, two people for the tent). It is the package's checklist with data-kp-checklist-claim added; the page says who is looking and answers each press.

Expected:

  • Each row ends in a column of its own: the people who bring it (their coloured mark and name) and one button. Rows nobody brings have a red edge, a ! and the words Nobody yet; the bar says how many.
  • Press I'll bring it on Gas stove: the button shows the spinner for a moment (the row does not move), then your name joins and the button says I'm bringing it. Press it again to let go: a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds.
  • Press I'll bring it on Tent: the pretend server answers that Tom took it first. The row gets a red outline for 3 seconds, says so in words and goes back to what Tom's claim makes of it. Nothing you typed or ticked is lost.
  • The chips All, Nobody yet, Mine carry exact counts. Nobody yet shows only the rows nobody brings; when none is left the list says Everything is taken and offers all items back. The key c on a focused row takes or lets go; Shift C toggles the gaps (press ? for the list).
  • Not signed in, the buttons are off and say why on hover or focus. The state buttons below show loading (the busy card over the list), empty and failed (with Retry), each at the list's full height. At 390 px the claim cell moves under the label and the button fills the line, 44 px high.

Formal's plain white card and navy ink: the gap is the only red on the page, a 3 px edge and a faint tint, so a missing person reads before any name does. The held button is the navy outline with a check; names are set in the body face, the marks in the palette the package already uses for people.
