The sixth adoption round, social: five parts for people who plan and talk together

Formal only, as every new part starts. One topic per part, five in all: who brings what on a packing list, who can come on which day, a QR code for a link, a field for a time of day and a console with a prompt. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does, and offers the few real choices as boxes to tick in the review dialog.

Everything here is the package running on its own stylesheets. Only the data is made up: the trip, the people, the dates, the links and the commands are samples, and a pretend server answers after a short wait.

Who brings it

Who brings it

A packing list where each item shows who brings it, and an item nobody brings is loud. Several people may bring one item (a pot for the stove, two people for the tent). It is the package's checklist with data-kp-checklist-claim added; the page says who is looking and answers each press.

Expected:

  • Each row ends in a column of its own: the people who bring it (their coloured mark and name) and one button. Rows nobody brings have a red edge, a ! and the words Nobody yet; the bar says how many.
  • Press I'll bring it on Gas stove: the button shows the spinner for a moment (the row does not move), then your name joins and the button says I'm bringing it. Press it again to let go: a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds.
  • Press I'll bring it on Tent: the pretend server answers that Tom took it first. The row gets a red outline for 3 seconds, says so in words and goes back to what Tom's claim makes of it. Nothing you typed or ticked is lost.
  • The chips All, Nobody yet, Mine carry exact counts. Nobody yet shows only the rows nobody brings; when none is left the list says Everything is taken and offers all items back. The key c on a focused row takes or lets go; Shift C toggles the gaps (press ? for the list).
  • Not signed in, the buttons are off and say why on hover or focus. The state buttons below show loading (the busy card over the list), empty and failed (with Retry), each at the list's full height. At 390 px the claim cell moves under the label and the button fills the line, 44 px high.

Formal's plain white card and navy ink: the gap is the only red on the page, a 3 px edge and a faint tint, so a missing person reads before any name does. The held button is the navy outline with a check; names are set in the body face, the marks in the palette the package already uses for people.

Who can come

Who can come

People in the rows, days or proposed periods in the columns. Each person sets their own row; the column everybody can is ringed and starred. It is the package's heat grid, made editable. An organiser can switch a trip between periods and days.

Expected:

  • Your own row (Kenny, on top, with a blue edge round its cells) answers a press: empty, can, maybe, cannot, each a glyph (check, tilde, cross) and a tone. The other rows do not react. The summary row fills from the bottom with the number who can; under the grid the legend explains the three glyphs and the ring.
  • Press a cell: only that cell shows the spinner for a moment, then the answer. Press Make the server refuse first and the cell goes back, shows a ! on red for 3 seconds and a line says which cell was not saved.
  • Drag across your own row (mouse): every cell you cross gets the same answer, and one Undo (6 seconds) takes the whole stroke back. With a cell focused, y m n set can, maybe and cannot, Backspace clears; in someone else's row they do nothing.
  • Choose these dates appears once the best column is known. The switch Periods / Days swaps the columns (the answers are kept per set). A column header press picks the column; a summary cell's hover card names who can, maybe and cannot.
  • Loading keeps the grid's shape with the busy card over it and the names beside it; No dates yet and Failed are plates at the grid's full height, the failed one with Retry; Dates chosen makes every cell inert and says why. At 390 px the name column stays and the columns scroll; the second phone frame shows the list per column.

Formal's plain ground: the tones are the package's own quiet green, amber and red, the best column a 2 px navy ring, and your row's cells carry a thin navy edge so it is the one that looks pressable.

QR code

QR code

A link shown as a QR code that a phone can scan, with the link as text and a Copy button under it. The code is drawn on the page itself: the link never goes to a service.

Expected:

  • A square white plate with dark modules and a wide white margin all round (the quiet zone), the link as text under it that wraps at any character, and Copy link. Point a phone camera at the first one: it opens https://kp-soft.dev/invite/7Hq2-nT9a.
  • Copy link shows Copied for a moment in the same width; with the focus on the code, Shift+C does the same. The state buttons show making the link (the busy card in the square), no link yet, a link too long for the format (the text stays copyable), an expired link (the code is gone, the link struck) and a hidden code with Show QR code.
  • Every state has the same square and the same caption height, so nothing jumps. A link over 512 bytes still draws, with a line that says it gets dense.
  • At 390 px the code is the full width up to 16 rem, centred, and Copy fills the line, 44 px high. The last frame puts the link and Copy first.

Formal frames the white plate with its own hairline border and radius and nothing else: no shadow, no tint, so the quiet zone is exactly the white a scanner needs.

Time field

Time field

One field for a time of day in 24 hours: typed, stepped with the arrow keys or picked from the package's own list. Unlike the browser's own time box it never shows AM and PM, and it looks like the rest of the theme.

Expected:

  • Type 0230, 2:30, 02.30, 2h30 or 230 and leave the field: it says 02:30. Type 7: 07:00. Type 24:00 or abc: the box turns red and the line says Use a time like 03:00; what you typed stays. Paste 2:30 PM: it becomes 14:30 and a quiet line says it was read as 24 hours.
  • Up and Down step by 5 minutes (Shift or Page Up and Down by an hour), 23:55 up is 00:00 and is announced as the next day. The clock button (or Alt+Down) opens a list: the nearest time is already highlighted, Night 03:00 and Morning 08:00 are chips above it, times within an hour of the nightly round 03:00 carry a ! and near 03:00. Enter picks, Esc closes.
  • The sentence below keeps its underline look; the field with a reason is off and says why; the loading one shows its own skeleton at its final width; the one whose presets failed shows a compact plate with Retry in the list.
  • At 390 px the field fills the line, 44 px high, and the list is a sheet at the foot of the screen with a title and rows 44 px high.

Formal's plain input box (navy focus ring, white ground) with a quiet clock at its end; the list is the same raised plate as a menu, the nearest time on a navy wash. In the sentence the same field is the navy underline in semibold.
