The side navigation at the window's edge, and its rail

Kenny, 2026-10-13 (translated from Dutch): "That same side navigation must have a button (e.g. a hamburger icon) at the very top left of the page that is static; the sidebar itself must come all the way out of the left side of the screen and push the other content to the right (or there must be enough margin so nothing is obstructed; make both demos)." And: "Maybe there should also be an option that does obstruct the content: make three demos and/or the option to choose the version through attributes." Pages 1 to 3 are three app pages, each in its own window, each with the button with three bars fixed at the top left. The navigation runs the full height of the window, its own items start beside the button, and it opens and closes with formal's slide; the close is the open backwards. Nothing dims the page in any of the three.

The three are one component with an attribute each, so a page picks one: data-kp-sidenav-mode="push" pushes the page, the same with data-kp-sidenav-reserve keeps the room free (the gutter), and data-kp-sidenav-mode="over" slides over the page. Pages 4 and 5 show the options you chose from the other libraries the same evening: names as tooltips and groups beside the icon rail, the width below which the rail collapses, and a rail that widens after a short rest of the pointer or on keyboard focus. A shut navigation is out of the tab order, Control+B opens and closes pages 1 to 3, page 3 names where the focus lands, and pages 1 and 3 carry the small tab at the edge. Judge them in the review dialog ("Review in a dialog" at the top) or open each page on its own.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so the navigation appears and disappears without sliding. Turn it off to judge the movement.

1 · Push

The navigation pushes the page aside.

Press the three bars at the top left: the navigation comes out of the left edge of the window and the whole page (header, figures, cards) moves right by the navigation's width, together with it. Press the bars again (now on the accent plate, beside the title North site): the navigation goes back into the edge and the page comes back, the same movement backwards. The small tab with the grip at the left edge, halfway down, rides on the navigation's edge and opens and closes it too. Check that the bars never move and never cover a link, and that Escape closes it. Tab through the page while it is shut: the navigation's links are skipped (a shut navigation is inert), and they come back the moment it opens. Press Control+B (⌘B on a Mac) with the focus anywhere but a text field: it opens and closes too. On a phone (40rem or narrower) the navigation lies over the page instead of pushing it, so nothing scrolls sideways.

4 · Collapsed rail

In the icon rail, names show as tooltips and groups open beside the rail.

The navigation stands as a rail of icons. Rest the pointer on an icon, or Tab to it: its name shows beside the rail as formal's tooltip, and leaves as it came. Rest on the Readings or Maintenance icon: the group opens beside the rail as formal's popover and closes as that opening backwards when the pointer leaves; from the keyboard, Enter or the Right arrow opens it and goes into it, Escape or the Left arrow folds it and returns to the icon. Press the button at the foot (») to widen the rail: the groups fold in place again. Make the window 60rem wide or narrower: the rail collapses by itself, and wider again it is what you left it.
