HTTPSwitchboard: try a translation, and the history of reloads

HTTPSwitchboard accepts a message in one shape and delivers it in another; here the messages are Alertmanager alerts and the destination is Home Assistant. Two pages the service does not have yet, both new use cases found while planning its move to kp-themes 11.0.0: Try a translation (see what a profile makes of a message without sending anything to Home Assistant) and the history of reloads of the profile file (today a refused reload is only in the log and in a metric).

Judge in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top), formal only. Mock on this page: the second profile uptime-kuma, every sample message, the template engine that renders the two templates, and the reload record. The Home Assistant address is a credential and is never shown. Every kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the hs- rules only lay the page out. Nothing moves until a button is pressed.

Try a translation

Try a translation: the whole page

Pick a profile, paste a sample message, and see the body that would be delivered, next to what changed. Nothing is delivered to Home Assistant; it is a preview.

Expected:

  • New use case (row 9 of the plan): today the only way to see what a template makes of a message is to send one through to Home Assistant.
  • Valid (the default): the example Alertmanager message with 2 alerts is in the box, the body on the right reads alert DiskAlmostFull, count 2 and both summaries joined with “; ”, and the comparison underneath shows the received lines removed and the delivered lines added.
  • No severity or instance: the body still renders, with “warning” and “unknown”, and a quiet note under the alert area says which defaults the template filled in.
  • No alerts (template error): the box is valid JSON but alerts.0 does not exist. An alert says what happened, why, and what to do now; its button puts the example message back. The body and the comparison say there is nothing to show.
  • Broken JSON: the box names the line and column; the cards say the message is not JSON yet. Empty box: both cards ask for a message.
  • Loading: the busy card sits over the body and the comparison, which keep their size. Service did not answer: both cards show the failure with Try again, which returns to the normal answer.
  • Pressing the other profile (uptime-kuma, a mock) swaps the template and the example, and everything follows.

Formal’s double-ruled cards and its mono type in the code and the diff. Look at the three cards keeping one footprint in every state, the line number gutter of the box, the sign column of the diff (a + or - beside each line, not only a colour), and the quiet note for a default that was filled in.

The sample box: every option of the JSON field

                    
A text box that knows JSON: it checks while you type, says where the JSON is wrong, and can lay the text out.

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Valid: the quiet line under the box reads “Valid JSON” with the number of keys. Broken JSON:
                            the line names the line and column (“Line 14, column 5: …”) and that line is marked in the gutter.
                            Empty: the line says Empty. Not JSON: a plain sentence is refused, because a translation needs JSON.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Checking: While typing (default) checks 300 ms after the last key; On leaving waits until the box
                            loses focus. Type a character into the box in each to compare.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Formatting: Button (default) shows Format and Minify beside the box; When leaving lays valid JSON out
                            as you leave the box and shows no buttons; Off takes the tools away.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Look: line numbers (default on), wrapping long lines (default scroll inside the box), and a 4-space indent (default
                            2) that shows when you press Format. The plain-text mode of the field is not offered: a translation of something that is
                            not JSON means nothing.
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Formal’s field plate with the gutter at its left: the numbers must stay clear of the text, the marked line must be
                        readable, and the Format and Minify buttons must keep their place when the line under the box changes.

When the template fails: the problem alert

                    
An alert with three fixed parts: what is wrong, why it is, and what to do now, with the fix beside them.

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Template error: one alert, not closable (a real problem stays until it is mended), with What, Why and
                            What now, and the button Use the example message beside them. Pressing it replaces the alert with the notes
                            of that message.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Notes: the quietest severity (info), each closable with its own close button: defaults the template filled in.
                            Closing one leaves the other where it was.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Error and 3 notes shows the worst first. Layout is the component’s option: One each (default),
                            One list in a single alert, and Fold from 2, one alert with the worst open and “Show the other
                            N”.

                        Formal’s alert plates: the severity is a shape and a word as well as a colour. Look at the three parts lining up in one
                        grid whatever their length, the fix button keeping its place, and the area keeping its height when the alerts are gone.

History of reloads of the profile file

                    
Every time the service read its profile file again, newest first: what changed, what needs a restart, and what was refused.

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • New use case (row 11 of the plan): a reload that was refused is only in the journal and in a metric today.
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Ready: the timeline lists 8 reloads with the newest at the top, each with its date (dd/mm/yyyy HH:MM) and an
                            outcome in one column: Applied (names the profiles that changed), Needs a restart (says why) and
                            Invalid (says where the file is wrong). The count line reads “8 reloads · 4 applied · 2 need a
                            restart · 2 invalid” and follows every arrival.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            The alert: the latest reload is invalid, so one alert says what is wrong, why, and what to do now, with the command
                            to run (Copy beside it). It goes away by itself when a later reload is applied.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            A reload arrives: the row appears at the top and the count line moves. While you read the older reloads (press
                            Scroll to older first), the row is counted in a pill, “1 new reload · back to the top”; pressing
                            the pill, or the Home key, jumps back to the top.
                        
    • 
                        
  • Loading, Empty and Failed keep the card’s size; Failed has Try again.
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Formal’s timeline: a solid ink dot and a hairline down the left. Look at the outcome words standing in one column for
                        every row, the long text wrapping inside its own column, and the pill sitting at the top of the list without covering the
                        first row’s date.
