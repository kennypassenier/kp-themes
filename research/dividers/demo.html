Dividers: softer shapes for pastel, and a knob to choose one

Kenny, on catalogue/page-effects.html#dividers in pastel: "The sawtooth isn't really fitting with 'Pastel' which is fluffier and rounder. And there should be an option." This page shows the pastel divider as it ships, five softer shapes, then a proposed knob, data-kp-divider-shape, switching live. Parts marked package are the stylesheets as they ship: the surfaces (data-kp-surface), .kp-section, .kp-stack, .kp-footer, the radio group (.kp-fieldset) and the dividers' own markup. Parts marked mock use the knob, which does not exist in the package: its rules are in this page's <style>, written as they would ship. The page opens in pastel; loaded registers: pastel, shade-light, dark. The theme menu in the bar reaches the other themes.

mock Theme:

0 · As it ships package

Look at: pastel's torn tabs from css/pastel-register.css: a 3.2rem bar in --primary cut to a zig-zag by a percentage clip-path, then the alt bar in --accent-foreground (a dark mint) with the teeth on top. The points are hard and the teeth stretch with the width, because the polygon is in percentages. This is what the five shapes below are measured against. In shade-light and dark this block shows their own drawings, untouched.

1 · Scallop mock

Look at: the same bar and the same two inks as today, but the edge is a row of half circles, the shape of a cake doily or a paper cupcake case. The circles are a fixed 1.5rem across (--kp-divider-tile) and the count rounds to fit the width, so there is never a half circle at either end and the circles stay round at any width. The alt one faces up into the app surface. Check the seam between the flat bar and the circles: there must be no hairline gap.

2 · Wave mock

Look at: a smooth, even wave with no corners at all: one tile is 3rem wide and 0.75rem deep, drawn as a small SVG path through the mask, repeated and rounded to fit. It is the calmest of the band shapes. Check that the wave is continuous where one tile meets the next (no step), and that the alt wave is the same wave mirrored, in the mint ink.

3 · Cloud mock

Look at: the "fluffiest" reading: round bumps of two sizes (a large one between two smaller ones, repeated every 4.5rem), like the underside of a cloud. It has more character than the scallop and less regularity; judge whether the small cusps where two bumps meet read as soft or as points. The alt cloud faces up.

4 · Pearls mock

Look at: no bar at all: a string of round beads (0.75rem, one every 1.5rem) on a 1px thread, spread with space so the first and last bead touch the edges. Much lighter than the band shapes; the surfaces meet with only the beads between them. Check whether this is too quiet to separate the hero from the app surface in pastel, where both grounds are pale.

5 · Soft rule mock

Look at: the plainest option: a 3px rule that fades out over its first and last 18 %, with three round beads at the centre. No edge shape at all, so it suits a page where the surfaces themselves already differ. Check that the fade reaches transparent at both ends and that the beads sit exactly on the rule.

6 · The knob, live mock

Look at: choose a shape and the two dividers in the frame change at once; the line under the radios shows the attribute a consumer would write. Theme default removes the attribute, so the register's own drawing returns (pastel's zig-zag, shade-light's blurred seam, dark's scale). Soft ink mixes both inks toward the page ground. The third divider, between the two app surfaces, carries its own data-kp-divider-shape="pearls": it must stay pearls whatever the frame is set to, which shows that the nearest element naming a shape wins. Switch to dark: every shape must still draw, in dark's own --primary. In a consumer the attribute would normally sit on <html>; here it sits on the frame so the other blocks on this page keep their own shapes.

7 · At 320 px mock

Look at: every shape in a 20rem (320 px) frame, the width of a small phone. The scallops, waves, clouds and beads must keep their size and simply be fewer; nothing may squash or stretch, and no divider may be wider than its frame (no sideways scroll). Compare the zig-zag frame, which uses the knob's fixed-size teeth rather than the register's percentage polygon.
