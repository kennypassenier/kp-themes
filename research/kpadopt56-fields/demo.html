The fifth and sixth adoption rounds, part 3 of 12: Fields and inputs

Formal is approved. Entering a value: a slider with a typed value beside it, a time of day, a text box that grows, a JSON box that checks what you type, and a star rating. This part asks the eleven other themes for 8 sections (88 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

Slider: one value and a range, with the value in a box

<div class="kp-slider" data-kp-slider> (js/slider.js): the native range input, drawn by the package, with the number in a small box beside it that you can type in. Drag, use the keys or type.

Expected: the bubble over the thumb is off by default (the number is in the box beside the track) and is an option you switch below (data-kp-slider-value: field, both, bubble, none).

  • On load no slider has a bubble. Press Box and bubble, Bubble only or No number: every slider below follows at once (the bubble shows while a thumb is dragged or has the focus: click a thumb or use Tab). Box only is the default. Drag, type 23.5 and press Enter, use the arrow keys.
The number shows:

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Time field

                    

                        One field for a time of day in 24 hours: typed, stepped with the arrow keys or picked from the package's own list. Unlike the
                        browser's own time box it never shows AM and PM, and it looks like the rest of the theme.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Type 0230, 2:30, 02.30, 2h30 or 230 and leave the field: it says 02:30. Type
                            7: 07:00. Type 24:00 or abc: the box turns red and the line says Use a time like 03:00;
                            what you typed stays. Paste 2:30 PM: it becomes 14:30 and a quiet line says it was read as 24 hours.
                        
    • 
                        
  •

                            line. The 390 px box is narrow, so it has no line numbers there. Press Broken (line 3): line 3 is marked in the
                            gutter.

Star rating: give one, show one

                    

                        <div class="kp-rating" data-kp-rating> (js/rating.js): five whole stars to give something a score, and the
                        same row read only to show one. An empty row says it is empty; it never looks like a zero.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Lasagne (first case) starts with five outlined stars and the words Tap a star under them. Press the fourth
                            star: four are filled in the accent colour (navy in formal, never red or green), the words say Great, a small
                            Clear button appears under the row (on a mouse only), a toast says Lasagne: 4 stars with Undo, and
                            the grey box under the case shows the event. Press the fourth star again: the rating goes (rating removed). Nothing
                            under the row moves when the words change.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Keys: Tab reaches the row once (one tab stop); the arrows move and choose, Home and End go to the
                            first and last star, Backspace or Delete removes the rating. No digit keys (they need Shift on your
                            keyboard). With a mouse, the stars up to the one under the pointer show lighter before you press (the second row of the
                            first case has this switched off, to compare).
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Show one: a read-only row of small stars with the number beside it (4.5 (12), the count exact), and the row
                            of nobody’s rating reads Not rated yet with outlined stars. A screen reader gets one name for the row
                            (“Rated 4.5 of 5 by 12 people”).
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Buttons above change the Lasagne row while you look: seven stars, half steps, a read-only row, and a tap that only
                            picks; the row redraws at once and keeps its value. Half stars (an option, not the default): the same row answers
                            to a press on the left or right half of a star; the keys step by one half. Save rating case: a tap only picks, the
                            two buttons appear, and the page keeps the old value until Save.
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the
                        edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in
                        from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.
                    

                    

                        Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something
                        is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an
                        ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.
                    

                    

                        Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom.
                        Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing
                        tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.
                    

                    

                        Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up,
                        stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text
                        keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.
                    

                    

                        Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the
                        part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that
                        text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.
                    

                    

                        Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check
                        the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and
                        that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.
                    

                    

                        Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif,
                        and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than
                        faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.
                    

                    

                        Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name
                        what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no
                        glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.
                    

                    

                        Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type,
                        symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is
                        covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.
                    

                    

                        Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and
                        amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are
                        struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.
                    

                    

                        Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and
                        stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces
                        or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Star rating: states, a table and phone width

                    

                        A rating that is being read, one that could not be read, a save that is refused, one that is locked, and the stars in a table
                        and on a phone.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Loading (button above the first case): the five stars keep their size as dashed outlines that slowly breathe,
                            nothing moves around them. Could not read: the stars and the words are replaced, at the same height, by one
                            red-edged plate with Could not read the rating centred and a normal-size Retry; Retry fires
                            kp-rating-retry (the grey box). Save is refused: press a star; the row is busy for a moment, then the
                            previous stars come back and Could not save the rating for Lasagne stands under the row for three seconds.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Locked: stars dimmed, the sentence Locked while the recipe is being edited in muted ink under them (not a
                            tooltip). Read only: drawn as an input, you can Tab to it, a press does nothing.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Words that are long wrap under the row, never cut. In the table, each recipe has a read-only row of small
                            stars; click Rating in the header: the table sorts by the number, so the best recipe comes to the top; an unrated
                            recipe sorts last.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Phone width (390 px box): the five stars are 56 px wide each and fill the row evenly, the words under the row, no
                            Clear button (a phone has no pointer; press the chosen star again to remove).
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        In dark, waiting is a comet with a film tail over the whole component; empty and failed are one centred plate at normal size
                        with a normal-size button. Check each sample keeps the component’s footprint, the chamfered corners hold, and at phone width
                        nothing is cut or touches an edge.
                    

                    

                        In cyberpunk, waiting is signal loss: the word and the plate glitch while the whole component stays in place. Check loading,
                        empty and failed keep the full footprint, the red is used only for a real failure, and at phone width the cut corners clip no
                        text.
                    

                    

                        In synthwave, waiting is the marquee chasing along the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full
                        footprint, the chase is visible and moving, and at phone width the bloom clips nothing.
                    

                    

                        In pastel, waiting is washi tape fluttering over the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint,
                        the misregistered second ink is visible but readable, and at phone width nothing is cut.
                    

                    

                        In forest, waiting is the row of planted trees along the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full
                        footprint, the trees stand and grow as the bar fills, and at phone width nothing is cut.
                    

                    

                        In blueprint, waiting is the section hatched across the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full
                        footprint, the hatching moves, a failure is flagged in amber or red and not just a colour change, and at phone width nothing
                        is cut.
                    

                    

                        In solstice, waiting is the sun crossing the whole component and a warning reddens the sky. Check loading, empty and failed
                        keep the full footprint, the crossing is visible and calm, and at phone width nothing is cut.
                    

                    

                        In brutalism, waiting is a lintel hoisted across the whole component and a warning is taped off. Check loading, empty and
                        failed keep the full footprint, the hard steps are visible, and at phone width the shadow clips nothing.
                    

                    

                        In deco, waiting is lift doors over the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the doors
                        and the inlay move slowly and cleanly, and at phone width the gold edge cuts no text.
                    

                    

                        In nostromo, waiting is the raster building up on the screen across the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep
                        the full footprint, the lamps tell the state, and at phone width nothing is cut.
                    

                    

                        In titanium, waiting is the machined film sweeping the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full
                        footprint, the sweep is visible, and at phone width nothing is cut.
