Homelab dashboard: the Metrics page on kp-themes 11.0.0

The Metrics page shows how hard the host pve and each stack are working, with what the host did marked on every chart. This page rebuilds its charts, its time window, its key figures, its drives and its traffic from the package's own parts, in formal. Each section asks one thing to look at and carries its options as defaults you can flip; nothing is required.

Mock on this page: every reading, hostname and drive is sample data shaped like the dashboard's own answers (host pve, the stacks jellyfin, immich, paperless, kyu, almanac and the others). Three things need the dashboard's backend first and are drawn from sample data here: a custom start and end for the charts, the SMART history of the drives, and the request samples per hostname. Nothing moves until you press a button.

Live charts that keep your place

Refreshes the charts every 30 seconds and adds the new readings without redrawing the chart, so a pinned reading, a zoom and the sources you switched off survive each refresh.

Expected:

  • The refresh control (.kp-refresh) opens at every 5 min so nothing moves on its own; on the real page it opens at every 30 s. Pick Every 15 s or press Play once: six refreshes follow two seconds apart, each adding one reading at the right edge, and the line beside the control says “updated 0 s ago” and counts up.
  • Your place is kept: Pin a reading, Zoom to the last 10 min and a legend click (Memory per container, turn off a source) all survive the control’s own Refresh now and Play once. Today the page throws the whole chart away and draws it again on every refresh, so it has to skip a refresh while you read.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): whether a refresh may run while you are reading. Both are real options; the button group While reading flips them and a paused refresh says it is waiting.
  • Data: Loading pulses both plots at their final height; Failed fills each plot with the sentence and a Retry; Empty says there are no readings yet. The two charts keep their height in every state.

One crosshair runs through both charts. After a refresh the pinned tooltip is still on the same moment, the zoom chip still says what is zoomed, and the sources you switched off stay off. The right edge grows by one reading per refresh (a few pixels) and nothing else moves. Look at the gap between the legend values and their plate edges, and that the readout line keeps its height.

What the host did, marked on the charts

Marks every operation, update and failure the host ran on the charts, with the work that took time drawn as a window and the failures as lines, and lists the same work in a ribbon under them.

Expected:

  • A key above the chart names the three kinds in words with their marks: an operation the host ran, an update, a failure. It comes from the chart’s own kinds; today the page draws that key by hand.
  • Windows of work (the nightly round runs about 42 minutes, an update a few) are drawn as bands from the start to the end of the work (until). Failures stay lines. Point at or pin one: its tooltip names the kind and the time span (dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm).
  • The ribbon (.kp-gantt) lists the same work as bars in lanes; a failed one is taller with a cross. Press a bar: the line under the ribbon says which one was opened. Press a window button on the chart: both follow.
  • The choices (tick in the dialog): how a band is drawn, and whether the ribbon is shown. Both are real options; the groups Windows drawn as and Show flip them.
  • Data: Loading and Failed keep the chart’s height; the ribbon shows its busy card or its sentence in the same lanes.

Hatching reads as stripes in the plot, not as a second chart; the key words never touch their marks; the tooltip lists a window as “from to until”. In the ribbon the lanes line up under the chart’s own plot, a failure is the only taller bar, and nothing is cut at the lane labels.

Typical level, runs and too-early charts

Draws each source’s typical level and rings the stretches that stayed on one side of it, so “memory above its normal level for 9 hours” shows without a rule in the backend; a chart with too little history says so instead of drawing a trend.

Expected:

  • Memory per container over 7 days opens on immich alone (click the legend to add the others). A dashed line marks its typical level and ringed readings mark the stretch since the 1.5.0 update, about 9 hours and 30 minutes, when its memory stayed above it. The key names what the rings mean.
  • Just started (3 readings, fewer than the 48 the chart asks for) replaces the trend with one sentence in the plot saying it is too early, counting readings; One reading draws a dot and says the line grows as more readings arrive. Both keep the chart’s height.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): whether the typical line and the rings are on by default. All three are component options; the group Normal level flips them at once.
  • Data: Loading, Empty and Failed fill the same footprint.

The dashed typical line is quieter than the data line and its words sit clear of the plot edge; rings are visible on the run and nowhere else; the too-early and one-reading sentences are centred and never touch the plot edge.

The time window: presets and a custom start and end

Sets how far back every chart on the page reaches, as one key press for the usual lengths or an exact start and end for a question like “what happened on Saturday night”, with the window written out in full.

Expected:

  • The presets (.kp-range) are one segmented key with Custom… after them; under them one line says exactly which window is drawn: “Last 24 hours · 18/10/2026 14:05 – 19/10/2026 14:05” (dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm, 24-hour, Brussels). It replaces the page’s own window line.
  • Custom… opens a panel under the key with a date picker and a time for the start and the end; an end before the start, an end in the future or a window past the limit is refused under the fields with its reason, and Apply waits until it is right. Cancel or Escape keeps the window as it was.
  • All three charts redraw for the window you apply and say nothing else changed. The dashboard’s data endpoint takes a preset only today, so an exact start and end needs it to accept from and to (backend item C1: the adoption plan’s request to the dashboard’s server); here the readings are sample data for any window.
  • The choices (tick in the dialog): which presets are offered and how long a custom window may be. Both are component options; the groups Presets and Longest custom window flip them and the picker is drawn again.

The preset key and Custom… stand on one line at desktop width and take two lines on a phone with every preset the same width; the line under them never touches the plate edge; the opened panel is the theme’s opaque popover with the date and the time side by side.

A window of its own for each section

Lets each section of the page choose how far back its charts reach, so fast things (CPU) and slow things (disks) can each be read at their own pace.

Expected:

  • Compute opens on the last hour, Storage and temperature on 7 days. Each section has its own window keys and its own zoom chip; pointing at a chart moves the crosshair in that section’s charts only.
  • Press 7 d in Compute: only its two charts redraw. Zoom Compute zooms the two Compute charts together; the chip says what is zoomed and Reset undoes it, while Storage stays as it was.
  • The choice (tick in the dialog): per section, or one window for the whole page. Both are real options; the group Windows flips them, and with one window for the page a key in either section sets both.
  • Data: Loading and Failed fill every plot at its height.

The two sections read as two separate cards of the page with a heading and one sentence each; window keys and zoom chips sit on one line above their charts and do not move when a chip appears; the four charts share one height.

Which machine, and its key figures

Picks whose charts the page shows (the host or a stack, or up to three stacks side by side) and sums each up in a key figure with its last 24 hours inside it.

Expected:

  • The picker opens on The host (pve). The usual machines stand first (host, jellyfin, immich, kyu), the other ten follow by name; each has its colour mark (.kp-idmark) and, where the fleet names one, its container number. Picking a stack swaps the key figures to that stack’s (CPU, Memory, Disk, Apps running, Restarts in the last hour) and redraws the chart.
  • Compare turns the same picker into a field of tags (at most three). The chart then draws one CPU line per stack in the colour of its mark, and the key figures follow the first one.
  • A key figure shows its number, a sentence with now, peak and the sample’s window, and its last 24 hours as a line. Rest on a figure: a card says its highest and lowest reading with the time (dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm). Press it: the line under the chart says which chart it would open.
  • The choices (tick in the dialog): the kind of picker and whether comparing is on. Both are real options; the groups Picker and Compare flip them live.
  • Data: Loading keeps the tiles and the chart at their height, pulsing; Failed says “not read” in each tile.

Marks and names never touch; the usual band is separated from the alphabetical list by its heading; the tiles share one height whatever the scope, with their trend and axis words clear of the tile edge; the raised list is the theme’s opaque plate.

    Traffic: the status mix and who is asking

    Shows how many requests reached the gateway in the last 24 hours, how many failed, and which hostname each one was for, as a share bar, a chart and either a heat grid or a table.

    Expected:

    • The status mix (.kp-stackbar) is one bar over the exact total, split into 2xx, 3xx, 4xx and 5xx with the counts in a list under it; 4xx is the warning colour and 5xx the destructive one, each also with its pattern. Press a part: the hostnames below show that class only, press it again for all (the Status class keys do the same).
    • The heat grid (.kp-heat) has one row per hostname and one column per hour, darker meaning more requests for that hostname; an hour with server errors is striped. Press an hour: the chart zooms to it and the chip says so; press it again to release. Press a hostname: only its line shows in the chart; press again to turn it off.
    • The table has the same hostnames with exact counts, the share of all requests as a bar, and 4xx and 5xx counts; sort any column. Open a row for the requests the gateway kept (sample data, with authorisation headers masked and a Copy as curl).
    • The choice (tick in the dialog): heat grid or table. Both are real; the group Hostnames as flips them. Data: Loading lays the busy card over the grid; Empty and Failed fill the same footprint with a normal-size action.

    The stripes on the 5xx part and on the failing hour are a pattern as well as a colour; the grid cells keep one square size, the hostnames stand in one column as wide as the longest, and no count touches its bar. In the table the numbers are right-aligned and the sort arrows sit clear of the headings.

    Drives: health history and the readings that warn

    Lists each physical drive with its SMART health and the readings that warn before it fails, and opens a drive to show how long it has been healthy.

    Expected:

    • One row per drive (nvme0n1, sda, sdb, sdc) in a sortable table: SMART as a badge with its word, temperature as a bar with its number (amber from 60 °C, red from 72 °C, of 80), pending and reallocated sectors as exact counts, and the hours powered on with the years.
    • Open a row: the health strip (.kp-uptime) with the share of days healthy and the incident count in exact figures, then the 24-hour temperature and pending-sector lines. Ruled cells are degraded days (pending sectors appeared), striped ones are down (SMART not ok). With One drive failing sdb opens by itself and says since when.
    • The choice (tick in the dialog): 90 days or 48 hours for the strip. Both are real; the group Strip covers flips them. Data: Loading, Empty and Failed fill the table’s own footprint with a normal-size Try again.

    The temperature bar and its number stay on one line without touching; the sort arrows sit clear of the headings; the strip’s ruled and striped cells differ by pattern, not only by colour; the two small lines in the detail have the same height.
