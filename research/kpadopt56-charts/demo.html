The fifth and sixth adoption rounds, part 10 of 12: Charts and maps

Formal is approved. Drawings of data: chart markers and a median line, what uses what, the path of a connection, and who can come when. This part asks the eleven other themes for 6 sections (66 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

Chart markers and median line

Events on a chart as dots above the plot and, for a window of time (a maintenance window), as a band. A key names the kinds and switches them. A dashed median line, the points of long runs ringed, and “too few days to say anything” as a state of its own.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Expected:

  • First chart (three days of pressure): a key above the plot with a switch per kind (operation, update, failure, maintenance window) and its exact count; press one and its markers leave the plot and the switch is struck dashed; press again and they return. The maintenance window is a band; point at it for its start and end in words. Two events earlier than the window show as “◂ 2” at the left edge of the markers, one later as “1 ▸” at the right.
  • Median: a dashed line in the source’s colour with “median 3.4 bar” at its end; the key says it too. The screen reader’s label of the plot carries the value.
  • Second chart (28 days of a mood score): the median and, with Runs on, a ring on every point of a run of six or more on one side of the median (the key explains the ring). With Runs off the rings leave.
  • Third chart (3 readings, 7 needed): the three points faint, and in the middle of the plot’s full height “3 of 7 days needed before a trend means something” at heading size. Press “Enough readings” and the chart draws itself.
  • Loading and Failed keep the chart’s height (the existing states). 390 px: the median’s words leave the plot and are in the tooltip and the key.

Uses map: which CV is used by which application

<div class="kp-matrix" data-kp-matrix data-kp-view="lines"> (js/uses-map.js, a second view of the matrix): the CVs on the left, the applications that use them on the right, a line for each use. The same data as the grid view, one switch away.

Expected:

  • Map: five cards on the left (CV Developer NL, CV Developer EN, Bespoke CV, No CV and CV Developer 2024), seven on the right, and a curved line for every use. Each CV card says how many applications use it (3 applications); the 2024 CV says Not used and has no line. Bespoke CV and No CV are faint, dashed cards with dashed lines: they are not real files but still group applications.
  • Point at a card (or Tab to it): its lines turn the primary colour and thicken, the cards it touches stay clear, everything else dims. Press a card to pin that light; press it again, or press Esc, to let it go. The pin stays after you switch to the grid and back.
  • Keyboard: Tab enters once; ↓ and ↑ move down a column, → and ← jump to the other column and land on the first connected card, Home and End go to the ends, Space pins, Enter opens the card’s link (the small arrow at its corner) or pins when it has none.
  • Grid (the switch above the map): the same relation as the matrix you already know, a green tick where a CV is used. Map brings the lines back. The grey box under the map shows the event the page received.
  • Tick under How the lines run in the dialog: the map follows (curves, right angles, straight). The long name (Federal Public Service Policy and Support) wraps inside its card; the line aims at the middle of the wrapped card.

Who can come

                    

                        People in the rows, days or proposed periods in the columns. Each person sets their own row; the column everybody can is
                        ringed and starred. It is the package's heat grid, made editable. An organiser can switch a trip between periods and days.

  • 
                            Your own row (Kenny, on top, with a blue edge round its cells) answers a press that opens a small menu:

                            cross) and a tone. The other rows do not react. The summary row fills from the bottom with the number who can; under the
                            grid the legend explains the three glyphs and the ring.
                        
    • 
                        
  •

  •

                            whole stroke back. With a cell focused, y m n set can, maybe and cannot, Backspace clears; in
