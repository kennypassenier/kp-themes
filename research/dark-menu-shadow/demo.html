dark's dropdown menu: the halo, a quieter halo, or what ships

Since scope-102 dark's dialog, card and popover family are lifted off the ground by the oxide film's own colours — --kp-halo, four fixed shadows in chart-2, chart-1, chart-3 and chart-4. The navbar's dropdown was not in that decision, so it still casts 0 12px 28px of the theme's background token at 45 %, on a ground that is that same token: about two of 255. The question is whether the dropdown should join the halo, and if so how loud. Kenny, 2026-09-16 (scope-105): "maak een demo om het verschil te zien … het is veel beter om dingen met het oog te vergelijken."

The bar, the dropdown, the card and their voices are package: the markup below is catalogue/navigation.html's dropdown bar, unchanged, with data-kp-nav-menu-open on the Readings item so the panel is frozen open. The three shadows are mock: rules in this page's <style> block, written as [data-theme='dark'] .dm-<state> .kp-nav__menu so each goes into css/dark-register.css by dropping the wrapper class. State 2 is not a copy of the halo — it is var(--kp-halo), the register's own token. The panel ground is a mock <div> painted var(--card), which is what dark paints .kp-nav-wrap and every panel with. Registers loaded: dark, titanium, nostromo; the rest are fetched on a switch.

mock Theme:

Every block is judged in dark whatever the menu says — the stages carry data-cat-theme="dark". The switcher is there to show that these rules are dark's alone: the last block follows the menu.

1 · On the page ground package + shadow

Look at: the same bar three times, each with Readings open. Put your eye on the panel's bottom edge and on the grid lines that run under it. In 1 · as it ships the grid runs straight under the panel and the only thing holding it off the page is its own hairline. In 2 · the oxide halo a cyan-violet-magenta glow stands round all four sides, the same glow the card and the dialog have worn since scope-102. In 3 · a quieter halo the same colours reach about a third as far. Judge two things: whether the panel needs lifting at all here, where its own ground (--card, 8 % lightness) is already three points lighter than the page's (5 %); and whether the halo's colour reads as the theme's oxide film or as a glow under a menu.

2 · On a panel package + ground, shadow

Look at: the same three bars standing on a panel — the ground painted var(--card), which is the colour dark paints .kp-nav-wrap, every card and every popover. This is the hard case: the open panel's own background is --card too, so with the shipped shadow the panel is held off its ground by one hairline and nothing else. Look for the panel's silhouette in state 1 — you have to find the border to find the panel. In state 2 the halo draws the silhouette for you; in state 3 it does the same more quietly. Judge whether the quiet one is enough here, because this is the ground where a dropdown needs the most help.

3 · Open over a card package + shadow

Look at: the panel opens over a card, and the card already wears the halo itself (scope-102). This is where a halo can read as a double edge: two glows of the same four colours, one round the card and one round the panel, a few pixels apart along the card's top edge. Follow the card's top-left corner in each state and ask whether you see one lit edge or two. State 1 has no second glow at all; state 2 has the full one; state 3 has a third of it. The card, its title and its body are the package's, with the shadow the register gives them today.

4 · Opened by hand package + shadow

Look at: the three bars above are frozen open with an attribute. These three are not: hover Incidents, or Tab onto it, and the panel opens the way it does in an application, through the package's own rules and js/components.js. Judge the shadow while it arrives — the panel fades in, and a shadow that is right at rest can still flash on the way. Tab once more: the focus lands on the first link inside the open panel and the panel stays open. Escape or a click outside closes it.
