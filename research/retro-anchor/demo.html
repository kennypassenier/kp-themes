What is retro's anchor element

Kenny, 2026-10-07 18:13: every theme gets one recognisable anchor element, the one thing every later decision of the theme departs from (forest: the tree progress bar, grotesk: the red plate that falls into register, synthwave: the page horizon, blueprint: the tracing pen). The colours are already right everywhere and stay the shared base. Retro has no anchor yet: its register draws the 1995 desktop (the page one application window on the teal, a navy title-bar ramp, grey chrome and bevels, sunken wells, the dither, the navy progress blocks, the selection bar, the hourglass) and moves it in hard steps, nothing easing, but it has no one moving thing that decides how the rest arrives, waits, points, presses and leaves; today a dialog is a stepped scale and every other arrival is a stepped copy of it.

Update 1. Kenny on round one: “None of these, I want 3 more attempts at this, redesign it completely from the ground up, based on an old windows UI. It needs to feel really retro and oldschool. This theme has the potential to be one of the most distinct ones we have, so it needs to feel professionally done.” So none of round one’s mechanics returns: every scene is now the 1995 desktop itself, drawn as it was (the teal desktop with its icons, the taskbar with Start and the clock, windows with the title-bar ramp, a menu bar and a status bar, dialog boxes with their icon and the dotted focus rectangle), and the anchor is one moment of that desktop everyone remembers, in whole frames and never eased.

One question, eight new candidates, each drawn only from what retro's world already owns (the Copying dialog's sheet flying between two folders, Solitaire won and the cards cascading with their painted trail, Defrag reading and writing its blocks, the Start menu sliding up out of the taskbar, the message box that dings and flashes its title bar, Minesweeper's face and its field flooding open, the Flying Through Space screensaver, a window dragged by its outline). Each one is shown the same three ways, on one clock: in its own scene, as a progress bar and as a button press, so you see whether it carries the theme. Pick one in the review dialog (arrow keys flip between the options); the first option is the recommendation, and each option says what follows from it and why it is or is not recommended.

Replay starts them together, the speed buttons slow every motion down, and Show looks at one place alone and large.
