The fifth and sixth adoption rounds, part 3 of 14: Dated options, toggles and keys

Formal is approved. Options with a date, a button that stays pressed, and keys that know their own list. This part asks the eleven other themes for 5 sections (55 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

Dated options: radio rows, and a search for a long list

<div class="kp-dated" data-kp-dated> (js/dated-options.js): choose a snapshot, a backup or a version; every option carries its date, its age and a size. The demo clock stands at Saturday 17/10/2026 14:05.

Expected:

  • Six snapshots: radio rows, the newest first and already chosen: 17/10/2026 04:00 with a blue Latest badge, 10 h 5 min ago under it, and 4.3 GB at the end of the row. The sizes (4.3 GB, 812 MB) end on one line down the page.
  • Choose another row (click, or the arrow keys): the row takes the primary edge and the grey box shows the kp-dated-change event. Type b on the focused rows of the second picker and the option starting with b is chosen.
  • Named options: each has its name in bold and, under it, the age and the date. Being written is dashed and grey, says why, and cannot be chosen.
  • Twelve snapshots are too many for rows: one search field. Type 04/10 and the list narrows to the options with that date; choosing one writes only its date in the field. The list is shown open in its own frame too.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Toggle button: one, filters and a one-of switch

                    

                        <button class="kp-button" data-kp-toggle> (js/toggle.js): a button that stays pressed until it is pressed
                        again. The words never change; the state is aria-pressed. Press things; nothing here is stored.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            One toggle: Only errors is a plain button. Press it: the face turns dark, the words stay the same, and the
                            line Showing errors only appears under the words (the row is there all the time, so the button keeps its height).
                            Press again: back to plain. The pin beside it is the icon look, a square.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Filters: four pills All 138, Error 12, Warning 31, Info 95. All starts on. Press
                            Error: All goes off and Error goes on; press Warning as well: both are on and the list below says what is
                            shown. Press All (or Clear filters (2) at the end of the row): everything else goes off and All is on again.
                            The Clear button keeps its place when nothing is pressed (it is only invisible), so the row never moves. The key
                            W presses Warning when the focus is not in a field.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            A one-of switch: Day, Week, Month; exactly one is on, pressing the one that is on does
                            nothing, and the arrow keys walk along the three (Tab enters the group once). Single or none lets the on one be
                            pressed off.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Every press says one polite line below the row (read by a screen reader, shown here in the grey box). The count is the
                            real number, never “9+”; the next section feeds a count past 99 and a count that is still being counted.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Not new, and what is new: the package already has the segmented control (js/segmented.js), shown under
                            the three cases above with the same filters and the same one-of switch. A group of choices is that part’s job, and a
                            toggle group looks and answers the same way. What the toggle adds is what that part cannot do: a
                            single button that stays pressed (a pin, “Only errors”; the segmented control is always a group), a
                            half-on state, a line under the words and a tick, Clear filters (N), a one-of switch that can
                            be off, a key that presses it, and an app that answers after a wait (busy, then rolled back with a word if
                            refused).
                        
    • 
                        
  • The choice (tick it in the dialog): whether a pressed toggle also shows a tick. The buttons below flip it too.
    •

Shortcuts that know their own list

                    

                        registerShortcuts() with js/shortcuts.js: the list that handles the keys is the list that fills the
                        help sheet, so the sheet cannot promise a key that does nothing. Chords such as “g i” wait for their second key.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Press g: a small cap “g …” appears near the bottom edge and leaves after 1.5 s. Then press
                            i, h or c: the line under the list says
                            where you went. Typing in the field never fires a letter.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Click a row (or Tab into the list) and press j / k to move,
                            x to select. Shift D (delete) only works
                            while something is selected.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Press ? or “Show the shortcuts”: the sheet lists the list’s own keys first, then “Anywhere”.
                            Delete is dimmed with “Select a row first (x)” until a row is selected. The frozen copies beside it show the same sheet
                            filled and empty.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            “Register ]” is the AltGr check (warn): the key works, and the developer gets a warning with a better suggestion in the
                            line under the list. “Register a clashing key” shows the development error for two registrations of one key.
                        
    •
