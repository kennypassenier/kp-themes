The fifth adoption round, rows: seven new parts for lists that are worked through

Formal only, as every new part starts. One topic per part, seven in all: ticking rows with the keyboard, the selection bar, the toast with Undo, the pill for new lines, the payload cell, the JSON box and the request inspector. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does, and offers the few real choices as boxes to tick in the review dialog.

Everything here is the package running on its own stylesheets. Only the data is made up: the orders, stacks, schedules, log lines and requests are samples.

Ticking rows with the keyboard

Ticking rows with the keyboard and Shift-click

A table (or a list) of rows with a tick box is worked without the mouse: j and k walk a cursor, x ticks the row, Shift+x ticks everything up to the cursor, Esc unticks all. Shift-click on a box ticks a range. It is on for every data table that has a select column (data-kp-select-keys turns it on elsewhere, ="false" off).

Expected:

  • Click the first row's words, press j a few times: a ring (not a fill) walks down the rows and the line under the table says which row it is on. x ticks the row and the ring stays; a ticked row has the selected tint, the cursor row the ring, so the two read apart.
  • Tick the first row, then Shift-click the fifth box: rows two to four follow. The locked row (order 1008, its box disabled) is walked over and the readout says 1 skipped: cannot be selected. A plain click on a box still toggles one row, no Shift needed.
  • Search "Ghent": the rows that do not match are hidden, and a Shift range or Ctrl+A never ticks them. The cursor and the ticks stay on their rows (by key) when the table is sorted or searched.
  • Esc unticks everything and says so; Enter on the row writes "Opened: …" in the readout. Nothing moves in the page when the keys are used.
  • The 390 px column is the same list as cards: no keys there; press and hold a card (a long press on a phone) starts selection mode, then a tap ticks the next one.

The cursor is a 2 px navy ring just inside the row (the rule the clerk draws round an entry), the ticked row the paper's selected tint; the keys line under the table sets its key caps in the package's own style. Nothing fades or slides: a key moves the ring at once.

    The selection bar

    The selection bar over a table or a list

    A bar that is there as soon as rows are ticked: how many, which, the page's actions for them, and a way to untick all. It serves the table and the card list of the same rows (data-kp-selection-for), shows what you see, and offers the rest ("Select all 312 that match").

    Expected:

    • Nothing ticked: the bar's place is kept empty (the list under it does not jump) and no panel shows. Tick two stacks: the panel is ruled open from its start edge in about 0.4 s, with 2 selected, the names, the actions and Untick all. Untick them: it is ruled shut the same way back.
    • Tick six or more: the names wrap and the rest is an exact +N more, never an ellipsis; press it to list them all. The count keeps the room of the next digit.
    • Update is off when a pinned stack is ticked, and the line says why ("2 of 3 can be updated"). The 3 pinned ones are marked in the table.
    • Back up: the count gives way to a spinner and "Backing up 3…", the buttons stop answering, then it ends. With "Next action: some refused" it ends with 2 done, 1 refused: disk full and a rule plus the reason on the refused row. Delete hides the rows and offers Undo (the toast bed below).
    • Tick every row shown: Select all 312 that match is offered; taking it says All 312 that match are selected with a button to go back to the 8 shown. Esc or Untick all clears.
    • The 390 px column: the count and an Actions button; the names fold into the count, the actions open under it.

    The panel is an opaque paper plate in a strong hairline with a 3 px navy rule on its start edge (the same rule the toast and the alert carry); it is ruled open and shut from that edge in a straight line, 0.4 s, nothing fades. The refused row carries a red-ink rule on its start edge and the reason in words.

      The toast with Undo and a clock

      The toast with Undo and a visible clock

      After an action, say what happened and offer Undo for a few seconds, instead of asking "are you sure?" before. The clock shows how long is left. In the default mode nothing reaches the server until the window closes. The toast travels with you across in-app pages, and commits on a full page load.

      Expected:

      • Press Delete on a schedule: the row goes at once and a toast says "Nightly backup deleted" with the clock and Undo. After the window it is committed: the log line "Server: deleted …" appears and the toast goes. Press Undo, or Ctrl+Z while the toast is up: the row comes back and the toast says Restored for 1.5 s.
      • Hold the pointer on the toast, or focus it with Tab: the clock stops where it is and goes on from there when you let go (the ring and the seconds stop together). Esc on the toast accepts the action now.
      • Press Delete on four schedules quickly: at most three toasts stand, the oldest is committed when a newer one pushes it out.
      • With "Server: refuses" the commit fails: the toast turns into Not done: … with a Dismiss button and the row is back.
      • Compare the four clocks plays one toast per clock side by side, all starting together.
      • The 390 px column: the toast takes the whole width and Undo is a normal-size button.

      The ring is a hairline circle with a navy arc that is taken back at an even pace (linear, the way a rule is ruled), the seconds in tabular figures inside it; the bar clock is a 3 px navy rule along the foot. The toast is the package's own plate, opaque, with its start rule. Nothing bounces, nothing fades: the toast is ruled in and out.

        The "N new lines" pill

        The pill for new lines on a live list

        A log or a feed follows its newest line while you are at that end. Scroll away and the lines that arrive out of sight are counted in a pill: "12 new lines · back to the tail". One press jumps there. Scrolling back to the end follows again by itself.

        Expected:

        • At the end of the log, Add 5 lines: the log stays at its end and no pill shows. Scroll up a little (more than 24 px from the end) and add 5 again: the pill is ruled in at the bottom, 5 new lines · back to the tail; add one more: 6 new lines, the number exact, the pill's width kept.
        • Press the pill, or End in the log: the log jumps to the end, the pill is taken out. Scrolling back down to the end by hand does the same. Space in the log (or the Pause button) stops following; lines are counted until you resume.
        • Only errors hides the other lines: new lines that the filter hides are not counted. Add 120 lines with the log scrolled up: the pill reads 120 new lines, never "99+".
        • Clear the log: the log says it is empty, centred, and no pill shows.
        • The second list is newest-first with a pill at the top ("3 new messages · back to the top"). The 390 px column keeps the pill at the foot, centred.

        The pill is an opaque paper plate in a navy hairline, its words in the package's sans with the count in tabular figures; it is ruled in from its start edge in 0.4 s in a straight line and taken out the same way backwards. The divider is a navy hairline either side of the time.

              The payload cell

              The payload cell in a table

              A table cell that folds long content to three lines, says "binary, 2.1 kB" for binary data, shows a foot line when only the start was kept, and can lay JSON out. It is the code block as a cell (.kp-code--cell): the words wrap, so a row never scrolls sideways.

              Expected:

              • Seven rows, one payload each: plain text; a short JSON object (keys in the key colour, Pretty and Raw in a slim line under it); a JSON body of 38 lines folded to three with a fade and Show all 38 lines; binary data as one muted line binary, 2.1 kB with Download; a body of which only the first 1 kB of 4 kB was kept (foot line); an empty payload line; and a last row that you set with the control.
              • Show all 38 lines opens it (in place, or in a dialog with "Where it opens" set to a dialog); Show less closes. The other columns do not move in either case. Copy copies the whole payload, folded or not.
              • The last row: Loading shows a skeleton line, Binary, Empty and Failed one line each, in the height of a cell; nothing under it shifts.
              • The 390 px column: the table turns into cards and the payload takes the card's whole width.

              The cell is the code block on the package's muted ground in the mono face; JSON keys in the primary navy, punctuation quiet; a hairline above the tools. The fold is the package's own fade; nothing moves except the height of the row.

              The JSON box

              The JSON box that checks what you type

              A text field that checks JSON, points at the error line and can format. By default text that is not JSON blocks the form (data-kp-json="strict"); a field that may also carry plain text says so itself (="allow-text"), decided per field.

              Expected:

              • Press Valid: the box fills with a small object and the line under it says Valid JSON · 3 keys in the quiet ink. Broken (line 3): the box gets the destructive edge, the line says Line 3, column 3: … with the reason, and line number 3 in the gutter is marked (bold, tinted). Empty says "Empty".
              • Send with broken JSON does not send: the focus goes to the box and the form says so. With valid JSON it writes "Sent N bytes" below. The second box, which allows plain text, sends text that is not JSON and says "Plain text, not JSON".
              • Format lays the text out (2 spaces); Minify puts it on one line; Wrap wraps long lines and the line numbers follow the wrapped lines; Copy says Copied in the same width. The box grows with its text to 20 lines, then scrolls.
              • In the box, Tab inserts an indent and Shift+Tab takes one off; Esc, then Tab leaves the box, so the keyboard is never trapped (the line under the box says so). The "Long" sample shows the gutter and the growth.
              • Sending and disabled: the tools stand still. At 390 px the gutter is hidden and the tools sit at the start edge.

              The box is the package's field in the mono face; the gutter a muted column with tabular numbers; the error line in the destructive ink and bold, with the field's edge turned red ink (the accountant's entry), never a tinted plate. Valid is quiet grey: nothing celebrates.

              The request inspector

              The request inspector

              A foldable block per captured HTTP request: method, path, status (in its tone and in words), when; opened, the headers as key and value (secrets masked), the body as code, "showing the first N of M", and Copy as curl, as JSON or the body. In a copy the secrets are masked; a separate button copies the curl with the real values.

              Expected:

              • Seven requests, newest first, each a row of the same columns: the method, the status as a badge with its word (200 Success, 404 Client error, 502 Server error), the path (breaking after slashes), when (dd/mm/yyyy HH:MM, the exact ISO as its title). The two failed ones are open; one is marked test.
              • The authorization header shows ******** with Show beside it; Show reveals it on screen, Hide masks it again. The filter counts are exact; 4xx shows only the 404 and the other 4xx.
              • Copy as curl copies the command with the secret masked; Copy curl with secrets (a separate button, only where a secret exists) copies the real value. Copied shows in the width of the longer word. The body's own Copy copies the whole body.
              • A binary body shows the one muted line; a truncated one says "showing the first 1 kB of 4 kB"; a request without a body says "No body".
              • Loading lays the busy card over a list as tall as three rows; Empty is centred, large, with Send a test; Failed has a normal-size Retry. All three fill the list's height. 31 requests shows 20 and Show 11 older.
              • The 390 px column: the summary takes two lines (method and status, then the path).

              The list is ledger lines between hairlines; the statuses are the package's badges (green, blue, amber, red ink), each with its word. Open and close are the accordion's own, straight and exact. The busy card, the empty plate and the failure are the package's card states.
