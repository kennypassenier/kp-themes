Cook mode with tracks

You asked to cook the pasta while the sauce is made. A step can now belong to a track; the step of the track you are on stays large and every track stands in a lane with its place and its clock. 6 sections, 6 pairs (formal only: the lanes use the theme’s own buttons, bar and plates). Every option here only asks for its default. The recipe and the clock are mock data: the timers run about 20 times fast so a minute lasts 3 seconds.

What changed since you approved it: Cook mode had one line of steps, so cooking the pasta while the sauce was made meant remembering which timer belonged to which step. Now a step can belong to a track (Pasta, Sauce). The lanes show every track at once, one tap switches, and Back and Next move only the track you are on.

Cook mode with tracks: pasta and sauce side by side

The spaghetti recipe in three tracks. The step of the track you are on is large; every track stands in a lane beside it with its place, what it is doing and its clock.

Expected:

  • Beside the step stand three lanes, Pasta, Sauce and Together. The lane you are on has a coloured edge; each lane says Step 1 of 3 and what it is doing.
  • Press Start a timer on Boil the water, then Next: the Pasta lane keeps the clock running and says it is waiting. Tap Sauce (or press T): the sauce steps appear, large, where the Sauce track stood.
  • Back and Next move only the track you are on. Together stays locked until Pasta and Sauce are finished.
  • The clocks of the lanes stand in one column and do not jump while they count; the bar under each lane is that track’s own place.
  • The three buttons under Lanes and Detail are options of the component: they change the lanes at once.

Formal: the active lane has a primary edge, the three lanes share one height, their clocks line up in one column, and nothing is cut or touches an edge.

A step that waits for another

Adding the spaghetti must wait until the water boils. The step says what it waits for and how long, and offers the track that can go on.

Expected:

  • Press Put the water on: the timer starts and Next moves on to Cook the spaghetti, which waits. It shows Waiting for Boil the water with the time left counting down, and Next is off.
  • Go to Sauce takes you to the sauce at once; the Pasta lane says Waiting for Boil the water and keeps the clock.
  • When the water’s time is up the step opens by itself (the sentence changes to the step and Next works).
  • The option When a track waits decides whether the page offers the other track (Suggest, the default) or goes there by itself (Switch).

Formal: the waiting step keeps its sentence readable, the plate names what it waits for with the clock in figures that do not jump, and the button to the other track is as large as the other buttons.

A timer ends on a track you are not on

The sauce simmers while you cook the pasta. When its timer ends, the band above the step names the track and gives the button that goes there.

Expected:

  • Press Simmer the sauce, go to Pasta: the sauce timer starts (about 8 seconds) and the Pasta track opens.
  • When it ends, the band above the step says Simmer is done (Sauce) with Next: Warm the meatballs and a Go to Sauce button; the Sauce lane turns to a warning plate that says Time is up.
  • Go to Sauce clears the band and the plate and shows the sauce step. The timer card in the list keeps ringing until you stop it there.
  • The option The band turns the band off; the lane still says Time is up.

Formal: the band is the package’s own attention band, worst first, with its fix button in one fixed place, and the ringing lane is a warning plate with its words, not only a colour.
