What titanium is

Titanium is a material, not a place: bare machined titanium laid over a carbon twill, finished with an anodised oxide film. On real titanium that film is what makes the colour: heat or an anodising bath grows a layer a few hundred nanometres thick, and its thickness decides which colour survives, in a fixed order: gold, bronze, violet, blue, cyan.

So everything that happens on screen is something a machine shop does to titanium: it is cut (a panel opens along a line), milled (a part is revealed by a tool pass), indexed (a count steps), stamped (a tick lands) and heat-tinted (a change leaves colour behind). Nothing breathes, bounces, swells or spins.
