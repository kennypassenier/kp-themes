The fifth and sixth adoption rounds, part 7 of 14: Lists, records and pages

Formal is approved. Rows and what sits in them: ticking with the keyboard, who brings what, a list with its detail, new lines on a live list and payloads. This part asks the eleven other themes for 6 sections (66 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

Ticking rows with the keyboard and Shift-click

A table (or a list) of rows with a tick box is worked without the mouse: j and k walk a cursor, x ticks the row, Shift+x ticks everything up to the cursor, Esc unticks all. Shift-click on a box ticks a range. It is on for every data table that has a select column (data-kp-select-keys turns it on elsewhere, ="false" off).

Expected:

  • Click the first row's words, press j a few times: a ring (not a fill) walks down the rows and the line under the table says which row it is on. x ticks the row and the ring stays; a ticked row has the selected tint, the cursor row the ring, so the two read apart.
  • Tick the first row, then Shift-click the fifth box: rows two to four follow. The locked row (order 1008, its box disabled) is walked over and the readout says 1 skipped: cannot be selected. A plain click on a box still toggles one row, no Shift needed.
  • Search "Ghent": the rows that do not match are hidden, and a Shift range or Ctrl+A never ticks them. The cursor and the ticks stay on their rows (by key) when the table is sorted or searched.
  • Esc unticks everything and says so; Enter on the row writes "Opened: …" in the readout. Nothing moves in the page when the keys are used.
  • The 390 px column is the same list as cards: no keys there; press and hold a card (a long press on a phone) starts selection mode, then a tap ticks the next one.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

    A packing list where each item shows who brings it, and an item nobody brings is loud. Several people may bring one item (a pot for the stove, two people for the tent). It is the package's checklist with data-kp-checklist-claim added; the page says who is looking and answers each press.

    Expected:

    • Each row ends in a column of its own: the people who bring it (their coloured mark and name) and one button. Rows nobody brings have a red edge, a ! and the words Nobody yet; the bar says how many.
    • Press I’ll bring it on Gas stove: the button shows the spinner for a moment (the row does not move), then your name joins and the button says I’m bringing it. Press it again to let go: a toast offers Undo for 6 seconds.
    • Press I’ll bring it on Tent: the pretend server answers that Tom took it first. The row gets a red outline for 3 seconds, says so in words and goes back to what Tom’s claim makes of it. Nothing you typed or ticked is lost.
    • The chips All, Nobody yet, Mine carry exact counts. Nobody yet shows only the rows nobody brings; when none is left the list says Everything is taken and offers all items back. The key c on a focused row takes or lets go; Shift C toggles the gaps (press ? for the list).
    • Not signed in, the buttons are off and say why on hover or focus. The state buttons show loading (the busy card over the list), empty and failed (with Retry), each at the list’s full height. Another person acts lets Anna take the first free item and the list follows. At 390 px the claim cell moves under the label and the button fills the line, 44 px high.

    In cyberpunk, waiting is signal loss: the word and the plate glitch while the whole component stays in place. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the red is used only for a real failure, and at phone width the cut corners clip no text.

    In synthwave, waiting is the marquee chasing along the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the chase is visible and moving, and at phone width the bloom clips nothing.

    In pastel, waiting is washi tape fluttering over the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the misregistered second ink is visible but readable, and at phone width nothing is cut.

    In forest, waiting is the row of planted trees along the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the trees stand and grow as the bar fills, and at phone width nothing is cut.

    In blueprint, waiting is the section hatched across the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the hatching moves, a failure is flagged in amber or red and not just a colour change, and at phone width nothing is cut.

    In solstice, waiting is the sun crossing the whole component and a warning reddens the sky. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the crossing is visible and calm, and at phone width nothing is cut.

    In brutalism, waiting is a lintel hoisted across the whole component and a warning is taped off. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the hard steps are visible, and at phone width the shadow clips nothing.

    In deco, waiting is lift doors over the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the doors and the inlay move slowly and cleanly, and at phone width the gold edge cuts no text.

    In nostromo, waiting is the raster building up on the screen across the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the lamps tell the state, and at phone width nothing is cut.

    In titanium, waiting is the machined film sweeping the whole component. Check loading, empty and failed keep the full footprint, the sweep is visible, and at phone width nothing is cut.
