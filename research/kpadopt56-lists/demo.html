Ticking rows with the keyboard and Shift-click

A table (or a list) of rows with a tick box is worked without the mouse: j and k walk a cursor, x ticks the row, Shift+x ticks everything up to the cursor, Esc unticks all. Shift-click on a box ticks a range. It is on for every data table that has a select column (data-kp-select-keys turns it on elsewhere, ="false" off).

Expected:

  • Click the first row's words, press j a few times: a ring (not a fill) walks down the rows and the line under the table says which row it is on. x ticks the row and the ring stays; a ticked row has the selected tint, the cursor row the ring, so the two read apart.
  • Tick the first row, then Shift-click the fifth box: rows two to four follow. The locked row (order 1008, its box disabled) is walked over and the readout says 1 skipped: cannot be selected. A plain click on a box still toggles one row, no Shift needed.
  • Search "Ghent": the rows that do not match are hidden, and a Shift range or Ctrl+A never ticks them. The cursor and the ticks stay on their rows (by key) when the table is sorted or searched.
  • Esc unticks everything and says so; Enter on the row writes "Opened: …" in the readout. Nothing moves in the page when the keys are used.
  • The 390 px column is the same list as cards: no keys there; press and hold a card (a long press on a phone) starts selection mode, then a tap ticks the next one.
  • Changed: Click a row’s words, press x: the row is ticked and the readout says 1 selected (the Not approved box is not touched). Press j: the cursor ring, now the film (cyan along the top, magenta and green at the ends), steps down one row; the ticked row keeps its light plate.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.
