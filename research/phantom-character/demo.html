What makes phantom phantom

Phantom is print: black, white and one violent red, cut paper, a halftone screen over everything, condensed italic capitals, hard offset shadows and no light. The anchor you picked says what phantom does: it throws a screen that resolves. A part flies in as its own halftone, a silhouette of dots in its shape, slaps down with a dead stop and resolves into the solid thing in three hard cuts, as a print comes out of its screen; it leaves by dissolving into dots and being snatched off.

So everything that happens on screen is a print: a part is thrown and resolves (arriving, opening), dissolves and resolves again (a live update), waits as a screen that will not resolve (loading, the busy bar), and dissolves and is snatched (leaving). The throw is the shove with its dead stop, the resolve is hard cuts; nothing eases to a stop but the shove, nothing fades, nothing glows, nothing blurs.

Already decided by you: the anchor = thrown as a screen, resolves at the slap (the anchor round, update 1); round one’s progress bar stays (the skewed track, the black halftone well, the deep-red second plate, the white shard head); the network graph changes in no theme. The questions below turn the anchor into rules for every other component; where a decided pick is at odds with the rule, the pick is on the page as an option named “the pick” or “today”. The full analysis is in themes/phantom/CHARACTER.md.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so nothing on this page moves: every scene shows its finished pose, the part resolved and landed askew. Turn it off in the system settings to judge the motion questions (1 to 4, 9 to 13, 15 and 17).
