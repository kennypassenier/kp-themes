Dashboard components to port, round two

The rest of what the admin dashboard still keeps to itself: a menu of every other action, a meter with a tick, a strip of key figures that never leaves one tile alone on a row, a key figure with its 24-hour trend, a month of days coloured by how they went, a picture of a network, and a help drawer with a guided tour. Each section below is that component written already in the package's style (ports2.css and ports2.js next to this page), shown in formal only. Where there is a real choice, the review dialog asks it as options to tick, and the buttons in the section let you see each option first.

Each section shows the component at full width and, beside it, in a frame as narrow as a phone (360 px), so both widths can be judged on one screen. The data is made up: a water company's pump houses, readings and the nightly backups of its services. "Now" is 04/10/2026 14:40, and every date and time is the clock in Brussels.

A menu of every other action

.kp-menu-button with a .kp-menu--rich: a button that opens the rare actions, grouped under small headings, each with a label over a one-line hint. An action that cannot be used now stays in the list and says why. The keys are those of a menu: ↓ or ↑ on the button opens it, ↓ ↑ Home End move, a letter jumps, Esc closes, Tab closes and moves on. A refill while it is open waits until it closes.

Open More ▾: the labels and the hints start at one edge, the groups have their headings, and Run a pressure test is grey and says why it cannot run. Press ↓ until it is focused: the focus stops on it, and Enter does nothing. Press Refill in 3 s, open the menu and wait: nothing moves until you close it. In the phone frame the open menu spans the header's buttons. The Headings and Cannot be used buttons open the wide menu themselves, so each option shows the moment it is pressed; Cannot be used is about Run a pressure test, the grey entry, and where its reason sits.

A meter with a mark

.kp-meter works anywhere: in a tile, at the start of a line of words, in a table cell. --kp-value is the share; --kp-mark an optional tick (a target, a limit, what is booked), which may lie past the end. A screen reader hears the real numbers, past 100 % too, and setMeter() writes it all in one call.

Each meter is as thick with a mark as without one; the mark reads on the dark fill and on the light track alike. The row Booked tonight has its mark past the end: compare the two options. The tile with the red edge has a red fill; the warning row an amber one. Press Book more: the tick moves past the end and the words say 130 %.

Key figures that never leave one alone

.kp-kpis[data-kp-kpis-columns="all 3 2 1"]: the strip takes the first count in the list at which every tile is wide enough, measured by the strip's own width (not the window's). If that leaves one tile alone on the last row, it steps down to a count that does not; only when none exists does the last tile get the row to itself.

Pick a number of tiles and a width: there is never one tile alone on a row with empty space beside it, except where no allowed count avoids it (seven at 700 px, five at phone width), and there the option decides. Every tile in a row has the same height. The table under it is the spec's own list of cases, worked out by the same function.

A key figure with its 24-hour trend

.kp-kpi--trend: a key figure whose whole tile opens its chart (a stretched .kp-kpi__link), with the 24-hour trend inside it as a second stop for the keyboard. Under the line the axis says where it starts and that it ends now; a pointer, a finger or the arrow keys read one point, with its date, time and value.

Move the pointer over a trend: a thin crosshair and the reading, as 04/10/2026 02:40 · 3.12 bar, kept inside the tile. Tab to a tile (the whole tile gets the ring), Tab again to its trend, then ← → Home End. Flow into the network stopped at 14:00, so its axis ends at 14:00, not now; Reservoir South started this morning, so it says today. In the phone frame the tiles are narrow: the value goes under the date and time, and the corner shows only ↗.

A month of nightly backups

.kp-calendar: a month as a grid of days, each a plate in the colour of how its night went, with how many of the services were backed up under the day's number. Always six weeks tall. One tab stop: the arrows move a day or a week, Home and End go to the week's ends, Page Up and Page Down change the month (with Shift, the year), Enter picks. Today is the day in Brussels. Beside it, the page's own detail of the picked day.

Green nights had every service, amber some, red none; the dashed days are still to come or from before the first backup. Today, 04/10, has a dark inner ring; the picked day an outer ring. Press ‹ Prev twice and back: the grid keeps its height in every month. Try the states: Loading pulses every day in place, Nothing to check and Could not read stop the pulse and say why. In the phone frame the detail goes under the grid.

A picture of the network

.kp-graph: one node in the middle, the rest on a ring around it, the links drawn by kind, each kind a colour and a dash named in the legend. Hover or focus a node to see only its links; click it (or press Enter) to keep it, several at once; Esc or Show all clears. The graph is one tab stop; the arrows walk the ring. A label points away from the middle and is shortened where it would touch another, the full name in its tooltip.

Hover Pump house 3: only its links stay, the rest fades. Click two nodes: both stay picked. Turn a kind of link off in the legend. Press Fifteen long names: in the phone frame no two labels touch and none is cut off at the edge; those that had to be shortened end in … and show the full name on hover. The hint sits under the picture, never over it.

Help, and a short tour

.kp-drawer: Help as a drawer at the side, with a head, a body that scrolls, and a foot that stays. Its cards hold .kp-help__lists, words beside their meaning. startTour() walks a reader over the page, one card per part: the count is exact (a part not on the page is left out before counting), Back and Next, Esc ends, and the focus goes back where it was. A finished tour is remembered.

Press Help in the little app: the drawer comes in from the right, its foot stays while the body scrolls. Press Take the 1-minute tour: the first card says 1 of 5 (the sixth step points at a map this page does not have), sits under or over its part without covering it, and Back, Skip, Next work, as do ← → and Esc. In the phone frames: the drawer as it looks on a phone, and a tour over the phone's tab bar.
