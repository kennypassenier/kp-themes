dark's dialog shadow: five options

Dark's dialog casts its shadow in the theme's own background colour at 45 % opacity. On the theme's own ground that is about two of 255 darker than what is behind it, so the dialog lies flat on the grid. Kenny, 2026-09-16 (scope-101), asked to see the options rather than be handed one: "toon alle opties in een demo". Each block below shows the same three surfaces — an open dialog, a card and an open menu on a popover — under one option, with the separation each one measures printed underneath.

The dialog, the card, the popover, the menu and their chamfers are package: nothing in this page's markup differs from catalogue/overlays.html. Every elevation is mock: rules in this page's <style> block, written as [data-theme='dark'] .ds-<option> .kp-dialog so they go into css/dark-register.css by dropping the wrapper class. The card also carries a mock .ds-elev wrapper, because the card clips its own paint away and cannot cast anything until it is restructured the way the dialog was — that is the card's cost, and only the card's. Registers loaded: dark, titanium, nostromo; the rest are fetched on a switch.

Every block is judged in dark whatever the menu says — the stages carry data-cat-theme="dark". The switcher is there to show that these rules are dark's alone: the last block follows the menu.

How the numbers under each option were measured

Separation is counted in device pixels in a 24px band around the panel, against the ground that would be there without the shadow. The page is photographed twice at the same scroll position, once as it stands and once with data-ds-elevation="off" on <html>, which takes every option's elevation away and changes nothing else; the two photographs are subtracted pixel by pixel in CIE L*. The band is the panel's border box grown by 24px minus the border box itself, so the ground inside the chamfer's cut corners is not counted. Read at devicePixelRatio 1 and 2.222, in firefox, 1280px wide. The figures were produced by verify.spec.mjs beside this page, which re-measures on every run and fails if the page prints anything else.

Two thresholds, because one is not enough to tell these five apart. Any difference (ΔL* ≥ 1) is one just-noticeable step on a hard edge: it says the shadow is painted at all. Visible (ΔL* ≥ 3) is the column to read: across a soft gradient a single step is below what an eye picks up, and three is roughly where a shadow starts to lift a panel. One measured surprise, worth knowing before judging: the shipped shadow is not painting nothing. It touches 44 % of the dialog's band by a step or more — but only where dark's pool of pointer light has given it something to take away, it passes three on four tenths of a percent, and its darkest pixel there is 4.34 of 100. Beside the popover, which stands away from the pool, it reaches 2.33 at its peak and not one pixel of the band passes three. That is the fault: a shadow that exists, is the right shape, and cannot be seen.

Every option is parked at the same scroll offset for the measurement, so the same patch of grid and the same amount of pointer light stands behind all five and the figures can be read against each other. Two runs of the spec produce identical counts.

0 · The fault mock

Look at: two identical dialogs. The left one is what ships — look for its shadow on the grid below it and you will not find one; the grid lines run under the dialog's bottom edge unbroken. The right one is the same rule with one value changed, the alarm colour instead of the background token, and it shows you where the shipped shadow is painted: a broad pool under the bottom edge, reaching about 24px out. The shadow is there, it is the right shape, and it is a colour you cannot see — it is the page's own ground colour, so at 45 % over that ground it removes about two of 255 and no more. Both follow the cut corners, which is what the ::before restructure of scope-100 bought: before it, the chamfer's clip-path cut the shadow off altogether.

A darker shadow on a near-black ground has nowhere to go.

3 · The oxide film's colour mock

Look at: not a shadow but the film itself, blurred, behind the panel — the same --kp-iris conic gradient the panel already wears as its border. Follow the halo round one panel: cyan on one side, violet, magenta and lime on the others, the same order as the border. Move the pointer across the page: the halo turns with the border, together, because it is the same gradient; stand still and nothing moves. Judge whether the colour is the theme speaking or a glow the theme does not need — dark's rule is that colour lives in the film and in meaning, and this is the film. Then compare the popover with the other two: it scrolls inside itself (overflow: auto), which clips a pseudo-element away — measured at 0 of 17616 band pixels — so there the film is faked with four shadows in its four wavelengths, and that one does not turn with the pointer. Judge whether the two can live beside each other — and then read the live block below, where the modal dialog turns out to need the same fake, because a dialog in the top layer clips its pseudo-elements too.

Opened for real, over the backdrop mock

Look at: press each button. The dialog opens modally through the package's own js/components.js, with the backdrop dimming the page behind it. Judge that no option looks wrong over a dimmed page: the hard plate must not look like a second dialog, the edge line must not glow, and none of them may fight the backdrop. Escape closes each one and focus returns to the button you pressed. The frozen copies above, with no backdrop, stay the honest test of separation.
Look at, and this one decides option 3: the dialog you open here is not wearing the oxide halo the block above shows. It cannot. The UA stylesheet makes dialog:modal a scroll container (overflow: auto), so a modal dialog clips its own pseudo-elements to its box — measured on this page with the halo forced solid red: full halo on <dialog open>, nothing outside the box after showModal(), only the two cut corners left. A filter is not a descendant and is not clipped, which is why options 1, 2, 4 and 5 arrive here unchanged. What option 3 opens with instead is the popover's fallback: four shadows in the film's four wavelengths, fixed, not turning with the pointer. Compare it with the frozen copy above and judge whether the idea survives the translation.
