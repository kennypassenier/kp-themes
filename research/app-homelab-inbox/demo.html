Homelab dashboard: Inbox and Notification rules

The Inbox is where the homelab tells you what needs you: a question from the host, something that failed, an app with a newer version, a stack that needs setting up, a check only a person can answer. Notification rules decide what reaches your phone and when. This page shows both pages rebuilt on kp-themes 11.0.0, in seven sections: the list and its triage keys, ticking many rows at once, a question from the host with its clock, the manual checks one by one, the delivery rules, the routing per kind and per stack, and quiet hours.

Mock on this page: all the data (stacks such as jellyfin, immich, paperless, vaultwarden, traefik, kyu on CT 109, almanac on CT 112, host pve) is sample data in the shapes the dashboard reads today. Three things need the server to grow before the real pages can do them, and here they run on sample data only: a snooze for one row (today a manual check can only say “later”, 7 days), a quiet-hours field in the notification settings, and a “Send a test push” endpoint. Each section says so where it applies. Judge in the review dialog; nothing moves until you press something.

The Inbox list: worst first, with keys, snooze and version jumps

Shows everything that needs you in one list, grouped by how urgent it is, and lets you work down it with the keyboard: open, snooze or mark seen without leaving the row.

Expected:

  • Seven items in five groups (Urgent, Updates, Setup, Checks, Snoozed). Every row has its kind as a word in the first column (Question, Failure, Update, Setup, Check), a bar on its start edge for how serious it is, and its buttons in the same three columns on every row: Remind me later, the action, Mark seen. A question cannot be marked seen, so its last column is empty, not shifted.
  • The update row shows a chip per app: jellyfin 10.11.11 → 12.1 carries the word major and a !; immich (a minor jump) and paperless (a patch) are quiet. A failure folds open (What went wrong) to its reason, what to do and a command with Copy.
  • Click a row, then j / k (or the arrows) walk the rows, Enter opens, s opens that row’s snooze menu, d marks it seen. Remind me later offers 1 hour, 4 hours, tonight, tomorrow, Monday, or a date and time, each with the exact moment it means; after a choice the row moves to Snoozed and its button says when it comes back, with End snooze. (Needs a server field for one row; sample data here.)
  • Mark seen: the row dims, the pressed button shows the spinner without changing width, then the row folds away and the focus goes to the same button of the next row. The chips keep exact counts (never 9+). Loading lays the busy card over the list; Empty and Failed fill the same height with a plate and a normal-size button.

White rows with a hairline between them, navy for the primary and a thin coloured bar on the start edge (red for failures, amber for warnings, blue for new). The kind words are small plates of one width; the buttons of one role line up from row to row; no text touches the edge of its plate; the major chip is the only loud one in the list.

      Bulk triage: tick rows, then act on all of them

      Lets you clear a long run of notices in one go: tick them with the keyboard or the mouse, then mark them seen, snooze them or fix them together, with Undo for a few seconds.

      Expected:

      • Ten notices from the last day, each with a box. The bar at the top of the list says Select rows to act on them while nothing is ticked. Tick two: it names them and offers Mark seen, Remind me later and Fix all; with 37 notices matching, it offers Select all 37 that match. Clear (or Esc) unticks everything.
      • Keys: j / k move the ring, x ticks the row, Shift+x ticks a run, Ctrl+A ticks all shown, Esc unticks. The ring and the ticked tint read apart.
      • Mark seen hides the rows at once and shows a toast 3 notices marked seen with a clock and Undo (6 seconds; nothing reaches the server before the clock ends). Remind me later does the same with the moment in the toast.
      • Fix all is only on for the notices that can be fixed (the two failed backups and the missed schedule); with others ticked it says 2 of 3 can be fixed. While it runs the bar says Fixing 3…; with One is refused it ends with 2 done, 1 refused: why and a mark on the refused row.

      A white bar with a hairline above the list, the count in navy and the names in plain ink; the ring on the row under the cursor is a thin navy outline, a ticked row a light navy tint. The toast is the opaque white plate with its clock ring.

        A question from the host: answer before it stops waiting

        When an operation on pve stops and waits for you, the card says what each answer would set in motion and how long the host still waits; if nobody answers, the safe answer happens.

        Expected:

        • Two open questions, oldest first, both with a still clock (Clock: Still). Each says what it asks, the time left in words, If nobody answers: Leave it stopped, and two buttons, each with its consequence written beside it (what Allow deletes, what Leave it stopped keeps). With the card focused, 1 and 2 answer.
        • The first question deletes backups, so Allow stays off until its box under the card is ticked (I know this deletes 14 backups (3.2 GB)); pressing the off button takes you to the box. The second restarts a service and has no gate.
        • Answer one: the buttons show the spinner, then the card says the outcome and stays with a Dismiss button. With the host set to Refuses the card says Not sent: why. Nothing changed; answer again. and the buttons come back. Clock: Running counts down for real: under 30 seconds the card turns urgent (stronger colour, bold time, announced once); at zero the safe answer is applied and the card keeps its outcome.
        • Leave all stopped answers every open question with its safe answer. Mock: the package has no batch answer yet (the button only calls each card’s own answer); it is the stand-in for that gap.

        An amber-edged card on white that turns red when urgent; the time left is navy ink with the ring or rule beside it; the two buttons stand in one column with their consequences in muted ink beside them, and nothing sits against the card’s edge. The gate under the first card is the amber warning plate with a plain checkbox.

        Manual checks, one question per screen

        Walks you through the checks only a person can answer, one at a time with big buttons, and offers the same answer as last time in one press.

        Expected:

        • Three checks. Each screen is one question, the stack it is about, and big answer buttons It works and It fails, then Same as last time: It works and Not now (asks again in 7 days). Choosing an answer moves on after a moment; Back keeps every answer given.
        • After the last one the page says what would happen: an answer It fails becomes a failure in the Inbox list (section 1), It works closes the check, Not now asks again on a date.
        • Loading lays the busy card over the question; Failed says the checks could not be read with a normal-size button; all keep the height of a question.

        A white plate with a navy progress bar, the question in large type, answer buttons at least 56 px high in one column, and the Back and Next buttons in the same place on every screen whatever the question’s length.

        Delivery: snooze everything, the daily digest, push off, test push

        Controls whether and when pushes reach your phone: hold everything for a while, set the hour of the daily digest, switch push off with a deliberate tick, and send yourself a test.

        Expected:

        • The word at the top says the state in one place: Push on · not snoozed, Snoozed until 20/10/2026 07:00 or Push off; the plate keeps one width for all three. Hold every push offers 1 hour, 4 hours, tonight 18:00, tomorrow 07:00, Monday 07:00 and a date and time, never more than 7 days (what the server accepts), each with the exact moment; after a choice the picker says Snoozed until … with End snooze, no seconds ticking.
        • Daily digest at is the package’s time field (24 hours, type 0930 or pick from the list). It saves as you change it: no Save button; a toast says Digest set to 09:30 with Undo for 6 seconds that puts the old time back. With the relay on Refuses the toast says it was not saved and the old time returns.
        • Turn push off… opens an amber box with a tick (I understand nothing reaches my phone until I turn it on again); Turn push off stays dimmed, saying why, until it is ticked. Send a test push shows the spinner, then Sent for a moment; refused, it shows Refused and the sentence with what to do right under it. (Needs a test endpoint on the server; sample data here.)

        White card, navy for the primary button and the state word, amber for the push-off warning. The time field is a plain underline-free box with a list; the snooze chips are small pills of one height with the moment under each label. The buttons of this card stand in one column and never change width when they show their result.

        Routing: which kinds push, which stacks are muted

        Decides, for every kind of notice and every stack, whether it pushes to your phone or waits for the digest, and shows what the last five pushes would have done under these rules.

        Expected:

        • By kind: nine chips, one press = always push (a tick), a second press = digest only (a cross, struck through), a third = by default. The line under the chips says in words what is set. (A server field per kind and severity does not exist yet; sample data here.)
        • By stack: fourteen stacks with their unread counts, each with a box and an Only button (Only jellyfin turns the others off), and an Every stack row that shows a mixed state while some are off. Every change is one request with the whole muted list; the line below says it. Counts are exact.
        • The last five pushes under these rules: each notice says whether it went to the phone, was held for the digest, or came from a muted stack; changing a chip or a box above changes this list at once. Loading, Empty and Failed fill the same height.

        Chips as small pills with a mark that is always there (tick, cross, or none) so nothing moves when one changes; facet rows with a coloured square and a pattern per stack, the count at the row’s end; the preview as quiet rows with a bar for severity.

          Quiet hours: hold pushes at night, see what it would have held

          Lets you say once that pushes wait between two times on chosen days, and shows on the last 14 days of pushes which hours that would have kept quiet before you save.

          Expected:

          • One sentence you fill in: Hold pushes from [22:00] until [07:00] on these days [Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun]. The times are the package’s 24-hour field (an end earlier than the start means past midnight, and the sentence says so: until 07:00 the next morning); the days are seven chips with the quick picks Weekdays, Weekend and Every day; none picked is refused with a line. A tick lets Question and failure notices through.
          • The grid under it is the last 14 days of pushes, one row per stack (the six busiest) and one column per hour, darker for more; the row at the foot totals each hour. Hours that fall inside the quiet window are drawn grey: those pushes would have been held. The line under the grid counts them exactly: 41 of 153 pushes would have been held (26.8 %).
          • The buttons Nights, Working hours and Weekend fill the sentence with a sensible window so you can see the grid change. Save is not needed: it saves as you change it, with Undo. (Needs a quiet-hours field on the server; sample data here.)

          The sentence at reading size with the fields as underlined boxes in navy; the grid in one navy hue getting darker, grey for the held hours, the hour axis naming every third hour, the totals row filled from its foot.
