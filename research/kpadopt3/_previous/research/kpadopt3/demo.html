Round 3 of the dashboard adoption: six options

The admin dashboard still drew six things itself because the package lacked them. Each is now an opt-in option of a component you already approved: without the new attribute or field, every one of them looks and behaves exactly as approved. Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample data, the frames, the readouts and the buttons that drive a demo are this page’s. Every section says what you should see under Expected. Formal only, judged in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top).

1 · A separator in the folding bar

<li class="kp-nav__sep" aria-hidden="true"> between two groups of links. The fold (data-kp-nav-fold) knows it: a separator stays only between two links that are shown, and in the More menu it becomes the menu’s own rule.

Expected:

  • The wide bar: Backups and System (the rarely needed two) stand after formal’s docket cut: a strong hairline as tall as a link with a short navy tick at its head, in the same gap as between two links. No word touches it.
  • The 33rem bar has folded from Logs on: More holds Logs, then a hairline across the menu, then Backups and System. No separator is left standing before More. The menu is open under the bar (it hangs from More, joined to it, on its opaque plate).
  • Drag the slider: links leave the bar from the end. While a link after the separator is still in the bar, the separator stays; once every link after it has folded, the separator folds with them and turns into the rule in the menu. The bar keeps its height at every width and nothing jumps.
  • The 360 px bar collapses into its toggle as before; opened, the separator is a rule across the list (the docket cut lying down: hairline, navy tick at its start) between Logs and Backups.
  • Tab through a bar: the separator is never a stop, and a screen reader does not hear it.

Compare the wide bar’s separator with the one lying down in the opened 360 px list, and the rule in the open menu.

2 · A choice filter that reads a word, not the cell

td data-kp-filter-value="running": the cell shows what it likes (a dot, the state, how long), the data table’s choice filter lists and matches it by the word, as data-kp-sort-value does for sorting. Search still reads what the cell shows.

Expected:

  • The Status column shows a dot, the state and how long (“Running · up 3 days”). Open Filters: Status lists exactly three choices, failed, running, stopped, not one per cell text.
  • Tick failed: the two failed hosts stay, the status line says “Showing 1–2 of 2 (filtered from 7)” and a pill says Status: failed. Tick stopped as well: three rows, two pills.
  • The search box still reads what is shown: type “up 3” and only the host that has been up 3 days stays.
  • The Kind column has no value of its own: its choices are its texts, as before.

3 · Run timeline: a day on the axis, and a colour legend

data-kp-gantt-days makes every day named on the axis a button that selects that whole day; the page hears it and jumps. A run’s series (1 to 5) inks a finished run in a chart colour, and setGanttLegend() names the series after the states.

Expected:

  • The last 7 days. Each day on the axis (“10/10”) is a button: pointing rules the navy rule under it. Press one: the whole day, 00:00 to 00:00, is selected as a drag would select it (the band across the plot, the toolbar row turned into the chip with its times, Zoom and Clear), the day on the axis is closed with the double rule, and the history under the timeline scrolls to that day inside its own box (its heading ruled), not the page.
  • Press the same day again: the selection lifts. Clear lifts it too. Tab reaches the days as one stop; ← → Home End move between them; Enter or Space presses.
  • The nightly round (series 1) and the updates (series 2) are plates of their own chart colour with their edge in the full colour and formal’s heavy rule on the start edge; a run with no series is the green of “succeeded”; failed, running and queued look as before. The legend names Nightly round and Updates after the four states.
  • The 360 px timeline names every other day (the axis keeps a label’s room); those days are its buttons. A day whose label would run past the end of the axis stands against its end; where that would touch the label before it, the last, partial day is not offered, so no two labels ever touch.

Press a day on the axis, then look at the band, the chip, the pressed day and the history box together.
