Round 3 of the dashboard adoption, page round 2: four options in every other theme

Review round 2. You approved the six opt-in options in formal (round 1). Four of them are seen, so this round asks the fifteen other themes for those four, each drawn in that theme’s own language: the separator in the folding bar, the run timeline’s day buttons and series runs, the week agenda’s next 8 on a phone and the facet swatch’s identity pattern. The choice filter’s word and the heat grid’s End are behaviour only, the same in every theme, and formal is approved: they are not on the page. Every section says what you should see under Expected, and the review dialog’s “Look at” line names what is this theme’s own; Previous version shows the package’s plain drawing from before this round. Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample data, the frames and the buttons that drive a demo are this page’s.

1 · A separator in the folding bar

<li class="kp-nav__sep" aria-hidden="true"> between two groups of links. The fold (data-kp-nav-fold) knows it: a separator stays only between two links that are shown, and in the More menu it becomes the menu’s own rule.

Expected:

  • The wide bar: Backups and System (the rarely needed two) stand after the theme’s own separator (the “Look at” line names it), in the same gap as between two links. No word touches it. In the themes whose wide letters fold the bar early, drag the slider below to see it standing.
  • The 33rem bar has folded from Logs on: More holds Logs, then a hairline across the menu, then Backups and System. No separator is left standing before More. The menu is open under the bar (it hangs from More, joined to it, on its opaque plate).
  • Drag the slider: links leave the bar from the end. While a link after the separator is still in the bar, the separator stays; once every link after it has folded, the separator folds with them and turns into the rule in the menu. The bar keeps its height at every width and nothing jumps.
  • The 360 px bar collapses into its toggle as before; opened, the separator lies across the list between Logs and Backups, the same mark lying down, inside the list’s own ground.
  • Tab through a bar: the separator is never a stop, and a screen reader does not hear it.

The separator is light’s divider stood upright: a hairline with a small open bead at its middle, in the same gap as between two links; in the opened 360 px list it lies across the list with the bead at its centre. The More menu keeps light’s own rule.

The separator is dark’s scale stood upright: a hairline that fades at both ends with one short lit cyan tick at its middle; lying across the opened 360 px list, the tick stands on the middle of a line that fades at both ends.

The separator is pastel’s divider stood upright: a column of small plum beads; in the opened 360 px list the beads run across the list as a dotted line inside the card.

The separator is a twig: a green hairline with one small leaf at its head; in the opened 360 px list the twig lies across the list with the leaf sitting on its start.

2 · Run timeline: a day on the axis, and a colour legend

data-kp-gantt-days makes every day named on the axis a button that selects that whole day; the page hears it and jumps. A run’s series (1 to 5) inks a finished run in a chart colour, and setGanttLegend() names the series after the states.

Expected:

  • The last 7 days. Each day on the axis (“10/10”) is a button: pointing marks it the theme’s way. Press one: the whole day, 00:00 to 00:00, is selected as a drag would select it (the band across the plot, the toolbar row turned into the chip with its times, Zoom and Clear), the pressed day is drawn the theme’s way, and the history under the timeline scrolls to that day inside its own box, not the page. The date never touches the edge of its own mark.
  • Press the same day again: the selection lifts. Clear lifts it too. Tab reaches the days as one stop; ← → Home End move between them; Enter or Space presses.
  • The nightly round (series 1) and the updates (series 2) are runs of their own chart colour with their edge in the full colour, in the theme’s own run shape; a run with no series is the theme’s “succeeded”; failed, running and queued look as before. The legend names Nightly round and Updates after the four states.
  • The 360 px timeline names every other day (the axis keeps a label’s room); those days are its buttons. A day whose label would run past the end of the axis stands against its end; where that would touch the label before it, the last, partial day is not offered, so no two labels ever touch.

Point at a day on the axis: a muted pill; press one: the indigo-tint pill with its ring, the band and the chip as before. The nightly round and the updates are pills in their chart colour with the small card shadow.

Point at a day: its foot lights in cyan; press one: it becomes the lit plate, the foot with its glow rising into it. A series run carries a 2 px leading edge in its ink, as a week pill does.

Point at a day: cyberpunk’s cyan target corners close round it and the date splits into cyan and red ghosts (the RGB split); press one: the yellow lock-on, a tinted ground with its corner notched and a hard yellow foot. A series run has the hard 3 px bar on its leading edge.

Point at a day: the laser draws its pink line under it; press one: it is lit in the pink tube with its glow, the date in the core’s white. A series run glows in its own neon.

Point at a day: it lifts on its plum peel; press one: it is stuck down, the pink chip in a plum ring. A series run stands on a peel of its own colour.

Point at a day: the cursor ring; press one: reverse video, the phosphor block with the date in the ground’s ink. Nothing eases. The series runs are the package’s blocks in their chart colours.

Point at a day: the moss wash in the leaf shape; press one: the green leaf with the trail’s foot under it. A series run grows from its foot in its own colour.

Point at a day: the pen’s cyan witness lines to either side; press one: an amber hatched section between amber witness lines, the dimension line under it. A series run is a hatched section in its ink.

Point at a day: a flare rises from its foot; press one: it wears the halo, the primary’s light rising from the horizon under it. A series run is lit from its foot in its own light.

Point at a day: it is kicked up-left off its hard shadow inside an ink frame; press one: it is driven in, the yellow block in its ink frame. The series runs are the package’s plates in their chart colours.

Point at a day: a gold rule under it and the date in the high gold; press one: it stands on the lacquer over the inlay’s double gold rule. A series run is a double rule in its ink, as a week pill is.

Point at a day: the red slash under it; press one: the red plate is thrown in under it, cut on the slant, the date in black ink. A series run carries a hard leading edge in its ink.

Point at a day: the red baseline; press one: it is inverted, the black block with the date in white. A series run has a hard leading rule in its colour.

Point at a day: its rim lights in the LED orange; press one: a key sunk in its well with its lamp lit under the date. The series runs are the package’s tapes in their chart colours.

Point at a day: its tool edge turns bright metal at once; press one: a blue-oxide plate with the chamfer pair and the oxide edge under it. A series run carries its tool edge along the top in its own oxide.
