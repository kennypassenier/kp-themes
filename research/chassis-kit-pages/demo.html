chassis-rs kit pages on kp-themes 11.0.0: the two client dialogs, login and passkeys (round 2)

Round 2 of the kit pages: only what is still open. The issue and the manage dialog of the clients page are redrawn as grid compositions (round 1 was rejected for plain left/right alignment); the login page and the two passkeys pages were not judged in round 1 and come back unchanged. Everything else was approved and has left the page. In formal. 5 sections, 5 pairs. The pages are rendered from data the running example service answered (clients, requests, status, health); rows it did not have are more rows of the same shape and say so on the page.

Log in: a passkey first, the token as the way that always works

The page says what the service is and that it is healthy before anyone is in; the passkey is the primary way, the pasted token the fallback, and “keep me signed in” is a switch.

Expected:

  • No navigation links before login, only the brand and the theme menu. The service’s health, version and age are on the page (the open /healthz already says them).
  • Ready: “Log in with a passkey” is the primary button, the token field below it with Show, a switch for 30 days and the secondary Log in. Wrong token says what is wrong, why and what to do, in the alert plate. Busy keeps the button’s width.
  • Passkeys not offered: the card is only the token form and its Log in becomes primary. Phone: one column, nothing scrolls sideways.

Two columns at the wide width (intro left, card right), one column on the phone; the passkey button is the only primary button on the card; the wrong-token alert is a red plate with three parts and the field is marked invalid.

Issue a client: the form beside the client it will make, then the token and first call beside the client made

Round 2: a composition on the 12-column grid instead of one column. The form (columns 1 to 7) stands against a live card of the client it will make (8 to 12); after Issue, the token and the first command are one block in the same place and the card shows what was made.

Expected:

  • Form: Name and Topic in the left card; the right card fills in as you type (mark, name, topic, state, what is made and where it appears). Topic opens Used before on top; Name taken shows the error under the field in words, and the card says not issued.
  • Issued: the token and the first command share one block (one label, one width), the card on the right becomes the receipt. Heads, contents and foot notes of both cards sit on the same three rows, and the dialog keeps its size in all three steps.
  • Phone (the Width control): the client card becomes a strip of mark, name, topic and state above the form; the buttons share the width equally.

The two cards share their top, their bottom and their three inner rows; the dialog is exactly as tall in Form, Name taken and Issued; at Phone the strip comes first and nothing scrolls sideways.

Manage a client: the controls in a rail, the requests as the wide area

Round 2: one grid of 12 columns. The client and its three figures across the top (5 | 7); the token and the command in a fixed rail (1 to 5); the test and the request inspector as the wide area (6 to 12); the danger zone as a band with its words under the rail and its three actions, each with its consequence, under the inspector.

Expected:

  • Rail: Token (Reveal shows it for 10 s, Copy never shows it) and Use it (the block never holds the token; the button copies it with the token). Wide area: Test it (the body is a JSON box that says what is wrong and where; the button sits beside it) above the Requests: method, path and status in words, authorization masked, Copy as curl.
  • Danger zone: Re-issue, Revoke and Delete are three equal cells, each with what it does; Delete asks for the exact name home-assistant. Revoked keeps every area: the rail says the token no longer works, Send test is off with its reason, Re-issue and Revoke are off with theirs.
  • Press the four request states: the inspector keeps its area. Phone: the three figures become one tall tile beside two, the test sits under the rail, the three actions stay three equal buttons.

The rail, the test and the inspector share their top and bottom edges; the danger words start under the rail and the three actions under the inspector, on the same columns as the areas above; the dialog is exactly as tall in every Client and Requests state.

Ways to sign in: this device marked, rename in place, remove behind a question

One list of the passkeys that open the dashboard: a “This device” badge on the one in use, a passkey unused for 90 days flagged, Rename and Remove in two fixed columns. The login token always works as the fallback. The rows are sample values: a passkey needs HTTPS and a real authenticator.

Expected:

  • Several: “Work laptop, Firefox” has the This device badge and comes first; Rename turns the name into a field in place (Enter saves, Esc cancels); “Old tablet” is flagged as unused for 90 days; Remove asks first and says what happens.
  • One passkey is removable too: the login token still works, and the page says so under the list. Not HTTPS shows one alert with what, why and the fix, and the primary button is off with its reason on hover. Empty is a plate with the register button.

Rename and Remove line up in two columns from row to row; the badge and the flagged state use words as well as colour; the confirmation names the passkey.

Register on another device: a code to scan

A passkey belongs to the device that makes it. The page draws a code of its own address, so a phone can open it, log in once and register itself.

Expected:

  • The code stands on a light plate in every theme (it is read by a camera), with the address under it and a Copy button; Loading shows the code being made, Expired withdraws it with a reason.
  • The three steps on the right say what happens on the phone, so nobody wonders what to do after scanning.

The code and the steps are two cards of one height; the code never shrinks below a size a camera can read; the address wraps instead of scrolling.
