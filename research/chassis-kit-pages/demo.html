chassis-rs kit pages on kp-themes 11.0.0: every kit page

The shell and the error page, the status page and the sections a service adds, the clients page, the login page and the passkeys page: everything the kit serves to every service. In formal. 12 sections, 12 pairs. The pages are rendered from data the running example service answered (clients, requests, status, health); rows it did not have are more rows of the same shape and say so on the page.

The navigation bar, the search palette and the shortcuts

Every page of the kit stands in this shell. The bar folds links that do not fit, Search opens a palette (Ctrl K) that reaches every page and the things you do most, and one sheet lists the keys.

Expected:

  • The bar: brand, the four pages, Search with its Ctrl K hint, the theme menu and Log out, on one row. Press Narrow: the links that do not fit fold behind More, nothing wraps to a second row.
  • The palette (open, left) groups places and actions, each with a description and its keys; Typed fills it with “rev ho”: words in any order find “Manage home-assistant”’s relatives and mark the matched words. The sheet (right) lists the chords the page registers (g s, g c).
  • The banner under the bar says when the connection is gone or the service refuses, with the exact number of changes waiting, and takes no room while all is well.

The bar is one row with the Ctrl K hint visible; the palette and the sheet stand side by side at the same height, every row has its description and key in its own column; the banner appears under the bar and pushes the page down.

The phone: a tab bar with exact counts

On a phone the pages are four tabs at the thumb; the rest and the session live behind More. A count on a tab is the exact number.

Expected:

  • Top: only the brand, Search and the theme menu. Bottom: Messages, Status, Clients and More; the current page is marked by more than colour.
  • Count on Clients: 3 shows 3, 1534 shows 1534 whole, never “99+”. More open shows the drawer with Passkeys, theme and Log out.

The tab bar sits at the foot of the phone frame with its four tabs equal in width; the count badge shows every digit without growing the bar; the More drawer opens from the bar and holds three entries with a hint each.

Log in: a passkey first, the token as the way that always works

The page says what the service is and that it is healthy before anyone is in; the passkey is the primary way, the pasted token the fallback, and “keep me signed in” is a switch.

Expected:

  • No navigation links before login, only the brand and the theme menu. The service’s health, version and age are on the page (the open /healthz already says them).
  • Ready: “Log in with a passkey” is the primary button, the token field below it with Show, a switch for 30 days and the secondary Log in. Wrong token says what is wrong, why and what to do, in the alert plate. Busy keeps the button’s width.
  • Passkeys not offered: the card is only the token form and its Log in becomes primary. Phone: one column, nothing scrolls sideways.

Two columns at the wide width (intro left, card right), one column on the phone; the passkey button is the only primary button on the card; the wrong-token alert is a red plate with three parts and the field is marked invalid.

The status page as an operations page

One page header with the freshness line, four key figures, what needs a person (one alert per problem, with its fix), then the cards: service, health per part, updates and the last 48 hours.

Expected:

  • Healthy: header, “updated 12 s ago” with the refresh control (every 30 s, remembered), four figures that include Up for and the client count, no attention band (it takes no room), three cards and the availability strip.
  • Degraded: the Health figure turns red, two problems appear (what, why, what now, with a Copy command), and the failing part is a red row. Problems as (choice) is One alert each by default; the other two modes are real options.
  • Retry failed deliveries (a service’s own section action) is a button with a spinner, then “Retried”, and a toast says Retried 12 entries: the page does not reload and keeps its place. Update found shows the version chip 0.1.0 → 0.2.0 with its size in words and a link to the release; Failed is one plate with Try again; Loading keeps the final geometry.

The figures strip is one row of equal tiles; the three cards share one height; the availability strip spans the whole width with its summary above; Degraded is readable without colour (words and shapes).

A service’s own sections: a table, key figures and a card that reads its own data

What a service adds to the status page is no longer only rows of text: a section can be a sortable table, key figures with checks, or a card that fetches its own JSON and shows loading, empty and failed by itself.

Expected:

  • Messages is the capture’s own section as a data table (sort, search, remembered); Delivery is key figures and two check rows; Store, read live is a card that reads /healthz and shows its state.
  • Press Loading, Empty, Failed: each card keeps its place and size, shows the theme’s busy card, the empty plate or the failed plate with Try again, and the other cards stay as they are.

In every state the three cards keep their footprint; loading is the busy card centred over the card, empty and failed fill the card with a heading-size plate and a normal Try again.

Clients: the list, with what needs a look first

The four figures are filters (a click toggles, “Show all” resets), idle clients are called out with their fix, and the table sorts, searches and remembers its sort. The first 3 rows are captured from the running example service; the other 8 have the same shape and sample values.

Expected:

  • Ready: four figures (In use, Idle, Never used, Revoked) with exact counts; one alert says how many clients have not called in 30 days and names them, with Show them. Each row: identity mark, name (hover for its details), state, last used as dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm and how long ago, requests, topic, messages, Manage in its own column.
  • Idle only is the Idle figure pressed: the table shows just those rows and the figure stays pressed until you press it again. Empty is a plate with Issue a client; Loading and Failed fill the table’s footprint.
  • The choice List as is Table by default; Cards is a real option and the table always becomes cards on a phone.

The Manage buttons stand in one column, the dates in one column with the ago words under or beside them; the pressed figure has a primary border; on Phone every row is a card with its name and state first.

Issue a client: a form that ends with the token in your hand

The name is checked as you leave the field, the topic offers the ones already used, and the new token appears in the same dialog with a ready command, instead of a reload and a hunt through the table.

Expected:

  • Form: Name and Topic with a help line each; Topic opens a list with Used before on top and lets you type a new one. Name taken shows the error under the field in words and keeps what you typed.
  • Issued: the token is in a secret field that stays shown until you hide it, with Copy; a first command is under it, and Done closes. Issue token is one button with a spinner and then “Issued”, never changing width.

The dialog is one column with its sections in a fixed order; the Topic list opens at its field with the opaque menu plate; the error is a line under the field in the destructive colour with words.

Manage a client: token, command, test and what it sent

Everything about one client in one dialog, in the order you use it: reveal or copy the token, copy a command, send a test with a body of your choosing, read what it sent, and the dangerous things last behind a typed name.

Expected:

  • Token: the field is masked; Reveal shows it for 10 s with a countdown and Copy never shows it. Use it: the command block never holds the token (safe to paste in a chat); the button copies the same command with the token in it.
  • Test it: the body is a JSON box that says what is wrong and where; Send test shows a spinner, then “Sent”. Requests are the inspector: method, path and status in words, headers with authorization masked, the body, and Copy as curl. Press the four request states.
  • Danger zone: Delete asks for the exact name home-assistant before Confirm turns on; Revoke and Re-issue ask once and say what happens. Revoked shows only the history and Delete.

The dialog is wide with one heading per section and a note under each; the secret line keeps its height while it counts down; the inspector’s states keep one height; the danger section is last and marked.

The first client: the page walks you through it and notices the first call

A new service has no clients, which is the moment people need most help. Three steps, the last one finishes by itself when the first request arrives.

Expected:

  • Press 1 to 4: each step shows its state (done, running with the theme’s spinner, waiting) with a note that says what happens, and the right card changes from “No clients yet” to “Connected”.
  • Nothing moves between steps: notes are all present from the start, so a note arriving later shifts nothing.

The step list has one fixed row height per step with its mark in one column; the running step has a spinner; step 4 ends with a primary button to the list.

Ways to sign in: this device marked, rename in place, remove behind a question

One list of the passkeys that open the dashboard: a “This device” badge on the one in use, a passkey unused for 90 days flagged, Rename and Remove in two fixed columns. The login token always works as the fallback. The rows are sample values: a passkey needs HTTPS and a real authenticator.

Expected:

  • Several: “Work laptop, Firefox” has the This device badge and comes first; Rename turns the name into a field in place (Enter saves, Esc cancels); “Old tablet” is flagged as unused for 90 days; Remove asks first and says what happens.
  • One passkey is removable too: the login token still works, and the page says so under the list. Not HTTPS shows one alert with what, why and the fix, and the primary button is off with its reason on hover. Empty is a plate with the register button.

Rename and Remove line up in two columns from row to row; the badge and the flagged state use words as well as colour; the confirmation names the passkey.

Register on another device: a code to scan

A passkey belongs to the device that makes it. The page draws a code of its own address, so a phone can open it, log in once and register itself.

Expected:

  • The code stands on a light plate in every theme (it is read by a camera), with the address under it and a Copy button; Loading shows the code being made, Expired withdraws it with a reason.
  • The three steps on the right say what happens on the phone, so nobody wonders what to do after scanning.

The code and the steps are two cards of one height; the code never shrinks below a size a camera can read; the address wraps instead of scrolling.

An error page: one plate, the way out and a request to quote

The kit’s error and its remedy, as a page in the shell with the navigation around it: what is wrong in plain words, why, what to do, two ways out, and the request number to copy into a report.

Expected:

  • Press 403, 404, 500, 503: each has its own drawing in the theme’s hand, a heading in plain words (no ids, no raw errors), the reason and the remedy under it.
  • The request number sits in a copyable value with a Copy button; Go back and Open the status page are normal-size buttons, and Back to the dashboard is the one primary button.

The plate is centred in the card at heading size; the two buttons are normal size; the facts below it use one label column.
