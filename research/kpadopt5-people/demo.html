The fifth adoption round, people: four components

Four pieces every app of Kenny’s draws for itself, built in the package first and shown in formal only: an avatar with a photo, the list of ways to sign in, a conversation in bubbles, and markers and a median line on a chart. After formal is approved the other themes follow.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does. The people in the examples are made up, and so are their photos (small drawings, not real faces); every message, time and number is sample content.

Avatar

Avatar with a photo

The identity mark (.kp-idmark, the plate made from a name) with a photo on it. A person without a photo looks exactly like today’s marks, so one component serves both; a photo that does not load leaves the letters.

Formal’s plate is a quiet square with soft corners; round ones are circles. Look at the letters under the photo while it arrives, at the photo fading in, at the status dot (its shape differs as well as its colour), and at the “+4” that opens the names left out.

Expected:

  • The table: five rows of looks, four sizes each (sm 24 px, md 32, lg 44, xl 56). Row one has a photo, row two has none and shows the letters, row three has a photo address that does not exist and so shows the letters with no error mark, row four is a mark still waiting for its name (the pulsing plate), row five has no name (dashed “?”). Every size is a fixed square before the photo is there: nothing moves when it arrives.
  • Replay the photos: the photos are taken away and brought back; the letters show first, then the photo fades in.
  • Status: online is a full green dot, away a ring, busy a dot with a bar; the word is in the mark’s label (“Ada Lovelace, online”, shown beside it), never colour alone.
  • A group: marks overlap with a paper edge; the rest is an exact number (“+4”; the second group shows “+116”, never “9+”) and is a button. Press it: a list of the hidden names opens under the group (it is shown open in the first card, and closes with Escape or a click elsewhere).
  • Beside a name: the photo mark sits before the name with a gap; a long name wraps and never runs under it.
  • The choice of frame (tick it in the dialog): the same marks with the plate’s edge, a ring, or the photo set in a mount. All three are shown by the buttons below as well.

Ways to sign in

List of ways to sign in

One row per way in (a passkey, a magic link, a password, a token) with when it was added and last used, Rename and Remove. Removing the only way in is refused, and the row says why and what to do first.

Rows are plain cards on one grid: the four icons, the names, the badge column and the two buttons begin in the same column from row to row. Look at the “This device” badge, the amber “Unused for 120 days” badge, the held-off Remove with its reason, and at the empty and failed states that take the list’s whole footprint.

Expected:

  • Ready: five rows, the one in use on top with a “This device” badge, then by last use. Each row says the kind, when it was added (dd/mm/yyyy) and when it was last used in words (“Last used 2 days ago”; the exact moment is in the tooltip). The old tablet carries “Unused for 120 days”; the recovery token says “Never used”. Rename and Remove stand in the same two columns on every row.
  • Rename: the name becomes a field with Save and Cancel; Enter saves, Escape cancels, and the focus returns to Rename. An empty name is refused with a line under it.
  • Remove asks first. The way in use needs its exact name typed; the others a plain Yes. The list loses the row at once when it is done. Removing the recovery token is refused by the (pretend) server a second after you confirm: the row stays and says “Not removed: …” in red.
  • Only one way: Remove is held off on that row and the reason stands under its name. With “Ask a typed name” it can be removed behind a typed name and a warning that you will be locked out; with “Allow” it is as any other.
  • Loading shows rows of the final geometry under the data table’s busy card. Empty and Failed fill the same height, the sentence at heading size in the middle with a normal-size Register or Retry. Not secure puts an amber warning above the list saying what to do.
  • 390 px: each row is a card and its two buttons are the full width under it.

Conversation

Conversation in bubbles

Messages as bubbles: theirs on the start side, yours on the end side, status changes as centred lines, a day divider between days, attachments as tags. Only the newest message is shown whole; older long ones fold to three lines with “Show all N lines” (configurable).

Bubbles are soft plates with a small corner where the sender’s side begins; the sender’s mark stands beside the first bubble of a run. Look at the folded older mails fading out, the day dividers, the failed message with its Retry, and at the box that keeps its height in loading, empty and failed.

Expected:

  • Ready: seven messages over three days, oldest first, scrolled to the newest. Two messages from the recruiter within five minutes share one header and one mark. A status change is a centred line. Times are dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm in each bubble’s label and HH:mm in its header.
  • Folding (the default is the first button): only the newest message is whole; the older long mail stops after three lines with “Show all 30 lines” and shows the rest when pressed. “After 12 lines” folds every message longer than 12 lines; “Everything whole” folds nothing.
  • Order newest first puts the newest at the top; Marks choose the sender’s mark on their side only, on both, or none; Days hides the dividers.
  • Send a reply adds a bubble that is faded and says “Sending…”, then becomes a normal one. Send one that fails ends as “Not sent: …” with a Retry that sends it. Scrolled up, a “Jump to newest” button appears over the foot of the box.
  • Long words and the long address wrap anywhere. Each bubble has a Copy text button, and a button for the page’s own action (Rename).
  • Loading shows one waiting bubble on each side under the busy card; Empty and Failed fill the same box, the sentence at heading size in the middle with a normal-size button.
  • 390 px: bubbles take 85 % of the width.

Chart markers

Chart markers and median line

Events on a chart as dots above the plot and, for a window of time (a maintenance window), as a band. A key names the kinds and switches them. A dashed median line, the points of long runs ringed, and “too few days to say anything” as a state of its own.

Formal’s chart colours: the first source in navy, info events in blue, updates amber, failures red. Look at the key above the plot, at the band against the readings, at the median’s label at the end of its line, and at the faint points under the sentence of the third chart.

Expected:

  • First chart (three days of pressure): a key above the plot with a switch per kind (operation, update, failure, maintenance window) and its exact count; press one and its markers leave the plot and the switch is struck dashed; press again and they return. The maintenance window is a band; point at it for its start and end in words. Two events earlier than the window show as “◂ 2” at the left edge of the markers, one later as “1 ▸” at the right.
  • Median: a dashed line in the source’s colour with “median 3.4 bar” at its end; the key says it too. The screen reader’s label of the plot carries the value.
  • Second chart (28 days of a mood score): the median and, with Runs on, a ring on every point of a run of six or more on one side of the median (the key explains the ring). With Runs off the rings leave.
  • Third chart (3 readings, 7 needed): the three points faint, and in the middle of the plot’s full height “3 of 7 days needed before a trend means something” at heading size. Press “Enough readings” and the chart draws itself.
  • Loading and Failed keep the chart’s height (the existing states). 390 px: the median’s words leave the plot and are in the tooltip and the key.
