Formal is approved. Telling the reader what is going on: a problem with its reason and its remedy, the offline banner, a transfer with its time left, the notification centre, the line under a heading, and the today card. This part asks the eleven other themes for 7 sections (77 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

A problem says what, why and what now

.kp-alert with the parts __what, __why, __remedy and __actions, and setProblems() in js/problems.js: the same shape as a chassis::Error (the error and its remedy). The layout is an option of the component: data-kp-problems-mode (each is the default, list, fold) and, with fold, data-kp-problems-fold-from (5 by default).

Expected: every problem is its own alert by default; the list and the fold are options you switch below, also the number five.

  • On load: six problems as six alerts, the worst first (one critical, two warnings, three notes). Press “List”: the same six become rows of one alert; press “Folded”: one alert with the first four open and “Show the other 2”.
  • Change “Fold from” (2 to 9): with Folded on, the alert folds once that many problems are there; with fewer you get plain alerts. Press a fix button: that problem leaves and the rest close up; “Remove all” leaves no space; “Bring them back” returns all six. The line under the controls shows the two attributes as they are right now.
  • Below: one problem on its own, a problem without a fix, a closable note, the run-on layout and the phone width.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Expected: the banner stands at the foot of its window by default; “under the navigation bar” is the option data-kp-placement="top". Both are shown here.

A transfer with its speed and the time left

.kp-progress-group--transfer with js/transfer.js: a bar with the file name, how much is done, the speed and the time left. The app reports bytes and moments; the package works out the rest.

Expected: three transfers, and the first two behave differently on purpose. The difference is whether the app knows how big the file is.

  • First bar (known size, 600 MB): the app knows the total, so it can say how much is done as a share. The bar fills from 0 to 100 %, and the row reads “240 MB of 600 MB · 40 %”, the speed and “about 30 s left”.
  • Second bar (size unknown): the app is still sending but has no total, so there is nothing to divide by: no percentage exists and the bar cannot fill. Instead the bar is a full band of stripes that drifts calmly, which means “busy, size unknown”; it is not stuck. The words say the same: “size unknown” in the last column, and the bytes that arrived so far count up live (“240 MB so far”) next to the speed. That counting is its progress.
  • Press Second one learns its size while it runs: the app now reports a total (1.6 GB). The stripes settle into a normal bar that shrinks smoothly to the real share and then fills like the first, without a restart or a jump.
  • Third bar: stalls after a few seconds (“stalled for 5 s”) and then fails with Retry.
  • Press Play once (about 12 s, the clock runs at five times speed). Reset clears all three.

Schedules

Each schedule starts a job at fixed times. Pause one to stop it without deleting it, and delete it when the job is gone for good.

Narrow, plain and full

Narrow: a lede that stops at 48 characters, for a card that is itself narrow, so the line never runs long.

Plain: the ordinary ink, for a lede that must not be missed.

Full: this lede runs the whole width of its box, which is right for a page-wide note and wrong for a paragraph a person has to read.

A very long explanation

A backup copies the files of a stack to the backup disk every night, keeps the last fourteen copies and removes the older ones. A restore puts one of the copies back next to the original; it never overwrites the running stack. If the disk is full the backup stops and says so on this page, and nothing already on the disk is deleted to make room. A copy that cannot be read back is marked and the next backup starts again from the files.

With a link

Restores never overwrite the running stack.

Older copies are removed after each backup; keep at least one.
Pick at least one weekday, or the schedule never runs.

Today card: open, done, paused, not known

data-kp-status-vocab="calm" on a status list (js/statusrow.js) inside a card: one row per thing in the day, each only Open, Done or Paused since a date with one-press Resume. No red, no counters, no “you still have”.

Expected:

  • Six rows, each 56 px high with a round mark that has its own shape: Morning pills and Walk after dinner open (a grey ring with a dot), Evening check-in and Water the plants done (a soft blue disc with a check, not green), Beweging paused (a dashed ring with two bars, a dashed edge, Paused since 03/10/2026 and a Resume button), Zamioculcas Raven: water not known (a dashed ring with a question mark and Soil sensor has no reading: a plant that reports “ok” without any data must never look done). There is no total anywhere on the card.
  • Resume: press it on Beweging: the row becomes open at once (same place), a toast says Beweging resumed with Undo; Undo puts it back paused since 03/10/2026, the original date. With the keyboard: Tab into the list (one stop), ArrowDown and ArrowUp move between rows, r resumes the paused row you are on.
  • Buttons above the list: Date, Length and Both change how the pause is told (the choice to tick); Pause button adds a quiet Pause on open rows. The second card has a pause longer than 30 days: Long pauses shows them as rows (the default) or gathers them in one line that opens them.
  • A row that is told it failed (the grey box under the first card has a button for it) is drawn as Not known, never red; the console says why.
