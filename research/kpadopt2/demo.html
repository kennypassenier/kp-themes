Round 2 of the dashboard adoption: the four still open

Round 2. Kenny approved seven of the eleven sections in formal (the action tiles, the status rows, the facet list, the heat grid, the crowded week and both run timeline sections), so only the four still open are on this page, each rebuilt from the package’s own parts: the matrix (every name the same room from its mark and its edges; the state picked with the package’s segmented control), the bar in a table cell (loading in the theme’s own loading picture, the table’s busy panel over the rows), the week agenda (its steps one control in the theme’s segmented track, a new week waits and its items arrive the theme’s way) and the week agenda’s states (the loading agenda loads, then its items arrive). Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample data and the buttons that drive a demo are this page’s. Every section says what you should see under Expected. Formal only, judged in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top).

Matrix: which stack can reach whichpackagemock data

Look at: state is told by the plate and a word or glyph (cover the colours in your mind: it still reads); the pinned cell, the lit row and column and the dashed rule marker are three different looks; the headers stay put while the narrow box scrolls; the grid does not change height between ready, loading, empty and failed.

Expected:

  • The matrix has the sources down the side and the destinations across the top; the corner says “from ↓ to →”. Every name keeps the same room from its colour mark and from the edges of its header, along the top as down the side. Each pressable cell shows its state in a plate colour and a word or glyph: a number is the ports open (allowed, as declared), open is a mismatch (more open than declared), × is blocked, 0 is nothing declared. A dashed empty cell is a stack with itself, a dashed cell with a dash is “unknown”. A red inner ring on a cell means the traffic was dropped on the way out of the source.
  • Press a name (side or top): its row and column stay bright and everything else dims; press more names to add them; Esc or the name again turns it off. Press a cell: it gets a ring and the line under the matrix names the pair. In the first matrix the arrow keys also pin the cell they land on (the dashboard’s behaviour); the cells that cannot be pressed are skipped and the arrows wrap round.
  • backup → database carries a dashed outline: that is a rule being read touching that pair.
  • Hover or Tab to a cell: a card says the pair and its state in words.
  • The second matrix sits in a box 22 rem wide: the box scrolls sideways, and the names on the top and the side stay in place (sticky) with the corner; scroll it and look at the headers. At the narrow width the side names get shorter and lose their colour mark.
  • State is the package’s segmented control (Ready, Loading, Empty, Failed) and swaps both matrices: loading keeps the shape, the names stay and every cell waits in the theme’s loading picture (formal: its lines ruled in, again and again); empty and failed show one sentence. The grid does not change height.

Cells are paper plates with a navy start rule (allowed), red ink (mismatch) and two red rules (blocked), the way the formal month calendar draws its days; the pinned cell has the double rule; names are small capitals; the headers stay while the narrow box scrolls.

A value with a bar in a data table cellpackagemock data

Look at: the bar and the figure in one cell, the figures aligned on their end edge, bars starting in one place; the sort by the value (not by the text); a row with no reading keeps the row height; the warning and destructive fills are the theme’s; at 390 px the rows are cards and the bars keep their width.

Expected:

  • Capacity per stack: each of CPU, Memory and Disk is a bar with its number on the end edge. The numbers line up on their right edge and the bars start in the same place in every row. From 75 % the bar turns warning, from 90 % destructive (platform’s disk is 94 %, backups’ disk 87 %, media’s memory 91 %); docs’ memory is 112 %: the bar is full and says so with its mark at the end. network has no reading: an empty track and the words “not read”, the same height as every other row.
  • Press a column heading (CPU, Memory, Disk, On disk): the rows sort by the value under the bar, not by the text. Press again to reverse. “On disk” shows “440 MB” and “12.1 GB” but sorts by bytes (it carries data-kp-sort-value). Shift + click adds a second sort key. The row for network (no reading) sorts first ascending.
  • Show loading puts the table in its loading state, as a page that reads the readings again: the rows dim and the table’s busy panel stands over them with the theme’s spinner and “Reading CPU, memory and disk”; every bar and every figure waits in the theme’s own loading picture at its final size (formal: a navy rule ruled across the groove, again and again, and a ruled line where the figure stands). Nothing moves sideways. Press it again: the panel goes and the readings are back.
  • Disk growth (second table) is written in markup only: data-kp-bar="0.82" on a span, and a sort value on the cell for “Full in”. Sort it by “Full in”: 6 days comes before 41 days and “never” comes last, because the sort value is 9999, not the text.
  • On a narrow window the table becomes one card per row with the column names in front of each bar; the bars still line up.

The bar is the formal meter (ruled column, navy entry) with its figure on the end edge in tabular figures; warning and destructive are the ledger’s ochre and red. Sorting follows the value under the bar.

Week agenda: seven days, every item at its time

A column per day, an hour axis, every item a pill at its time of day, a line across today at the current time. Below 40rem of its own width the days become rows. Items open with a click or Enter.

Expected:

  • Seven day columns starting today. Today carries the word Today under its number, a coloured number and a tinted column; a line crosses it at the current time and the pills above the line are quieter.
  • Every pill shows its time and its name, ending in an ellipsis when the name is long. The pill with a ring is the next run; the struck-through ones are off or skipped; the dashed one is the host’s own round, for reference, and does nothing when pressed.
  • Hover a pill (not the dashed one): a card with its detail. Click it or press Enter: the readout under the agenda logs the item.
  • Tab enters the agenda once, on a pill. The arrows move: up and down inside a day, left and right to the nearest item of the next day. Tab leaves.
  • Above the agenda: the range as its title on the left (in the theme’s display face) and, on the right, one control in the theme’s own segmented track: a chevron back, Today, a chevron on. Hover a chevron: its tooltip names it (Previous, Next), as a screen reader does. Today is greyed while the seven days from today are shown, and stays in the Tab order.
  • Press a chevron: the title moves by seven days, the days of the new range arrive the theme’s way, both agendas wait in their loading ghosts for a moment (a real page reads the new items), then the items arrive one after the other. Today brings the first range back the same way.
  • The 360 px column holds the same days as rows, with every item shown and nothing cut off.

Check the full-width agenda and the 360 px one. Ledger entries: a heavy 4px rule on the left of each pill, 2px corners, the weekday in small capitals, dotted dividers between the days like leaders.
