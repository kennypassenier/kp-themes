Round 2 of the dashboard adoption: eight components

Eight things the admin dashboard still draws itself, now built in the package (Kenny’s rule: a consumer draws nothing). One page, eleven sections, judged one after the other in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top), formal only until it is approved: action tiles, status rows and the facet list (nx-action, perstack-chip, hl-opt); the heat grid, the matrix and the bar in a table cell with the table’s data-kp-sort-value (bk-heat, fw-mx, mp-caps, mp-growth); the week agenda and the run timeline (sch-week, ac-tl). Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample data, the buttons that drive a demo and the rows that stand in for hover, focus and press are this page’s. Every section says what you should see under Expected.

Hover, focus and press rows are drawn by copying the package’s own rules (the page reads the stylesheets and re-writes :hover as a class), so they show the theme’s real look. That needs the page to be served over http (as the catalogue does); opened as a file, those rows show only the plain tile.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so hover and press change the look but nothing moves or fades.

1 · Action tiles

A button or a link drawn as a tile: a title and the one sentence that says what it does, in a grid whose tiles on one row are all as tall as the tallest. Use it where a short list of things to do (or places to go) needs a sentence each, on a landing page or in a drawer. A button tile does something; a link tile goes somewhere (and shows a chevron).

An engraved plate with a 3 px corner. Point at a tile: nothing moves, the title takes a 2 px navy underline (the quill). Press and hold: a double navy rule is laid inside the plate (the seal). The destructive tile has a red bar at its start edge and a red title; the disabled one is a quieter plate.

A white card with a hair of shadow and a round corner. Pointing settles it 1 px toward the paper, a press lands it flat 2 px down with the shadow gone. Check the destructive bar is inside the corner, not over it.

A machined panel with a square-ish 2 px corner. Pointing lights a thin line of the four chart colours down the start edge, with a faint glow; keyboard focus is an inset double ring, not an outer one. A press is a plain colour step.

A holo plate: a hairline rim, spaced capitals for the title. Pointing lights a 14 px circuit grid over the tile and the rim goes yellow with a glow; the grid must not make the sentence hard to read. A press washes the tile yellow.

An arcade marquee: a pink frame with a glow, the title in spaced display capitals. Pointing widens the glow; a press dims the tube and drops the tile 1 px. Check the glow stays inside the grid gap and does not touch the next tile.

A sticker on a dashed washi outline with its own shadow. Pointing lifts it 2 px on a longer shadow, a press sticks it down. The outline stays dashed in every state.

A cell of the screen, all mono, with a gutter at the start. Pointing writes a green > prompt in the gutter and boxes the tile in a 2 px green outline 3 px out; a press is reverse video (green ground, black type). Nothing eases.

A herbarium sheet with the leaf corner (top-start and bottom-end square) and an italic title. Pointing lays a dashed trail ring round the tile (the blaze); a press washes it moss. Check the ring follows the leaf corner.

A drawn box: a dashed frame, mono capitals. Pointing closes the frame to a line and lights an 8 px drafting grid in cyan across the tile; a press is a cyan wash. Keyboard focus keeps a dashed outline.

The horizon: an amber line along the foot of the tile with a warm glow rising from it. Pointing thickens the line to 3 px; a press thins it to 1 px and lowers the tile 1 px.

A slab: a 3 px black line on a 6 px hard shadow, a lavender tint, heavy capitals. Pointing grows the shadow to 8 px (the tile does not rise); a press drives the slab into its footprint, shadow gone. Focus is a 4 px ink outline.

Lacquer with a fine gold inlay rule inside the plate, spaced capitals. Pointing brightens the inlay to full gold and the rim to gold; a press sinks the plate 1 px under an inner shadow (the wax seal).

Cut paper: a square slab on a 3 px deep-red shadow, condensed capitals. Pointing throws a red bar in behind the title in two cuts and flips its ink to black; the tile itself does not move. A press drops the slab 3 px onto its shadow.

A transit board: no frame, a red bar hanging from the top edge. Pointing grows the bar from 6 to 10 px, a press to 14 px (it is an inset shadow, so the tile never changes size). The bold titles must stay on one line where they fit, in every column width.

An indicator panel: a muted plate with a dark well along its top and mono capital titles. Pointing wakes the console: heavy scanlines over the plate; a press darkens the plate and seats it 1 px. Check the scanlines do not cut through the sentence.

An anodised plate with a 4 px edge along the top. Pointing turns that edge to the primary; every change is instant and linear (metal does not ease). A press keeps the primary edge and lights a hairline on top.

2 · Status rows

One row per item that is read or checked one by one: a mark, the item’s name, a short detail and, when it failed, a Retry that tries that one item again. The four states are each a shape and a word as well as a colour: a disc with a tick (ok), the theme’s spinner (loading), a disc with a cross (failed, with its reason and a Retry), a dashed ring (empty, with the reason there is nothing).

3 · Facet list

Checkbox rows that narrow a list by one property, each with how many items it would show, and an only button that selects just that row. “Everything” turns every row on. The checkboxes are the theme’s own; only is the theme’s ghost button, a sibling of the label (never inside it), shown when the pointer is on the row or focus is in it, and always on a touch screen.
