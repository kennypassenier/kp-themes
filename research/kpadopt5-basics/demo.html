The basics: calm mode, shortcuts, the lede, the problem alert and the flash button

Five small parts every page of your apps needs, in formal only. Each section below says under Expected what you should see and what each control does, and under “What to look at” what to judge. Your answers were built in: calm mode stops movement and effects but loading keeps moving softly; the shortcut engine warns about keys that need AltGr; a refusal shows a short word on the button and its sentence under it. One thing is yours to pick: how several problems are shown (section 4).

Calm mode: movement and effects off, loading breathes

html[data-kp-calm] with js/calm.js: one switch turns the whole page still, also when the system does not ask for less motion. A theme keeps its colours and its still ornaments; only the moving goes. A loading part is the one exception: it breathes as one slow pulse, so you still see that something is happening.

Expected:

  • Press the switch: the readout under it says calm is on. The toast button now shows its toast without sliding in, the hover on the button is instant, the number jumps to its new value instead of counting, and the spinner and the grey lines of the card change into one slow pulse (about 2.4 s).
  • Press it again: everything moves as the theme moves it. “Motion” stops just the transitions; “Motion and effects” (the default, your answer) also stops every animation; “Everything” also holds loading still. The switch is a normal row in the theme picker.
  • The last sample is what a reader sees when their system already asks for less motion: the switch shows on, dimmed, and says why when you point at it. This demo does not keep your choice (it would stop every other review page too); in an app the choice is kept per device.

Formal’s still look is complete without motion: ruled paper, no glow. In calm the ruled arrival of the toast and the card’s loading lines should simply be there, and the spinner a slowly fading ring.

The line under a heading that explains it

                    

                        .kp-lede under a section or card title says what this part does; .kp-field__why under a field says
                        why it asks. One class instead of three apps’ own (.explain, desc, .text-secondary).
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Each lede is a muted paragraph under its heading, never wider than about 70 characters. “Narrow” stops at 48, “Full” runs
                            the width of the box. “Plain” uses the ordinary ink instead of the muted one.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            The long lede is folded to two lines with “More”; “More” opens it and becomes “Less”. The last lede ends in a “Learn more”
                            link.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            The card shows the lede in every state: pick Loading, Empty or Failed under it. The lede never leaves, so you always know
                            what is loading. The fields show “why” lines; the blocking one is in the alarm ink.
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Formal’s lede is quiet: the body face in the muted ink, no rule and no ornament, so the heading’s own rule above it does the
                        work. Check that it sits one small step under its heading and that a wrapped line keeps the same left edge.
                    

                

                

                    

                        
Schedules

                        

                            Each schedule starts a job at fixed times. Pause one to stop it without deleting it, and delete it when the job is gone
                            for good.
                        

                        
Narrow, plain and full

                        

                            Narrow: a lede that stops at 48 characters, for a card that is itself narrow, so the line never runs long.
                        

                        
Plain: the ordinary ink, for a lede that must not be missed.

                        

                            Full: this lede runs the whole width of its box, which is right for a page-wide note and wrong for a paragraph a person
                            has to read.
                        

                    

                    

                        
A very long explanation

                        

                            A backup copies the files of a stack to the backup disk every night, keeps the last fourteen copies and removes the older
                            ones. A restore puts one of the copies back next to the original; it never overwrites the running stack. If the disk is
                            full the backup stops and says so on this page, and nothing already on the disk is deleted to make room. A copy that
                            cannot be read back is marked and the next backup starts again from the files.
                        

                        
With a link

                        
Restores never overwrite the running stack.

                    

                    

                        

                            
                            
                            Older copies are removed after each backup; keep at least one.
                        

                        

                            
                            
                            Pick at least one weekday, or the schedule never runs.

A problem says what, why and what now

                    

                        .kp-alert with the parts __what, __why, __remedy and
                        __actions, and setProblems() in js/problems.js: the same shape as a
                        chassis::Error (the error and its remedy). Several problems can be shown three ways; you pick one with the ticks.
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Three versions of the same six problems, one under the other. The worst is first in all of them: the red-ink one
                            (critical), then two warnings, then three notes. Each problem says what is wrong in bold, why under it, and “What now”
                            with what to do. The token problem has a command in code with Copy (it turns into “Copied”).
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Press a fix button (“Check again”, “Renew now”…): that problem leaves and the rest close up; focus and the other alerts do
                            not flicker. In the third version, once five are left, the first four stay open and “Show the other 2” opens the rest;
                            after fixing down to four it becomes four plain alerts.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            “Remove all” leaves no space at all (an empty container takes no room); “Bring them back” returns all six. Below: one
                            problem on its own, a problem without a fix, a closable note, the inline run-on layout and the phone width, where the fix
                            button drops under the text at full width.
                        
    •

                        mark, never a coloured block; a note is a navy-tinted card. “What now” is a bold label, not a heading. Text never touches the
                        edge or the fix button.

A button that shows its result

                    

                        .kp-button with js/flash.js: while its work runs it shows the theme’s spinner; then a short word
                        (“Sent”, “Refused”) for a moment; then its own words again, always in the same width. A refusal’s sentence stands in a line
                        under the button until the next press. (Mock: no message is sent and nothing is copied.)
                    

                

                

                    
Expected:

                    
    
                        
  • 
                            Press “Send test”: the words give way to a spinner (the button keeps its size), then “✓ Sent” on a green plate for 1.5 s,
                            then “Send test” again. The width readout under the first row shows rest, busy and result at the same px.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            “Send test (refused)”: after the spinner a red plate says “✕ Refused” for 3 s, and under the button a line says what is
                            wrong and what to do; the line stays until you press again. Nothing in the row moves.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            Press “Work once” twice quickly: the counter says the work ran once. The disabled button explains itself when you point at
                            it. The long label wraps per word and never inside one. “Play all” presses every button.
                        
    • 
                        
  • 
                            The two small options below put the sentence on the button itself (it grows) or in a toast; they are options for other
                            pages, the line under the button is what you chose.
                        
    • 
                    

                

                

                    

                        Formal’s success is paper with a navy rule under the word; a refusal is paper with the 3 px red edge and the word in red ink,
                        the same entry as its warnings. The spinner is formal’s own ruled ring.
