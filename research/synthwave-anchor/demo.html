The four rules of scale

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon on the night, a grid floor, a striped sun. The anchor is the one shape and motion every decision is made from; the colours are already right and are the base. Strict grammar in every attempt: horizontal cuts, radius 2 px, nothing notched, nothing flickers, a neon is a near-white core with the colour in its glow, pink signals, cyan labels, laser yellow warns.

  1. The foot line. The tube is on every component's own foot, as wide as the component, 2 px; a floor of 42 % of its height above it, on blocks.
  2. The page horizon. One tube for the page, as wide as the column, 3 px; components carry 1 px of its light and the horizon lights up under a hand.
  3. The lit length. A hairline on each foot; the lit part is the state times the width.
  4. The press horizon. A hairline whose height is the state: foot on hover, lifted to 80 % of the height on a press; the same tube is the arrival.

Where another theme owns a neighbouring idea each attempt says so (solstice's horizon divider, nostromo's tube strike, cyberpunk's wipe). Kept out on purpose: a glitch, a sun on an arc, an LED bank, a hard slab, dimension lines, a block cursor, growth, mis-registered plates. The network graph is not touched.

The real sizes, for comparison: the package's own button, badge and bar.
