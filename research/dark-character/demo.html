What makes dark dark

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black ruled with a faint grid, near-white ink, and one mechanism, the anodised oxide film that runs along every edge that matters and turns with the pointer. The anchor you picked says what the instrument does: it passes a line. A spectral line of film light sweeps a part from its start at an even pace and lays the film along its edge behind it, turning; when the line reaches the end the figure takes the film through its letters and the turn settles, cyan at the top. Held, the line sweeps back and takes the film with it.

So everything that happens on screen is one pass: a part is laid by the line (arriving, opening), re-laid by it (a live update), swept while it waits (loading, the busy bar, the spinner), and taken off by the pass back (leaving). The film is the only colour and the line the only light: nothing fades, nothing pops, nothing blinks, no lamp lights and no trace flares.

Already decided by you: the anchor = the line lays the film down, turning (the anchor round, update 1), and round one’s progress bar stays exactly (the chamfered ticked track, the film fill whose colour turns as it fills, the `]` head) with the line at its head; the network graph changes in no theme. The questions below turn the anchor into rules for every other component; where a decided pick is at odds with the rule, the pick is on the page as an option named “the pick” or “today”. The full analysis is in themes/dark/CHARACTER.md.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so nothing on this page moves: every scene shows its finished pose, the part laid, cyan at the top. Turn it off in the system settings to judge the motion questions (1 to 4, 9 to 13 and 17).
