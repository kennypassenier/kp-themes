research/futuristic

Three page shapes from the screen

Rough but real mockups of the strongest layout concepts in README.md: a dispatch console with a ticker rail, a case file on an evidence board with HUD brackets, and a ship status page inside an LCARS-style elbow frame. Every paint is a package token; the new shapes are inline CSS in a fui layer. Switch the register to see the same shapes under three themes.

Mockup 1 · concept B · dispatch console

Harbour district dispatch — night watch

Look at: the ticker rail at the top (the package's own .kp-marquee — cyberpunk runs it, nostromo and terminal hold it still); the priority plates, where the number is the carrier and the colour comes second; the unit board's health words beside their dots; the log's dashed rows in the mono face; and how the three regions stack in one column under 60rem. The only thing here that is not in the package today is the grid that holds the four regions and the two small tiles.

Mockup 2 · concepts C and D · case file, evidence board

Case HD-2026-0913 — Dockside warehouse arson

Look at: the bracketed scene photo — four corner gradients, the same technique the cyberpunk register uses on its h2, generalised to any box — with a readout in each corner and a reticle in the middle; the stamp on the summary card, which is the package's own data-kp-label hook, so nostromo pops it down, cyberpunk clears it and terminal inverts it; the pinboard below, where every card is tilted at most 1.5 degrees and the red strings are anchored to the centres of a fixed three-by-two grid, so they land exactly — and are hidden under 48rem, where the board becomes one column; and the chain of custody as a .kp-timeline.

Mockup 3 · concepts A, E and F · elbow frame, systems board, command line

USCSS Kestrel — systems board

Look at: the elbow, drawn without an image as a block with a big outer radius and a cut-out of ground colour with a small inner one; the segmented rail with right-aligned mono numerals, where the last segment absorbs the remaining height; the top and bottom bars ending in a pill; LCARS's warm-structure versus cool-data split mapped onto the package's accent, primary and info pairs, so every label keeps its contract foreground; the readouts in the display face with tabular figures and a ticked gauge whose value is also written out; and the command line under the board, which is what the Expanse does to say "this screen is functional". Under 48rem the rail narrows and drops its sub-labels.
