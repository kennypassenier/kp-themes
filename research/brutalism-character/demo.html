What brutalism is

Brutalism is the slab: raw construction, shown as it is. Off-white paper with a dot grid, boxes drawn in one 3 px ink line, each standing on its own hard shadow, plates in sweet-shop colours with black ink, square corners, Archivo Black capitals. "Hard Copy" (2026-09-08) was printed matter; your later picks carry it onto the building site: the hazard band "like warning tape over wet concrete", the pour, cast, sheared flush, bolts, stacked blocks.

The two meet in one idea: nothing is disguised. The line is the structure, the shadow is the weight, the colour is paint, a control names what it is (your scope-12). The hard shadow is the box's footprint on the page; it never moves. So what arrives is dropped onto its footprint and stops dead; what is pointed at is lifted off it; what is pressed is driven into it; while something waits, its reading is poured in its own place; a new value is slammed onto its offset, as the headline's words are; a warning is taped off; what leaves is lifted off and gone. Nothing eases into place, nothing bounces, nothing fades.
