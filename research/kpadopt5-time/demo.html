Time and sentence: seven new parts

Seven parts that schedules, reminders and settings forms draw by hand today, built in the package: a weekday picker, a list of the next times a schedule runs, a snooze picker, a picker for options that carry a date and an age, a form that reads as a sentence, a slider with a typed value, and a bar for unsaved changes. This page asks formal only; the other themes follow once formal is approved.

Judge in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top): 1 topics, one step each in formal. Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does. Everything with a kp- class and every script under js/ is the package; the sample data, the frames marked “phone width” (390 px boxes standing in for a phone), the fixed clock (Saturday 17/10/2026, so a reminder for “tomorrow” always means 18/10/2026), the event readouts and the buttons that drive a state are this page’s.

Weekday picker: a week and a month

<div class="kp-weekdays" data-kp-weekdays> (js/weekdays.js): the days a schedule runs, as chips, with a line in words under them. Press chips; nothing here is stored.

Expected:

  • Week: seven chips of one width, Mon to Sun, Mon and Thu filled. Under them the quick picks Weekdays, Weekend and Every day, and the line Mon and Thu.
  • Press a chip: it fills or empties and the line follows (Mon, Wed and Fri). All seven is allowed and reads Every day; Mon to Fri reads Every weekday. A quick pick fills its days, shows pressed while they match, and pressing it again clears them.
  • Month: the days 1 to 31 and Last in seven columns; 1 and 15 filled, the line On the 1st and 15th; the last day reads On the last day.
  • Keyboard: Tab enters the chips once, the arrow keys move along them, Space or Enter turns one. The grey box under each picker shows the event the page received.

The chips are formal’s own pills (white with a hairline, navy when on); the line under them is plain ink, the quick picks are small ghost buttons. Seven chips, all one width, and the month’s days line up in the same seven columns.
