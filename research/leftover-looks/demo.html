Two leftover looks: phantom’s second plate and deco’s menu inlay

Two findings from the package review, each shown here as a proposal next to what the package does now. Nothing in the package has changed: the proposals live in this page’s stylesheet only, and the package is changed only for the choice you tick. Judge them in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top): phantom first, then deco.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so the menus appear without their inlay being drawn. Turn it off to judge the movement.

1 · Phantom

Cut paper cannot show its hard shadow.

Phantom’s cards, menus, toasts and dialogs are cut with a clip-path, and a clip path also clips the part’s own box-shadow, so the deep red second plate the character asks for (G5, G7) is declared for the toast and the dialog and never seen. Top row: what the register does now; below it, three ways to let the plate show.

Compare each column with the top row: in “Now” a card, a menu, a dialog and a toast are flat paper on the black ground; in the three proposals each has its deep red second plate. Look at the cut corners (top start and bottom end): the plate must follow the cut, not square it. Then pick one of the three.

NowThe package as it is: the plate is declared and clipped away.

1 · One hard plateRecommended. A deep red copy of the part, shifted down and to the end.

2 · The plate as a screenThe same copy, printed as the register’s halftone dots.

3 · The plate as a slashA red slash standing out of the end edge only.

2 · Deco

A menu’s inlay starts in the wrong place.

A deco plate’s gold inlay is drawn from the middle of its top edge, round both ways. For a menu that opens from a button it starts at the middle of the menu, not under the button, because the register cannot know where the button is. Proposal: js/menu-button.js writes the button’s centre on the open menu as --kp-menu-anchor-x, and deco’s register draws from there.

Press “Start”, “Middle” and “End” to open the menu of the button at that place on both bars. Watch where the gold line begins: in “Now” always from the middle of the menu’s top edge; under the button in the proposal. “Hold the draw at a fifth” freezes the first moment of the inlay so you can see where it begins.
