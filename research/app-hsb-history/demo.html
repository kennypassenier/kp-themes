HTTPSwitchboard: figures and history

HTTPSwitchboard accepts a message in one shape and delivers it in another. This page shows how its status page could show what happened over the last 24 hours: four key figures with their trend, one strip of hour cells per profile, and the ways the page can stay current.

New capability needed

Today the service keeps counters only, so none of the history below exists yet. It needs a small per-hour record per profile: for each of the last 24 hours how many messages were received, delivered and failed, the total delivery time, and how long the profile was not working. Hours before the service started recording show as “no data”.

Real profile: alertmanager (kyu topic alerts.raw to Home Assistant). Mock profile: uptime-kuma (POST /uptime-kuma), invented here to show a second row. The clock is fixed at 19/10/2026 14:40 so every figure agrees with every other. Judge in the review dialog; nothing moves until you press something.

Key figures: received, delivered, failed, average duration

Four numbers for the last 24 hours of one profile, each with its trend by the hour; a number counts up or down when it changes.

Expected:

  • Four tiles in one row (two on a tablet, one on a phone), all the same height. The numbers are real counts, written in full: 31 alerts received, 29 delivered (93.5% of received), 2 failed, 149 ms average. The figures add up: received equals delivered plus failed, and the same totals feed the strips and the list in the other sections.
  • A message arrives and A delivery fails change the numbers: they count from the old value to the new one and land on it exactly, and the trend gets a new last point. Profile swaps in the mock uptime-kuma figures.
  • Trend tiles (default): point at a trend or use the arrow keys to read one hour, as 19/10/2026 11:00 · 5 alerts. Sparklines: quiet lines; press Failed to list only the failures with their reason, press again to see the latest deliveries.
  • Loading lays the busy card over the whole card; Empty says history is not recorded yet; Error says it could not be read and Try again works. The card keeps its size in every state.

Formal sets the numbers in its serif figures with the ruled tile edge. Check that a unit (alerts, ms) stays clear of its number, that the trend ends at the right edge, that nothing jumps when you press a state, and that no label is cut with an ellipsis at 390 px.

    Uptime by the hour, per profile

    One strip per profile: each cell is an hour of the last 24, coloured by how that hour went, with the share of working time on top.

    Expected:

    • Two strips of 24 cells: alertmanager and uptime-kuma (mock). The first four cells of each are grey (no data: recording began at 19:00); then green (up), amber (degraded) and red (down) cells. The line above each strip gives the share and the incidents in exact figures, for alertmanager 91.7% · 5 incidents; the share is cut, never rounded up, so any time down never reads 100%.
    • Point at a cell or Tab to the strip and use the arrows: a hover card names the hour (19/10/2026 02:00) and says why, for example Denied: the hub refused the credentials. Press it (default): the quick info lists the lines of what happened. Escape closes it and returns to the cell.
    • Right now: Hub down turns the current hour red and the summary adds an incident at once; Working restores it. Loading, Empty and Error keep the strips the same size; Loading lays the busy card.
    • The tones are the package’s plain ones on purpose: a calm palette would soften a red hour, which this page must not.

    Formal draws thin square cells in its muted green, amber and its destructive red with the same hairline gap. Look for a clear gap between the strip and its title and summary, for the pointed cell lifting a few pixels, and for the quick info plate being opaque and readable over the next strip.

    Staying current: refresh chooser or live push

    A list of delivered alerts that keeps itself up to date, either on a timer the reader sets or by the service pushing each alert, with a count of what came in while the reader looked elsewhere.

    Expected:

    • Refresh chooser (default): opens at Every 5 min (a slow thin rule counts down to the next read). Start at 30 s sets the default interval; the line says updated N s ago, a thin rule counts down to the next read, Refresh now (or the R key) reads at once. Fail the next refresh makes the following read fail: the list stays, the line says the refresh failed and when it retries, with Retry now.
    • Live push replaces the chooser with a Live mark. Alerts arrive (6 over 12 s) plays six alerts one by one once, then stops.
    • The count of new alerts: scroll the list down with Scroll down, then let alerts arrive (Refresh now adds three; push adds six): a pill at the top says 3 new alerts · back to the top with the exact number, and a divider marks where the new ones start. Press the pill (or Home) and the list jumps up and follows again. While the list is at the top, new alerts just appear and there is no pill.
    • Loading, Empty (no alerts yet) and Error (the list could not be read, Try again) fill the same card; the toolbar above the list keeps its place.

    The pill is formal’s opaque plate with its ink outline, over the rows and never through the text. Check that the count does not shift the words when it grows from 9 to 10, that the rows keep their columns while new ones arrive, and that the chooser’s buttons are the same segmented control used elsewhere.
