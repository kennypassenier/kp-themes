The fifth and sixth adoption rounds, part 10 of 11: Pictures, documents and printing

Formal is approved. Things you look at or take away: the picture gallery, the document preview, anchors on headings and rows, a QR code, and a printed checklist. This part asks the eleven other themes for 8 sections (88 pairs), one theme per step in the review dialog. The sections were approved in formal in several rounds; each is shown here in its latest version, with the defaults you chose.

Every section says under Expected what you should see and what each control does (written for formal; the line under “What to look at” says what is the theme’s own).

Gallery: tiles and the lightbox

<div class="kp-gallery" data-kp-gallery> (js/gallery.js): a grid of small pictures; a press opens the picture large, with previous and next, also for the clip.

Expected:

  • Grid: a dashed Add pictures tile first, then thirteen square tiles (twelve pictures and a clip with a play mark). Point at a tile: its border turns primary and the name of who added it appears on a plate along the bottom edge. The pictures themselves are never tinted or dimmed.
  • Press a tile: the lightbox fills the window: the title and an exact position (4 of 13) at the top, the picture as large as fits, previous and next on its sides, the caption, who added it and when (dd/mm/yyyy HH:MM) under it. Left and Right (or the side buttons) step; Home and End go to the ends. At the last picture the next arrow is off and says This is the last picture (the end is the end); it does not wrap.
  • z or a double click zooms to the picture’s full size (you scroll inside the stage) and back; + and - zoom in and out; f goes full screen; Esc closes and the focus returns to the tile. The clip plays with its own controls; stepping away stops it. After three seconds without moving, the controls fade; moving the pointer or pressing a key brings them back.
  • Loop (second gallery): the same lightbox with data-kp-lightbox-loop="on": at the last picture the next arrow goes on to the first. Delete in the foot of the first gallery asks first; the demo only removes the tile.
  • Tick under The shape of a tile in the dialog: the first gallery follows.

Dark is a spectral instrument: near-black with a faint grid, chamfered corners and one film of cyan-to-magenta light along the edges that matter. Check that this part wears that film on its frames and focus, that anything opening or arriving is swept in from the left over its own box and never just faded, and that text keeps clear of every edge.

Cyberpunk is a netrunner’s HUD: signal yellow on a near-black ground with scanlines, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong, and plates with a cut corner. Check the part reads as a holo card, that things appear in hard glitch ticks (never an ease or a glide), and that no text sits against a cut corner or the rim.

Synthwave is the drive into the sunset, 1984: a neon horizon, the grid floor, the striped sun and tubes with a soft bloom. Check the part sits on its floor with neon-tube edges, that things rise over the horizon or switch on like a tube (nothing tears or runs diagonal), and that text keeps clear of the glow and the edge.

Pastel is a risograph sticker sheet in a planner: lavender milk paper, plum ink, a second ink that never quite lines up, stickers and taped-in notes. Check the part looks printed in two passes and stuck on, soft and never sharp, and that text keeps clear of the sticker edge and the tape.

Forest is a ranger’s field kit: a map on kraft paper with contour lines and trail blazes, and wood that grows on it. Check the part has the leaf corners (top-right and bottom-left rounded), that things grow out of their anchor rather than pop, and that text keeps clear of the corners and the blazes.

Blueprint is the working drawing: a Prussian-blue sheet, a cyan pen line, white lettering and an amber checker’s pencil. Check the part is drawn by the pen (outline first, then inked), that corner brackets and dimension lines sit where they say, and that no lettering touches a line or a bracket.

Solstice is the low sun on warm charcoal: light comes from below and catches the foot of every plate, the voice is a serif, and everything is slow. Check the warm light rises from the foot of the part, that things are kindled or eclipsed rather than faded, and that text keeps clear of the lit edge.

Brutalism is the slab: one hard ink line, a hard offset shadow where the part would fall, flat paint and controls that name what they are. Check the part stands on its own shadow, that things drop onto their footprint and stop dead (no ease, no glow), and that text keeps clear of the line and the shadow.

Deco is the grand lobby of 1925: deep blue lacquer with a chevron wallpaper, gold hairlines and small jewels, ivory type, symmetry and slow motion. Check the part has its gold inlay (a hairline, not a flat fill), that it draws itself on and is covered by a great fan when it leaves, and that text keeps clear of the inlay.

Nostromo is the ship’s console, 1979: a moulded beige case with label tape, screws, vents and a lamp on every switch, and amber CRT screens set into it. Check the part reads as plastic case or as screen (not a mix), that things switch on or are struck on the tube in whole frames, and that no label touches the case edge or a vent.

Titanium is a material: bare machined titanium over a carbon twill with an anodised oxide film, cut, milled, indexed and stamped. Check the part looks machined (crisp edges, the film only where it carries meaning), that nothing breathes, bounces or swells, and that text keeps clear of the milled edge.

Operations guide

                        

                            How the nightly jobs of the home server are run, checked and restored. Read the section you need; every section has its
                            own link.
                        

                        
Back up the stacks

                        
Every stack is backed up at 03:00. The first run of a new stack takes longer because nothing is cached yet.

                        
What is left out

                        
Caches, temporary uploads and the media library, which has its own copy on the second disk.

                        
Restore a backup

                        

                            Pick the stack, pick the snapshot, press Restore. The stack stops while its files are put back and starts again
                            afterwards.
                        

                        
Small print

                        
A heading of level 4 gets no mark: it is rarely worth a link.

                        
Schedule a run

                        

                            Choose the days and the time of day. The next times it will run are listed under the picker, so a mistake shows before it
                            costs a night.
                        

                        
A heading that is long enough to wrap onto a second line so that you can see the mark keep its own room at the end

                        
The mark does not take the heading’s width: the words wrap exactly where they would without it.

                        
Last words

                        
That is all. Everything above can be linked to from a chat.

Time field

                        

                            One field for a time of day in 24 hours: typed, stepped with the arrow keys or picked from the package's own list. Unlike
                            the browser's own time box it never shows AM and PM, and it looks like the rest of the theme.
                        

                    

                    

                        
Expected:

                        
    
                            
  • 
                                Type 0230, 2:30, 02.30, 2h30 or 230 and leave the field: it says 02:30. Type
                                7: 07:00. Type 24:00 or abc: the box turns red and the line says
                                Use a time like 03:00; what you typed stays. Paste 2:30 PM: it becomes 14:30 and a quiet line
                                says it was read as 24 hours.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                Up and Down step by 5 minutes (Shift or Page Up and Down by an hour), 23:55 up is
                                00:00 and is announced as the next day. The clock button (or Alt+Down) opens a list: the nearest time is
                                already highlighted, Night 03:00 and Morning 08:00 are chips above it, times within an hour of the
                                nightly round 03:00 carry a ! and near 03:00. Enter picks, Esc closes.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                The sentence below keeps its underline look; the field with a reason is off and says why; the loading one shows its
                                own skeleton at its final width; the one whose presets failed shows a compact plate with Retry in the list.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                At 390 px the field fills the line, 44 px high, and the list is a sheet at the foot of the screen with a title and
                                rows 44 px high.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                The groups marked (live option) change one setting of the main component while it runs and it redraws at once,
                                nothing reloads: every choice you are asked about is a real option with a default.
                            
    •

                            as a menu, the nearest time on a navy wash. In the sentence the same field is the navy underline in semibold.

Console

                        

                            A prompt and, for every command, a block that shows whether it still runs, worked or failed. The package draws the prompt,
                            the recall of earlier lines and the blocks; what a command may do stays with the app. Your earlier lines are kept only
                            while this tab is open (a line can hold a secret).
                        

                    

                    

                        
Expected:

                        
    
                            
  • 
                                Type uptime and press Enter (or Run): a block appears with the command in the mono face,
                                Sending…, then Running for 1 s with a spinner, then exit 0 · Done with the output under it.
                                Up and Down walk your earlier lines; Esc clears the line; Ctrl+L clears the screen and a
                                toast offers Undo for 6 seconds. The key : jumps to the prompt from anywhere on the page (press ? for
                                the list).
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                The sample buttons run the other cases: a long output (folded to 12 lines with Show all), one that prints as it
                                goes with Cancel, a failing one (exit 1 · Failed), a line with no output ((no output)), one the app
                                refuses (what and what to do now, your line stays in the prompt), a dropped connection (Not sure it finished
                                with Check again) and one over the 4 KB limit (Only the first … shown). rm -rf / needs a second
                                press:
                                Run it anyway?
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                Each block has Copy command, Copy output (each says Copied in the same width), Run again and Collapse. When you
                                scroll up in a long list, a pill says how many commands arrived out of sight and jumps back.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                Empty, loading, failed and a console with running switched off all keep the body's height; the empty one invites you
                                to run a command, the failed one has Retry. At 390 px the select goes above the prompt, Run takes its own full line
                                and the bar stays at the foot.
                            
    • 
                            
  • 
                                The groups marked (live option) change one setting of the main component while it runs and it redraws at once,
                                nothing reloads: every choice you are asked about is a real option with a default.
                            
    • 
                        

                    

                    

                        

                            Formal keeps it plain: a card with a hairline, the output blocks on white over a pale ground, the prompt glyph and the
                            focus ring in navy, the exit codes in the success green and the destructive red with their glyphs.
