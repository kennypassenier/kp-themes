HTTPSwitchboard: health and problems

HTTPSwitchboard accepts a message in one shape and delivers it in another. Its status page shows one row per profile. The real profile is alertmanager: it reads the topic alerts.raw from the kyu hub (subscription switchboard) and delivers to a Home Assistant webhook on homeassistant.lan:8123 (the webhook’s path is a credential and is never shown). The second profile, uptime-kuma, is a mock: it takes POST /uptime-kuma and exists only to show two profiles side by side.

Judge in the review dialog (“Review in a dialog” at the top): five sections, one step in formal. A profile is starting (nothing tried since the service started or since the last recheck), working, failing (deliveries fail and messages wait on the hub), denied (the hub refused the switchboard’s credentials) or hub-down (the hub cannot be reached). Everything with a kp- class is the package; the sample data and the buttons that drive a state are this page’s. Nothing moves until you press a button, except the freshness lines, which tick by design.

Attention band: the profiles that need a person, worst first

.kp-attention with setAttention() (js/attention.js): one alert per profile that is not well, the worst on top, each with the button that goes to its fix. When every profile works the band takes no room and a calm note stands in its place.

Expected:

  • On load: alertmanager is denied and uptime-kuma is starting. The band shows two alerts, the red one first (denied is critical), the note about the other under it, although the page handed them over the other way round.
  • Press other states for either profile. Failing is a warning, starting a note, denied and hub-down are critical. An alert that is still true stays the same element (it is not announced again, and a focused button keeps its focus); a profile that turns working leaves the band in the theme’s way and the rest close up.
  • When both work, the band is gone and the drawing All profiles are working stands in its place. The same band is shown again at 390 px under the first, driven by the same buttons.
  • A button in the band (See the fix) says in the grey line under the band what it would open; the real page scrolls to the problem alert of that profile (next section).

Formal is a ledger: navy rules and red ink for what needs the eye. Look at the critical alerts wearing red ink along their start edge, at the order (red before amber before note, whatever the order given), at the words keeping clear of the edge and of the button, and at the band closing up without a gap when an alert leaves.

Problem alert: what happened, why, and what to do now

.kp-alert parts with setProblems() (js/problems.js): for each unhealthy profile one alert that says what is wrong, why, and what to do, with a fix button and, where a command helps, the command with a Copy button.

Expected:

  • On load: both profiles at once, as two alerts. alertmanager (denied, red) says the hub refused our credentials, why, and What now: re-issue the switchboard’s kyu token. uptime-kuma (failing, amber) says deliveries to Home Assistant fail and gives journalctl -u http-switchboard with a Copy button.
  • Copy puts exactly the command on the clipboard and the button says so for a moment. Hub down carries systemctl status kyu (check kyu on CT 109). The webhook’s path never appears; only the host does.
  • Starting is a note you may close (it is not a fault, so it is the only dismissible one). All four kinds shows one of each kind at once, as a sample, for judging the layouts.
  • Layout switches data-kp-problems-mode at once: Each, List, Folded. With Folded, the alert folds once as many problems are there as Fold from says (2 to 5; 5 is the default). Run on sets data-kp-problem-layout="inline". The choice in the dialog only sets which layout is the default.
  • Pressing a fix button says in the grey line what the real page does; the problem stays until the profile really recovers.

A problem is a ledger entry: red ink along the start edge for critical, the three parts one under the other with their words aligned, the command in code on its own line with Copy at the end, and the fix button in the fixed place at the end of the alert. Look at the text keeping clear of the edge and of Copy, at the folded alert, and at the phone frame.

Last delivery: how long ago, and when it is too long

data-kp-ago (js/freshness.js): the words “last delivery 3 min ago” tick by themselves, and turn into a warning plate once the profile has been quiet longer than it is expected to be.

Expected:

  • On load: alertmanager says last delivery 3 min ago with a quiet time of 1 h, so it is fresh. uptime-kuma (mock) says no delivery yet. The seconds tick; the line keeps the width of the longest text of its unit, so the words beside it never move when it goes from 59 s to 1 min.
  • Point at a line (or focus it): the moment shows as dd/mm/yyyy HH:mm in Brussels time. The exact counts beside it (received, delivered, failed) are the real numbers.
  • Last delivery moves the moment: Now, 3 min ago, 40 min ago, 2 days ago, or Never. Quiet time is data-kp-stale-after: when the line is older than that it gets the warning plate with no other thing moving, and says so once to a screen reader (data-kp-ago-announce="state"). Example: 40 min ago with 15 min allowed is stale; with 1 h it is not.
  • Deliver now sets the moment to now for both profiles and the plate goes away. The sentence under each line says the quiet time in words.

A stale line turns into formal's warning plate, the way the ledger marks an entry that needs the eye; nothing around it moves when it turns. Look at the words keeping clear of the plate’s edge, at the figures lining up in their own column in both rows, and at the three columns holding their places when the line gets longer.

Connection banner: the link to the kyu hub

data-kp-connection (js/connection.js): a band that says the switchboard has lost its hub, is trying again, was refused, or is back. It takes no room while the hub answers.

Expected:

  • Online, the banner takes no room in either page. Unreachable (after 1.5 s of grace): The kyu hub is unreachable, why, the exact count of waiting messages, “next try in 3 s” and Retry now; the countdown runs.
  • Retry now (or the countdown reaching zero) says Reaching the kyu hub… with the attempt number, then the mock hub answers: Connected to the kyu hub again in green for 3 s, then the band leaves. Refused is the red plate The kyu hub refused our credentials with the hub’s own sentence; retrying it is refused again. Back brings it back at once. 1 s drop shows nothing at all (the grace). Retrying shows the attempt under way.
  • Waiting sets data-kp-pending (0, 3, 128 or 1204; the exact number, never “99+”). At 0 the count line is left out. The page below the bar says what the profiles do meanwhile.
  • The dialog’s choice sets whether the banner stands under the bar (data-kp-placement="top") or at the foot; both pages follow it at once. The phone page shows the one-line form with Retry now.

The offline plate is a warning entry with its red rule along the start edge, the refused plate the destructive one, the back plate green; the plates arrive as a rule ruled across and leave the same way backwards. Look at the words keeping clear of Retry now and of the edge, at the count staying exact, and at the page below moving down (top) or being covered (foot) and coming back without a jump.

Profiles area: loading, empty and could not be read

.kp-card[data-kp-state] (js/card-state.js) with the busy card (js/busy-card.js) and data-kp-art (js/art.js): the card that lists the profiles keeps its place and its size whether it is reading, empty, failed or full.

Expected:

  • Ready: two profile rows on one grid (name and route, the three exact counts, the health badge). Loading: the data table’s own busy card in the middle of the card, animated, saying Reading the profiles…; the rows stay under it, dimmed and inert.
  • No profiles: the empty plate with the none drawing, No profiles are configured, one sentence and the button Show how to add one. Could not be read: the profiles file could not be read, as the dialog’s choice sets (error drawing, or the card’s red alert), with Try again: it goes to loading for a moment and back to ready.
  • All four states fill the same footprint: the card does not change height, and the header and the card under it do not move. The phone card below follows the same buttons at 390 px; its rows stack but keep the order name, counts, badge.

Loading is the seal being pressed on the ledger, centred over the whole card; empty and failed are the ledger’s own drawings with ruled strokes, centred, the title at heading size. Look at the card keeping its height in all four states, at the words keeping clear of the plate’s edge, and at Try again and the add button being normal-sized buttons.
