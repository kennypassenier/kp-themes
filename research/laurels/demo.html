Laurels and platforms: four directions from the ground up

Kenny rejected catalogue/media.html#laurels in shade-light: "this just looks ugly, redo these completely from the ground up". This page shows the laurels and platforms as they ship, then four genuinely different directions. Every direction uses the same claims and the same four platform names as the catalogue block, so only the design differs. Parts marked package are the stylesheets as they ship. Parts marked mock are lr- rules written for this page, placed on the package's own classes (.kp-laurels, .kp-platforms, .kp-card); they first reset the registers' styling, so each direction is drawn only from tokens and looks the same way in every theme. The page opens in shade-light; loaded registers: shade-light, pastel, dark. The theme menu in the bar reaches the other themes too.

0 · As it ships package

Look at: in shade-light, the accent dot stands alone on the first line because .kp-laurels b is display: block in css/components.css; the pill radius (999px) is drawn around two lines of text, so the corners crowd the words; the platforms line is four monospace words with a rule over it and nothing to say what they are. This is the state the four directions replace. Switch to pastel and dark to see that each register answers differently today.

A · Figures: a stat band mock

Look at: each figure is set large in the display face and is by far the loudest thing in its column; the source sits under it at body size, muted, and wraps as a sentence. A thin rule in --border-strong marks where each column starts. The platforms become one quiet line that says what it is: a small uppercase label (an optional <b>, which the formal register already styles in .kp-platforms) and the names separated by drawn dots, not characters. In the narrow pane the columns stack, no figure breaks across lines, and a platform name never splits. Markup change: none for the laurels; the platforms label is optional.

B · Wreaths and store badges mock

Look at: the literal reading of "laurels": each claim is centred between two branches drawn for this page (a stem and leaves, painted in --primary through a CSS mask, mirrored on the right). The bold value still has to be the loudest thing, above the branches' colour. The platforms become badges in the manner of the app-store download badges: inverted (--foreground ground, --background text), a small glyph drawn for this page, a small "Available on" line over the name. Check dark: the badges turn light on a dark page. In the narrow pane the wreaths stack one per row and the badges wrap whole. Markup change: none for the laurels; each platform gains a glyph and a <small> line.

C · The proof strip and the name wall mock

Look at: the press-strip reading: a caption at the start of one ruled band, then each claim as a single sentence with its value in bold, between hairlines. Nothing is large; the band's two rules in --border-strong carry the section. The platforms become a logo-cloud grid of equal cells separated by 1px hairlines, the names set as wordmarks in the display face, muted until hovered. A consumer with real logos would put a monochrome SVG in each cell instead of the word. In the narrow pane the caption, the claims and the cells each take a full row. Markup change: a caption element beside the list, and a caption over the wall.

D · The ledger card mock

Look at: a spec-sheet reading inside the package's own .kp-card: the source on the left, a dotted leader, the value on the right in the mono face with tabular figures, so the three values line up in one column. The <b> is moved to the end with order, the markup is unchanged. The platforms become the card's foot on the --muted plate: a label, then each name with a tick drawn from two borders in --primary. In the narrow pane the leader shrinks first; a value never wraps and never overlaps its source. Markup change: a title element inside the card, and the optional <b> label in the platforms.
