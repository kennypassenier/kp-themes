Effects and surfaces

A small vocabulary of attributes that say what a passage is — a hero, an app surface, a divider, a phrase that should land — and leave what that looks like to the theme. A theme that has an answer paints it; a theme that has none stays quiet, and the page reads the same either way.

When to use it

Mark the structure of a page you want a theme to be able to dramatise: the opening surface, the working surface, the seam between them, the words that carry the point. Do not use the attributes to ask for a particular effect — there is no attribute for a glitch or a flicker, only for the thing a theme may glitch or flicker. A theme decides, and a reader who asked for reduced motion gets the words without the drama.

Accessibility

  • Built in — every reveal keeps the words in the document from the start; an effect changes how they arrive, never whether they are there.
  • Built in — a reader who asked for reduced motion gets no reveal at all, and the marked phrases are shown at once.
  • Built in — a highlight on a marked phrase keeps the contrast of the text around it; the contrast gate measures the pair.
  • Yours — mark the structure, not the wish: the attributes name what a passage is, and a theme that has no answer leaves the page as it was.

Variants and states

NameWhat it is
data-kp-surface="hero"The opening surface: the one place a theme may be loud.
data-kp-surface="app"The working surface: forms, tables, cards. A theme keeps it readable before anything else.
data-kp-surface-align="center"Sets a surface’s content on its middle: the text centres, a narrower child takes auto margins and a row centres its controls. Off unless a page asks; every surface keeps its content off its own inline edges either way, one lg step in.
data-kp-dividerThe seam between two surfaces. A theme may draw it as a rule, a tear, or nothing.
data-kp-revealheadline, emphasis or rule: the element a theme may bring in with a reveal. Once per session unless data-kp-reveal-every="load" says otherwise.
data-kp-reveal-triggerA control that replays the reveals of the surface it sits in.
data-kp-label-openThe word a stamp takes once the file is open, beside data-kp-label for the word it wears while sealed. A theme whose demo changes the stamp answers it; the rest keep the one word.
data-kp-openWhat the module writes on a reveal container while its trigger holds it open, so a register can answer the open state without a script of its own.
<mark>Emphasis inside running text. Themes answer it with a highlight, a glow, or a decipher — never with less contrast than the text around it.
data-kp-textThe copy of a headline or a navigation label that a register’s glitch pseudo-elements read; the module writes it on a headline, the NavBar writes it on its links.
data-kp-effectsWhat the module writes on the document root before first paint, so a stylesheet can tell an armed page from one without the script; data-kp-effects-done follows when the reveals of a load have run.
kp-effect-unknownThe event the module dispatches on an element that names a surface or a reveal it does not know, so a typo is heard rather than silently quiet.
--kp-measure: liveBlueprint's own device, outside the six-hook vocabulary [S48]: on the root, the module wraps every `data-kp-reveal="headline"` element in `[data-kp-measured]` and builds two dimension lines beside it — never a fixed width. The horizontal one (`[data-kp-dim]`, `[data-kp-dim-line]`, two `[data-kp-dim-tick]` and a `[data-kp-measure]` label) reads the headline's own `getBoundingClientRect().width`, on load, on resize and once the page's fonts are ready, and writes the same rounded number to the line's `style.width` and the label in one breath. The vertical one (`[data-kp-elev-line]`, two `[data-kp-elev-tick]` and `[data-kp-elev-measure]`) needs no measurement of its own width to size itself — CSS containment does that — only a read to print the wrap's own height into its label.
