Page effects

What a theme does to a page rather than to a component: the hook vocabulary of js/effects.js — the headline reveal, the marks in a lede, the rule under a heading, the dividers, the dossier with its stamp and redactions, the hero and app surfaces — and the parts that had no page of their own: the call-to-action link and the end-side navbar, the button readout, a compact form, the shortcut sheet on a phone, a plain table falling into cards, and the theme picker's focus ring. Until this page these were judged by tests only. The markup in every block is the markup the concept pages use.

A reveal is shown twice. The live copy plays when the page loads and again on Replay, and again whenever the theme is switched, so each theme's own routine can be watched. The copy in the dashed stage is at rest: attached as for someone who asked for less motion, so it is the look the live copy must end on. Only the stage is what a verdict records.

Headline

Look at: in every theme the headline arrives once and then stands as exactly its own text — no leftover glyph, no caret, no word moved off its baseline — in the theme's display face on the hero ground, and nothing in the arrival flashes. The live copy must end looking exactly like the copy at rest. Then per theme: formal fades and rises once, whole, no glyph ever touched; cyberpunk deciphers left to right out of glyph noise and ends with one slice burst; nostromo pops down under a clip, the text whole throughout; synthwave is chrome, one tracking wipe and one shine; terminal types itself with a block caret that leaves with the last letter; retro clears out of a dither under a hard white shadow; brutalism slams in word by word onto its yellow offset; phantom shouts in word by word; dark, titanium, shade-dark and shade-light resolve word by word out of a blur; light opens through a rounded clip window; pastel's overprint springs from a wide mis-registration to its 2 px rest offset; sepia settles from a pale ghost of the ink to the full ink; solstice is covered by a calibration overlay that clears; lapis stands covered, then burnishes to flat gold; grotesk resolves from a blur and brightness sweep; blueprint fades in inside a measurement frame whose readout names the box it holds; deco frames it in a cartouche that scales in; high-contrast wipes in from the left; forest simply stands.

Lede marks

Look at: the two marked phrases in the lede. In the live copy they start covered or empty and clear one after another after about a second and a half; the words stay in their places the whole time, so no line re-breaks when a mark lands. At rest, in every theme, the marked words must read at full contrast on their plate and still look like the same sentence as the words around them. Per theme: formal washes the accent in under each phrase, left to right; blueprint washes in cyan; light sweeps a highlighter; pastel fills mint, then violet; sepia deepens from ink to the plate colour; shade-light fills left to right in the accent; dark ignites and settles to the signal; lapis is ivory with no rule, then gold ink with a gold underline; nostromo is a highlight — ink on transparent with a signal-orange underline, no plate; terminal is inverse video; retro is the selection, white on navy; brutalism the yellow plate with the line; phantom black ink on the red plate; synthwave a tube switched on, a near-white core with a glow; cyberpunk a redaction bar that lifts. deco, forest, high-contrast and shade-dark never cover the lede at all: a gold highlight, a felt highlight, a plain accent plate and a static signal plate, already there on load.

Section rule

Look at: the rule under Sign the handover. In the live copy it is drawn only once the heading is scrolled into view: scroll the heading out of the window, press Replay the rule, and scroll back — it must draw in front of you, once, and then stand. At rest, in every theme that draws one, the rule runs the width the theme gives it, sits under the heading without touching its descenders, and is the theme's own colour, not the browser's. Per theme: formal a 2 px primary line drawn from the left over the whole width; nostromo grows from nothing to a full underline; retro a groove that rules itself in twelve steps; synthwave a laser line drawn from the centre; terminal dashes that type themselves out; brutalism a six-pixel bar; phantom a rail that sweeps from grey to red; dark and titanium the oxide, from the leading edge; deco fades toward transparent; light, blueprint, forest, pastel, sepia, solstice, lapis, grotesk, shade-light, shade-dark and cyberpunk draw left to right. high-contrast draws once on load.

Dividers

Look at: the plain divider (data-kp-divider) between the hero and the app surface, and the alt divider (data-kp-divider="alt") between the app surface and the footer, laid out as the concept page lays them. In every theme a divider spans the full width, never overlaps the text of the surfaces it separates, and the two surfaces never touch without it: a theme that paints nothing still leaves one step of room. Per theme: formal a hairline with a short navy tick, the alt one dashed; cyberpunk the razor tear — above the ridge exactly the surface above, below it exactly the surface below, with no seam of a third colour, and the alt one a different tear; nostromo a row of vent dots, the alt one on the darker sidebar plate for the footer seam; synthwave the horizon, a 2 px line with its glow over a receding grid; grotesk two black rules with a signal hairline exactly centred between them; high-contrast a razor tear whose two tones swap; lapis the four-ring frame with the girih dot, the alt one a plain hatch; terminal dashes with a plus at each end, the alt one doubled; forest torn paper, the alt one two concentric rings; pastel pearls, round beads on a thread with no bar and no bead cut at either end, plum then mint ink; light hairline seams, the alt one tinting its circle with the accent; dark the instrument's scale — hairline, ticks, the oxide at centre; brutalism a marquee strip, the second running the other way; titanium a milled groove with the film lying in it; retro a dithered band, then a plain two-line groove; solstice a hairline with an accent peak, the alt one on the opposite side; phantom torn paper of three clipped plates, the second mirrored; deco a double rule with a lozenge; sepia a double warm-brown rule, the second with a diamond; shade-light a blurred seam, felt rather than seen; shade-dark a swell in the next surface's own tone.

Divider shapes

Look at: the shape knob (data-kp-divider-shape, scope-93), each row a plain divider and the alt divider under one value. In every theme: zigzag a bar cut by small teeth of one size, scallop a bar edged with half circles, wave a smooth wave with no corner, cloud bumps of two sizes, pearls round beads on a thin thread, line a thin rule fading at both ends with three beads at its centre, none an empty step of room. No tooth, circle or bead is cut in half at either end, the plain divider is in the theme's primary ink and the alt one in its muted ink (in pastel its mint), the alt one faces up, and nothing is blurred. The last row names theme inside a wave: it shows the theme's own dividers from the Dividers block above, not a wave.

Dossier

Look at: the dossier card (data-kp-reveal="emphasis" with a trigger inside). The left copy is covered, waiting for its trigger; the right copy is the same file opened. In every theme: while covered the three redacted phrases are not readable but keep their place, so opening the file re-breaks no line; opened, they read at full contrast; the stamp reads the card's own data-kp-label ("Sealed") and never covers the title or the text; a theme that swaps the word shows data-kp-label-open ("Cleared") once open. In the live copy, press Open the file: the redactions must lift in one motion or on a short stagger, never a flicker, and a second press covers them again; Replay puts it back covered. Per theme: formal names its word, static, and clears left to right; nostromo's label tape swaps its word and the redactions clear together; grotesk's bars cut away in three steps; retro is a Notepad window with a read-only stamp and a dither brush lifting off; terminal a bracketed stamp with cells clearing; brutalism a tilted stamp with a pixel outline and bars sliding off; phantom a rotated red plate and censor plates shearing off; solstice a slightly rotated stamp and a boundary-coloured bar lifted right to left; lapis a seal over the redactions; deco jewel redactions; synthwave the stamp in the OSD face and noise that clears; blueprint solid ink blocks cleared left to right; forest and sepia a rotated stamp in the destructive ink; pastel a rotated stamp that swaps its word; dark a log with a redact bar; high-contrast staggered by 90 ms.

Surfaces

Look at: the hero surface (data-kp-surface="hero") and the app surface (data-kp-surface="app"), each in a stage tall enough for the texture to show. In every theme: the hero paints its own ground (the theme's hero colour) and the app paints the theme's background, so the two read as two grounds; every text on both — microlabel, heading, muted line, link, each button — clears its contrast floor on its own ground; and a theme's texture stays inside the surface it belongs to rather than bleeding over the stage around it. In every theme no text or button touches a surface's left or right edge: the package keeps one lg step of room inside the ground (--kp-surface-padding-inline). The third stage is the same hero with data-kp-surface-align="center", an option that is off unless a page writes it: the microlabel, heading and line centre, and the row of buttons sits in the middle. Per theme: cyberpunk's hero is the yellow ground and its app the void; synthwave's hero carries a striped, static sun and a floor that drifts without changing brightness; phantom's hero carries its plates; lapis confines its girih tile to the two surfaces and turns the page-wide texture off; forest and shade-light paint their own app ground; titanium's ground is carbon twill under a moving light.

Call to action and end side

Look at: the last link of each bar is the call to action (.kp-nav__link--cta). In every theme it is the one link that reads as an action rather than a place — a plate, a bracket, a frame — yet keeps the bar's height and its neighbours' baseline, and its hover and focus stay visible on its own plate. Per theme: formal a primary plate with the radius; terminal bracketed; brutalism lifts away from its shadow on hover; nostromo a plate that lights under the pointer. The second bar carries data-kp-nav-side="end", for a bar that sits on the end side of the screen: only cyberpunk answers it — its clipped strip mirrors, the notch moving from the top left to the top right and the cut corner from the bottom right to the bottom left. In every other theme the two bars must look identical.

Button readout

Look at: the first button carries a readout (.kp-button__readout, the word "READY"), the second does not. At rest, in every theme, the readout is invisible and the two buttons are exactly the same height: the readout is out of the flow and costs the control nothing. Hover or Tab to the first button: in dark and titanium the small reading appears above the control, in the theme's oxide, and goes again when the pointer leaves; in every other theme nothing appears at all. The readout is decoration — a screen reader hears "Send", never "READY Send".

Compact form

Look at: the same form twice; the right one sits under data-density="compact". In every theme the compact one is visibly tighter: the gap between the fields and the gap between a label and its input both come down, and so does the height of the inputs and the button — but a control never gets smaller than a comfortable pointer target (24 px is the floor; use the Devtools ruler), the labels stay the same readable size, and nothing overlaps.

Shortcut sheet at 360 px

Look at: the keyboard shortcut sheet (.kp-shortcuts) inside a window 360 px wide — a frame, because the sheet's width follows the window, not its container. In every theme the whole sheet, its border included, fits inside the frame: no horizontal scrollbar under it, no edge cut off, and the long meaning in the last row wraps inside its column rather than pushing the sheet wider. On a wide screen the same sheet is 490 px (Data › Shortcut sheet shows it there). The frame follows the theme of this page.

Plain table in 400 px

Look at: a plain .kp-table with data-kp-cards, in a container 400 px wide while the window stays wide. In every theme it falls into cards: the header row is gone from sight, each row is a card, and each cell is a line with its column's name (from data-label) before the value, told apart from the value by weight or colour. No cell runs past the container, and the table never scrolls sideways.

Theme picker focus ring

Look at: Tab into the row of theme buttons and walk it with Tab. In every theme the focused button shows a ring of two visible channels: a solid outline at least 2 px wide, standing at least 2 px off the button, and a second, contrasting colour with it, so the ring shows on a light and on a dark ground alike. On the button of the theme on screen, the selection's own border and check must not hide the ring. Pressing a button switches this page's theme.
