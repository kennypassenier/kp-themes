Variants

Look at: six variants that must read as one family at six weights. Primary (--primary) is the loudest thing on the page and there is one per view. The bare .kp-button is the everyday secondary look, and --secondary must match it. Ghost has no ground until touched; destructive is the only one allowed the alarm colour. Mirror exists in the registers only — the themes that answer it draw the flipped silhouette, the others must leave it looking like the bare button, never broken. A theme that cuts a notch or a bevel does it to every variant, not to one.

States

Look at: hover and press with the mouse, then walk the row with Tab. Every state must be visible without colour alone — a lift, an inset, a frame, a shift. The pressed state must differ from hover by more than a hair: the rule is at least ten steps of lightness, and the two themes that could not reach it were changed rather than excused. The focus ring must stay visible on the ground behind it in every theme, including the ones that paint their own elevation. Disabled must look unavailable and must not respond to hover at all.

aria-busy is the consumer's to set and the consumer's to clear; the package never leaves a button busy with no way back.

Sizes

Look at: three sizes on one baseline. The label must stay centred at every size, the padding must scale with the text rather than jump, and the small size must still be large enough to hit — 44 px is the floor on a touch screen. Use the ruler in Devtools to check the height of each.

Icons

Look at: the icon and the label must share a baseline, and the gap between them must not grow with the button. The icon-only button must have an accessible name — hover it and read the tooltip, then Tab to it and listen: a screen reader has to say something other than "button".

Extremes

Look at: two panes, both resizable by their bottom-right corner. In the first, narrow it until the long labels run out of room: they must wrap or ellipse inside the button, never push the button past the pane, and never be cut mid-word with no sign that something is missing. The second pane holds only the short buttons, so it narrows to a few characters (Kenny found the long labels stopped the first pane long before a one-character button was tested): the one-character button must stay square-ish rather than collapse to a sliver, and the button with no label at all is a fault to see, not to hide.

The keyboard accelerator

Look at: no theme draws the marked letter any more: retro, the one register that answered data-kp-key, left the package on 2026-10-09. In every theme the letter looks like the rest of the label and the button keeps its own hover. In every theme the label must read as one word — "Save", never "S ave".

In a row, and on a card

Look at: buttons side by side must line up on their text, not on their boxes, and the row must wrap rather than scroll when the pane narrows. On the card, the pair must sit against the card's ground and not against the page's — a theme that paints its card a different colour has to keep the button legible on it.

Delete with an undo

Look at: a destructive button that acts at once and offers the way back instead of asking first. Each Delete carries data-kp-undo-action: pressed, its row (the nearest data-kp-undo-target, else the table row, else the parent) goes at once and a toast says so with Undo on it, in the theme's ghost button. Press Undo and the row is back where it was and kp-action-undo fires; wait out the window and kp-action-commit fires, the moment an app sends the delete to its server. The first row's window is the default six seconds, the second's data-kp-undo-ms="12000", with its own words (data-kp-undo-text, data-kp-undo-label). The line under the stage logs both events. data-kp-undo on each button is what satisfies the destructive contract without a confirmation; a destructive button with neither is disabled and reported. The second stage is the React Button with onUndo, frozen while it offers the undo: .kp-button__undo sits beside the button on its baseline with a small gap, holding a ghost Undo. Confirm by pressing twice

Look at: data-kp-confirm-mode="inline", the variant of the confirmation dialog. The first press opens nothing: it arms the button, and its label becomes the phrase in data-kp-confirm; the second press acts. The label is the only change the package makes (it also sets data-kp-armed for a page that wants more), so the phrase must still fit the button on one line, close enough to the old width that the second press lands on it. Wait a few seconds, or Tab away, and it disarms back to Remove reading. The second button sets its own window with data-kp-confirm-ms="8000".

A button that points back

Look at: data-kp-direction="back" says a button goes backwards — the data table's pager writes it on its Previous by itself, and an app may write it on its own Back. It is meaning, not look: only cyberpunk answers it, by turning the button's cut corner to the side it points to, so its Previous and Next mirror each other. In every other theme the two buttons must look exactly alike.
