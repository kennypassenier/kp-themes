Media and bands

Pictures and the rows that sit around them: .kp-media in its four ratios with .kp-media-figure for a caption, the movable dashboard .kp-grid in its .kp-grid-wrap, the running band .kp-marquee, and the two quiet rows from the concept demo, .kp-laurels and .kp-platforms. Every picture here is an inline SVG in a data URI, so the page makes no request of its own.

Ratios

Look at: one landscape picture in four frames: the default 16:9, --wide 21:9, --square and --portrait 3:4. Each frame keeps its own shape and the picture covers it, so the square and the portrait crop the sides of the pump house rather than squash it — a stretched roof is the fault. The fifth frame is the portrait again with the --kp-media-fit: contain knob: the whole picture shows, and the bands above and below it are the frame's muted ground, which must be visibly part of the frame and not a gap in the page. Each theme may give the frame an edge; it must be the same edge on all five.

Before the picture arrives

Look at: the frames with no picture in them, which is every frame for the first moments of a slow page. Each must already be its final shape on the muted ground, so the text under it does not jump when the picture lands — the sentence under the square must sit exactly as far down as it would with a picture. An empty frame must read as a place for something, not as a grey button; in a theme whose muted ground is close to its page, check the frame is still there at all.

Figure and overlay

Look at: the caption under a figure sits close to its picture, smaller and muted, and belongs to it rather than to the next paragraph. The overlay text lies on a dark gradient at the bottom of the picture: it is white on that gradient in every theme on purpose, and it must stay legible over the pale sky of this picture too. The second overlay is a long sentence in a narrow frame: the gradient must grow with the text, and no line may run over the pale part of the picture without a ground under it.

Grid

Look at: six tiles on a six-column grid, each on the card surface with a boundary. Tab to a tile: its focus ring must sit just outside its boundary and not be cut by the tile next to it. Press the arrows to move it and Shift with the arrows to resize it; the neighbours stay where they are, and a tile can never leave the grid. The Alarms tile is two rows tall; its text must stay centred in the whole tile, not in its first row. A tile holding more text than fits must clip inside its boundary, not spill onto the tile below.

Grid in a narrow box

Marquee

Look at: the first band runs: its items pass right to left without a jump where the row repeats, at the theme's own speed, and a screen reader hears each item once — the moving copy is hidden from it. With reduced motion asked for in the system, the band must stand still. Scroll it out of view and back: it resumes where it was. The second band has no data-kp-marquee, which is the no-script answer: the same items laid out as a still row that wraps. The third holds long items; in the running band a long item must stay on one line, and in the still row it must wrap.

Laurels and platforms

Look at: laurels are a quiet row of claims: each value bold in the page's ink on its own line, its source muted under it, the items wrapping as a row rather than stacking into a list. The platforms line is smaller and muted still, a row of names with a wide gap and nothing between them. Both are quiet by default and a register may dramatise them; whatever it does, the bold value must stay the loudest thing in each laurel. A theme that draws a box around each laurel or platform (nostromo boxes the laurels) keeps room between the words and the box’s edges. In the narrow pane both must wrap without an item splitting across two columns.
