Overlays

What floats over the page: .kp-dialog (and the .kp-confirm a destructive button opens), .kp-popover holding a .kp-menu, a .kp-menu-button with its rich menu, a .kp-drawer and the tour's .kp-tour, and .kp-tooltip. Everything that opens is shown open on the page, pinned inside its stage so it can be compared across themes, and each has a live trigger that opens the real thing.

Dialog

Look at: the open dialog is on the popover surface with a boundary, and must stand apart from the page behind it by that boundary alone — the frozen copy has no backdrop, so this is the honest test of the frame. Title, text and the two buttons keep one left edge; the buttons sit at the end. Now press Open the dialog: the live one is modal, so the page behind must dim (by darkening, not by tinting it with the theme's colour), focus must move inside, Tab must stay inside, and Escape must close it and put focus back on the button you pressed.

Dialog with description and close

Look at: the description sits under the title in the muted ink at a smaller size and must still pass contrast on the popover surface. The close button is pinned in the top corner: it must not overlap the title — a long title has to stop at least half a rem short of it, in every theme — and it must be a target you can hit. The dialog holds a form: its fields must take the dialog's width, not the page's, and the dialog must never be wider than the window minus a margin; narrow the window to check.

Confirmation

Look at: .kp-confirm is the dialog js/components.js opens in front of a destructive button. It is narrower than a dialog — the width of a question — with the question as its title, the fixed warning under it, and two answers: Cancel in the ordinary weight and Yes, do it in the alarm colour. Press the live button: focus must land on Cancel, not on the destructive answer, so a stray Enter does nothing harmful; Escape must not close it. The variant without a dialog, data-kp-confirm-mode="inline", is on the Buttons page under Confirm by pressing twice.

A dialog with sixty rows

Look at: press Open sixty readings. The modal dialog is taller than any window, so it stops a margin short of the window's edges and scrolls its .kp-dialog__body inside itself: the title stays at the top and the buttons stay at the bottom while the rows move between them, and the page behind must not scroll along. The frozen copy below is not modal and takes the same cap (--kp-dialog-max-height, the window minus 2rem), so it scrolls its body the same way; the stage around it still scrolls so it does not cover the rest of this page, and that scrollbar belongs to the catalogue, not to the dialog. While a body is taller than its box, js/overlays.js keeps data-kp-popover-overflowing on it with --kp-scroll-view, --kp-scroll-ratio and --kp-scroll-progress beside it; the short dialog in the last stage has no such state. Nothing is drawn from them except in retro, which draws its own 1995 scrollbar on every dialog body: on the long one the arrows are ink and a thumb sits in the track and moves as you scroll (press an arrow, the track or drag the thumb: it scrolls), on the short one the bar is disabled — grey embossed arrows and no thumb. In every other theme the two bodies differ only in length.

Popover and menu

Look at: the menu is a .kp-menu inside a .kp-popover: the popover surface, a strong boundary, and items with a small inset. Hover each item: the highlight is a soft wash of the ink and must span the item's whole row, not just its word. The separator is a hairline; the destructive item is the only one in the alarm colour. Press Row actions on the live copy: the menu must open against the button, close on Escape or a click outside, and return focus to the button.

Menu extremes

Look at: a disabled item, an item whose label is a whole sentence, and a menu with twenty-four items. The disabled item is faded and out of the pointer's reach, so it must not light up on hover in any theme. The long label must wrap inside the popover, never push it wider than the window. The long menu must stop at the popover's maximum height and scroll inside itself, with the items keeping their height rather than squashing to fit — the fault this rule was written for was a second column appearing beside the first.

A menu of every other action

Look at: .kp-menu-button with a .kp-menu--rich (js/menu-button.js), on the package's own .kp-menu: open More ▾. The groups have headings in small muted capitals, every label and every hint start at one edge, and Run a pressure test is grey: it keeps its hint, and says why it cannot run on a third line in italics. Press ↓ on the button: the menu opens on the first entry; ↓ ↑ wrap, Home and End go to the ends, r jumps from Restart to Run. The focus stops on the grey entry and Enter does nothing there. Esc closes and puts the focus back on the button, and the line under the menus shows the page's own Escape listener heard nothing; Tab closes and moves on. Press Refill in 3 s, open a menu and wait: nothing moves until it closes. Loading shows one grey entry at the height of a real one; Twenty-four actions scrolls inside the menu with the focus kept in view; No actions greys the wide button (its title says why) and takes the phone one away (data-kp-menu-empty="hide"). The menu of one action has no heading. In the phone pane the open menu spans the header's buttons, inside the pane.

Help in a drawer

Look at: .kp-drawer: a head (the title, the close button, one sentence), a body that scrolls, and a foot that stays. The frozen copy is the drawer as a phone shows it, in a pane 360 px wide: scroll its body and the head and the foot stay. Its cards are .kp-helps, each a .kp-help__list of words beside their meaning, the meanings starting at one edge across every card; in a card of 22rem or less the word goes over its meaning. Press Open Help: the real one is a dialog.kp-drawer, which comes in from the end edge at the full height of the window, with its foot at the bottom; Esc or ✕ closes it and the focus goes back to the button. Its Take the 1-minute tour closes it and starts the tour of the next block; when the tour ends the focus comes back to Open Help.

Help, and a short tour

Look at: the tour's card, .kp-tour, frozen beside the part it talks about: that part has a ring in the focus colour and the rest of the stage is dimmed around it. Press Start the tour (js/tour.js, startTour()): the first card says 1 of 5, because the fourth of the six steps points at a map this page does not have, and the count leaves it out. The card sits 12 px under its part, or over it in the lower half of the window, never on it, and 16 px inside the window; scroll and it follows. Back, Skip and Next (Done on the last), ← and → on the card, and Esc anywhere work; when it ends, the focus goes back to the button that started it, and the line under the buttons says it was remembered (js/remember.js, under kp-remember:tour:catalogue:done). Start the tour on the phone runs it over the phone pane, its tab bar included. Help ? in the little app opens the drawer of the block above.

Tooltip

Look at: a tooltip is a .kp-popover with .kp-tooltip on it: the same surface and boundary as a menu, smaller text and less padding. It describes; it never holds the only copy of anything, so each trigger here has its own visible label. The short tooltip must hug its text; the long one must stop at its maximum width and wrap into a short paragraph, not stretch into one line across the page. The first two are shown open and held open for the eye; the two under them are live: js/overlays.js opens a tooltip after a moment under the pointer and at once on focus, closes it when the pointer or focus leaves or on Escape, and points the trigger's aria-describedby at it.
