Switch (concept)

A switch is a checkbox that says on and off instead of ticked: the real control is an <input type="checkbox" role="switch">, so it submits with a form, reads its state from checked, and needs no script. This page is a concept (gap-11, Kenny's answer of 2026-09-13: "with the switch"): its styles live on this page under .cat-switch-concept, on the package's tokens, and nothing here is in css/components.css yet. The package version waits for your look at it in every theme.

States

Look at: off, on, and both of them disabled. Off is a muted track with the thumb at the start; on fills the track with the primary colour and moves the thumb to the end — the position says the state as well as the colour, and the word beside the track says it a third time. Click the label, not only the track: the whole row is the target. Tab to each: the focus ring is the package's own and must be visible around the track on the page's ground. Disabled fades and refuses the pointer, and a disabled switch that is on must still read as on.

role="switch" on a checkbox is what makes a screen reader say "switch, on" rather than "checkbox, checked". The word beside the track is aria-hidden, because the role already announces the state.

Invalid

Look at: a switch that must be on before the form goes through. It takes the same outline an invalid checkbox takes, in the alarm colour, and the message under it says what is wrong in words. Turn it on: nothing here clears the message, because the concept has no script — in a form, js/forms.js would.
Lock the line out before you start the repair.

A long label

Look at: a label of sixty characters. It wraps beside the track, every line starting at the same inset, and the track stays whole at its own size on the first line rather than shrinking or dropping under the text. Drag the pane narrow to see it wrap.

Keyboard

Look at: Tab lands on the switch, once per switch. Space flips it, as it ticks a checkbox — that is the browser's own behaviour, and the reason the switch is a checkbox rather than a button with a script. Enter does not flip it; inside a form it submits, as it does on a checkbox. The disabled switch is skipped by Tab. Shift+Tab walks back the same way.
