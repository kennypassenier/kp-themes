Structure

The components that arrange content rather than hold it: .kp-accordion, .kp-tree, .kp-timeline, .kp-split, .kp-reorder and .kp-wizard. Each is trivial with a mouse and is where keyboard access usually stops, so most of the notes below ask for the keyboard.

Accordion

Look at: each item is a native <details>: the summary is bold, the body is in the muted ink, and a hairline separates the items. The first item is open. The open and closed marker is the browser's own triangle, and every register now answers the summary in its own voice — the marker's colour, the summary's type, and a mark on the open one — so switch themes and judge whether each still reads as a disclosure, with the triangle visible on the summary's baseline in the dark themes. Press Enter or Space on a summary to toggle it. The third summary is a whole question: it must wrap with its marker staying at the start of the first line.
Who may reopen a locked-out line?
Only the shift lead who signed the lock-out, or the site manager when that person has left the site.
What counts as a pressure drop?
A fall of more than one bar within thirty minutes on any line.
If the gauge on the wall and the sensor disagree by more than half a bar, which one do we believe until the vendor has been out?

Neither, on its own. Close the line and log both readings:

  • the gauge, read at eye level;
  • the sensor, from the readings page, at the same minute.

Tree

Look at: a selectable tree (data-kp-selectable). The twisty before each branch points the way the branch will go and sits in the muted ink; leaves have none and must still line up with the branch labels. Line 2 is open, Line 1 is closed, and readings-0240.csv is written as selected (aria-selected="true"): it must carry a bar at its start edge, a wash of the primary colour over its own row only, and a heavier weight, in every theme. Click once into the tree and use the keyboard: Down skips the closed branch's children, Right opens a branch and then steps into it, Left closes it and then steps out, Home and End jump, and typing "pu" finds pump-log.txt. Enter selects the focused item. The focus ring must hug one row, not the whole branch under it.

Tree extremes

Look at: six levels deep, in a narrow pane. Each level must step in by the same amount; by the sixth the label still needs room to read. The deepest file has a name with no space to break at: it wraps inside the pane (the package breaks it anywhere rather than let it push the pane wider). The branch with a long name must wrap under its own twisty, not under the twisty of its parent.

Timeline

Look at: each event has a ring in the primary colour and a hairline running down to the next ring — and the last event must have no line hanging under it. The time sits above the text in tabular figures, muted and smaller, so a column of times lines up digit for digit. The fourth event's text is a long paragraph: it must wrap in its own column, never under the ring. The second timeline has one event, and must show a ring with no line at all.

Split pane

Look at: two panes and a separator you can grab. The separator is a narrow bar on the muted ground with a rule on each side; the pointer turns into a column-resize arrow over it. Drag it, then Tab to it and use Left and Right (Shift for big steps, Home and End for the limits of 20 % and 80 %): the focus ring must be drawn inside the bar so the panes do not cover it. Each pane scrolls on its own when its content is taller. The second split is data-kp-orientation="horizontal" — a top and bottom split: the panes stack, the separator is a bar across the full width with a rule above and below it and a row-resize pointer, and Up and Down (or a drag) move it. Registers that give the separator a thickness keep that thickness here.

Reorder

Look at: each row is on the card surface with a boundary, and its handle is a real button with a boundary of its own and a grab pointer. Tab to a handle and press Down: the row moves, focus stays on the same handle, and a screen reader hears "Pressure moved to position 2 of 5". Drag a handle with the mouse: the row you hold (data-kp-dragging) turns its frame to the primary colour, dashed, and lifts off the list with a shadow; letting go takes all of it away. Drag Time's handle all the way to the bottom in one movement: the row must follow the pointer past every row it crosses, while the button is still held, not stop after one; the screen reader hears "Time moved to position 5 of 5", and the order is the one four presses of Down would have left. The fourth row's label is long: it must wrap beside the handle, with the handle staying at the top of the row.

Wizard, frozen on step 3 of 4

Look at: the step list as js/wizard.js writes it on the third step, not attached. Done steps show a check in the success colour inside their circle, the current step a filled circle in the primary colour with a bold label and aria-current="step", and the step still to come a plain numbered circle in the muted ink. The three states must be told apart without colour: check, fill, and outline. The second list has seven steps with long names in a narrow pane: it must wrap onto rows with each circle staying beside its own label. In every theme that draws a frame around the wizard, nothing inside may touch it: the step list, the step and the buttons keep at least 0.75rem from the frame on all four sides (--kp-wizard-padding, 1rem by default).

Wizard, live

Look at: the wizard exactly as the package documents it, with no form around it. Press Next with the reference empty: the wizard must refuse, keep the step list where it is, put focus on the field, and say why under it — the wizard writes the message itself when no form[data-kp-form] is around it. Type into the field and the message goes. Fill it in and press Next: the second label turns current, the first gets its check, focus moves to the new step, and the status line announces "Step 2 of 3". Back on the first step must be unavailable, and on the last step the Next button must read Finish. The same room inside the frame as the frozen wizard, on every step.
