Feedback

Alerts

Look at: five alerts — the plain one and the four flavours. Each flavour is a full plate in its status colour, and the text on it must pass contrast on that plate, not on the page: check the warning one in particular, where yellow grounds tempt a theme into pale ink. Each flavour also names itself in words ("Warning: ") so the plates do not rely on colour. The icon sits on the first line of the text, not centred on the block, and the close button sits at the far end at a size you can hit. An alert has no hover state: pointing at one must change nothing.
The export schedule changes to 02:00 from Monday.

✓ Success: The incident was closed and the vendor was told.

! Warning: The service contract for pump house 4 ends in 12 days.

i Info: Readings are delayed by up to five minutes tonight.

✕ Error: The alarm panel stopped reporting at 03:12.

The close buttons work without a framework: js/overlays.js hides the alert after a cancelable kp-alert-dismiss event, and a toast's close button removes its toast. Reload the page to bring them back.

A long alert

Look at: four sentences in a warning. The text wraps into a comfortable block, and every wrapped line starts at the same inset as the first — never back under the icon. The icon stays at the top beside the first line, and the close button stays at the top of its column rather than drifting to the vertical middle. Narrow the pane: the text column shrinks while the icon and the close button keep their size. Read it in the theme with the loudest warning plate; if you have to squint by the third line, the ink on the plate is too weak for body text.
! Warning: The nightly export of the pressure telemetry for pump house 4 did not finish. It stopped at 03:12, after 41 of the 60 files had been written. The files that were written are complete and can be used as they are. Run the export again from the admin page to write the remaining 19, or ask the on-call engineer to do it before the day shift starts.

Toasts

Look at: the stack below is a real .kp-toasts region, pinned to the corner of this stage instead of the window so it can be reviewed in place. Five toasts: the plain one on the popover surface, and four with a meaning — success, info, warning and destructive — each on the same plate and ink as the severity badge and the alert of that meaning, with its words saying the meaning as well; every theme restates the four, so no toast of meaning may look like the plain one. The words and the buttons on each plate must pass contrast on it. The toasts float on the popover surface with a boundary that must stay visible over anything behind them. The words sit in .kp-toast__body; the action button and the close button sit at the toast's right end, level with the words, in every toast and whatever the length of its text — so the buttons of the five line up down the stack. A close button that drops below the text or hugs it with no gap is a fault. Hover each button: its ground turns a shade of that toast's own plate (green on the success toast, red on the error toast, the popover colour on the plain one), never the theme's page hover, and its word still reads on it. Then press the buttons to raise real toasts in the window's corner: they must stack upwards from the bottom, above any texture or scanline the theme paints, and leave after five seconds.
Saved. The handover note is visible to the day shift.
Success: incident INC-4471 closed.
Info: 3 new readings since you opened this page.
Warning: the vendor has not answered in 2 hours.
Error: the note was not saved; you are offline.

Badges

Look at: the plain badge is a muted pill; the seven status badges take their plate from data-status, and each plate's text must pass contrast on that plate in every theme — draft and withdrawn are the two that drift towards grey-on-grey. The second row is severity: .kp-badge--success, --info, --warning and --destructive, on the same four token pairs an alert of that severity wears, so a badge and an alert that mean the same thing are one colour; each word must pass contrast on its plate in every theme. All of them share one height and one baseline with the text around them, so a row reads as a sentence with pills in it, not a staircase.
Plain Draft Sent Screening Interview Offer Rejected Withdrawn
Healthy Scheduled Degraded Down

In running text: incident INC-4471 is Sent to the vendor and the quote is Draft.

A long badge

Look at: a badge holding 40 characters, and a badge holding an identifier with no spaces. By default the badge is allowed to break anywhere, so in a narrow pane it wraps — the pill must grow taller with its rounded ends intact (in nostromo a pill too, since 2026-09-13), not clip the text and not leave the text hanging outside the pill. The third copy sits under --kp-badge-wrap: normal, the knob for a column of labels: it breaks only between words. Drag the pane narrow and compare the three.

Tags

Look at: a .kp-tag-list of .kp-tag pills. A tag differs from a badge by its hairline border and its remove button; side by side, nobody should mistake one for the other. Hover a remove button: it fills with the alarm colour, and the × on it must stay visible. The remove buttons have names ("Remove Night shift") — a row of identical × is useless to a screen reader. The tag holding a sentence must wrap inside its pill; the list must wrap onto new lines rather than scroll.

Progress

Look at: four native <progress> bars painted by the theme: empty, a third, full, and indeterminate (no value). The track must be the theme's muted ground with a hairline, and the fill the theme's accent — never the browser's own grey track or blue fill, which is the fault that shows first in a dark theme. The empty bar must still show its track, so an empty bar is not an invisible one. The indeterminate bar wears diagonal stripes in the accent on the muted track, so it can never be mistaken for the full one above it — "done" and "no idea yet" must look different in every browser. Without a reduced-motion preference the stripes drift slowly; with one they stand still and still read as stripes. The percentage beside the second bar sits on its centre line, in the muted ink on the page’s ground, and must read at 4.5:1 — it is a label, not a plate. The filled part of each bar follows the shape of its track: square in a square track, rounded in a rounded one. Under them stands a group of three labelled bars: their tracks must all begin in the same column and end in the same column, whatever the label in front of them is called, and the readings must line up on their last digit. A bar that starts where the word before it happened to end is the fault this group exists to prevent [fix-64].

Spinner

Look at: the spinner is a ring with one bright quarter turning over a track in the same hue at a quarter strength, so it turns visibly even in a theme where primary is black. It must be round, not an ellipse, at every size, and it must not wobble. Switch the system to reduced motion: the spinner stops, and a stopped spinner must still read as "busy" from the ring's shape. Beside a label, the spinner sits on the text's centre line. The large one sets --kp-spinner-size, the knob the React component writes.

Skeleton

Look at: a skeleton comes in the shape of what it stands in for: three lines of a paragraph, a circle for an avatar, a block for an image. It pulses gently — slowly enough that it never approaches a flash, and not at all under reduced motion. The pulse must be visible on the page's ground and on a card's; in a theme where the muted colour equals the ground, a skeleton is an invisible one. Consecutive lines keep a small gap between them.

Empty state

Look at: .kp-empty is what a list, a table or a search result shows when it has nothing in it — the component on its own, before any list around it. There are two here, and they must not say the same thing. Nothing yet is a list that has never had an item: it says so and offers to create the first one. Nothing found is a search that matched nothing: it offers to clear the search, because offering "create" to someone who just typed a filter is noise. In every theme both sit in a frame, centred, the title in full ink and the body muted but readable, the action a real button; narrow the pane and the text wraps inside the frame.
