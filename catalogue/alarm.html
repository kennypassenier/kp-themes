Alarm

A full-screen dramatic alert, bigger than a toast: a code line, one huge word, the reason under it and one way out, over a plate that takes the whole window and blocks the page behind it. It is the package's .kp-alarm since your decision on 2026-09-15 (scope-94, "Overal vol drama" and "Blokkerend, zoals de demo"), built from the prototype in research/alarm/: the full drama in all twenty-two themes, each in its own colours and faces, and both ways out. showAlarm() raises it from a script, a data-kp-alarm button raises it from markup, and <Alarm> or useAlarm() raise it in React. Each block shows one alarm held open in a frame, and a button that raises the real one.

Must be acknowledged

Look at: the frame first. It is the alarm held open and still, as a reader who asked for reduced motion sees it: the plate, the frame, the hazard bars, the code line in the theme's mono face, the headline in its display face, the reason under it and one button. It must read as this theme's alarm, in its own colours, and still as an alarm rather than a dialog. The headline must read at a glance and the button must be the only thing that looks pressable. Then press Raise the alarm: the plate fades in, the headline flickers in and each letter decodes through two noise glyphs, a split copy slices through it, the glow breathes and the bars march. Only the button closes it: a click, Enter or Space. Escape does nothing, a click on the plate does nothing, and waiting does nothing. The page behind cannot be clicked and Tab stays inside. When it closes, focus is back on the button you pressed. Raise it, Escape allowed is the same alarm with escape on.

Closes by itself

Look at: the frame holds the countdown at five of eight seconds left. The bar shrinks towards the start and must read as time running out, not as progress; the words under it count whole seconds. Keep open is a quiet button, because the point of this mode is that nobody has to touch it. Then press Raise it for 6 seconds: it blocks the page exactly like the acknowledged one, the bar empties and the alarm goes away by itself, with focus back on the button. Raise it again and press Escape: it closes. Raise it a third time and press Tab once: focus lands on Keep open, and pressing it stops the clock, brings the Acknowledge button back and gives it focus; from then on only that button closes it. With reduced motion asked for, the bar steps a whole second at a time instead of sliding.
