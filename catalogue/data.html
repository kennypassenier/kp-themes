Showing data

The small patterns every data-heavy page rewrites badly: a status (.kp-health), a spec sheet (.kp-spec), a diff (.kp-diff), a value with a copy button (.kp-copyable), identifiers and addresses (.kp-id, .kp-url), masked values, timestamps and truncation, the keyboard shortcut sheet (.kp-shortcuts), and the table column that gives way first (.kp-col-low). The extremes are the ones that push pages sideways: a 90-character URL, a 70-character id, and a diff of forty lines. At the end, three a dashboard needs: tiles of one height (.kp-tiles), key figures (.kp-kpis) with their meters, column counts and 24-hour trends, and a state word that keeps its width (.kp-state-word).

Health

Look at: every status is a word and a dot, and the word is what carries it — cover the dots with a finger and the row must still read. The dot takes the success, warning and alarm colours and has a boundary, so a yellow dot on a pale card is still a dot. The fourth has no data-state at all: its dot must look neutral, not like a fourth colour. In the narrow pane the long status must wrap beside its dot, with the dot staying on the first line.

Log

Look at: four columns that do not move — time, source, level, message — so every message begins in the same place whatever the source before it is called; cover the first three with a finger and the left edge of the text must be straight. The level is a word before it is a colour: read the list with the colours ignored and it must still say which line failed. Each source name keeps its own hue out of the theme's five chart colours, and the same name takes the same hue in every theme and in the terminal — media is the third chart colour in both. The error line takes the destructive ink for its level and its message, the warning line the warning ink, and a debug line is quieter than the rest. The pointed-at line wears the muted ground and no second colour. In the narrow pane a long message wraps under itself, inside its own column, and the three columns in front of it stay put.

Diff of forty lines

Look at: three columns — line number, sign, text — and the sign is the carrier that survives without colour: cover the tints and every changed line must still be findable by its + or -. Added lines take a success tint and removed lines an alarm tint, both faint enough that the text on them still passes contrast; in a dark theme they must stay tints, not slabs. Line numbers are right-aligned in tabular figures. Blank lines keep their height. Narrow the window until the added reopen_by line is too long for the box: it must wrap inside the text column with its number and sign on the first line only, and the diff must not scroll the page sideways.

Copyable value

Look at: the value is in the mono face with its button beside it on one baseline. Press the first Copy: the button's own word must change to Copied and back after a moment, on the success colour — the word is the confirmation, the colour only the second carrier. Every register restates that plate over its own button rule, so it must hold in every theme, hovered or not. The second row is frozen in that confirmed state so it can be compared across themes. The third is frozen after a refused copy (data-kp-copy-failed): the package announces the refusal in a toast and marks the button too — the destructive ink and a dashed frame — while the word stays "Copy", because pressing it again is the way out. Compare it with the idle button above it. The last value is a 70-character key in a narrow pane: it must wrap, and its button must stay inside the pane.

Ids and URLs

Look at: .kp-id is in the mono face at nine-tenths of the text around it; .kp-url keeps the body face. Both have nowhere to break, so both are allowed to break anywhere: in the narrow pane the 90-character URL and the 70-character id must wrap at the pane's edge — ragged, but inside. The same values inside a sentence must wrap with the sentence rather than leaving a gap before them on the line above. Nothing on this block may push the page sideways at 320 px.

Shortcut sheet

Look at: the sheet is a dialog on the card surface with a strong boundary. Group labels are small capitals in the muted ink; each row has its key on the left in a fixed column and its meaning muted on the right, so the meanings start on one line. The last row's meaning is long and must wrap inside its column, not under the key. The frozen copy is not modal. Press Show the shortcuts, or press ? anywhere outside a text field: the live sheet opens modal, and Escape closes it.

Low-priority column

Look at: the Updated column is .kp-col-low, header and cells together. In the wide table it is there, muted like any timestamp. The same table sits in a pane narrower than 40rem, where the column must be gone — header and cells, never one without the other. Drag that pane wider than 40rem and the column must come back. The width that decides is the table's own box, not the window's, so the two tables disagree on one page and both are right.

Numbers that count up

Look at: data-kp-count on an element that holds the final number. The module reads it, counts from 0 to it and writes the same string back, so every number must end exactly as written — the same grouping, the same decimals, the € and the m³ untouched. The page's lang decides which mark groups and which is the decimal point: the Dutch line counts to 1.118.204,75, the English one to 1,204.50. The fourth sets the two knobs inline — --kp-count: 2400 (milliseconds) and --kp-count-from: 1000 — so it starts at 1000 and takes longer; the fifth, --kp-count: 0, never counts. Press Count again to watch the live copy: the figures must not shift sideways while they count (tabular figures), and nothing around them may move. While it counts the element carries data-kp-count-state="running", then done, and a kp-count event reports the value; the line under the stage shows the last one. With reduced motion asked for, every number is simply there.

Card with a header, actions and a footer

Look at: the card's slots as components/card.jsx renders them and a server writes them. The .kp-card__header is a row: the title at its start, .kp-card__actions at its end with a small gap between the buttons, and the two on one baseline — not the buttons' boxes centred on the title's box. The body under it is in the muted ink, the footer is set off by a hairline. In the narrow copy the actions must wrap under the title or stay beside it, never push the card wider than its pane. The buttons must read on the card's ground in every theme that paints the card apart from the page.

Tiles of one height

Look at: .kp-tiles lays the package's own .kp-card out in a grid, and every tile is as tall as the tallest tile of the whole grid, on every row: the short second row is as tall as the first, whose second card has the longest text. Each card's footer keeps to its bottom edge, so the footer lines (Read … ago and Open) of a row sit at one height however long the text above them is, and the body never touches the footer's rule in the tallest card. A tile's title is a step smaller than a page card's. Narrow the pane: the tiles go to fewer columns and then one, and stay one height.

A meter with a mark

Look at: .kp-meter, a used-of-total bar that works anywhere: in a table cell, at the bottom of a key figure (.kp-kpi__meter), at the start of a line of words (.kp-meter--inline). --kp-value is the share and --kp-mark an optional tick across the bar (a target, a limit, what is booked), which sticks out a little above and below without making the meter any taller. A share past the end fills the bar and puts a small ▸ at its end (data-kp-over); a mark past the end stops just short of the end with the ▸ after it. The red tile's meter is red, the warning row's amber. The third column is what a screen reader hears: the real share, past 100 % too. Press Book more: the capacity tile's mark moves past the end and its words say 130 %, through setMeter(). Loading pulses the tracks (still under reduced motion), with no fill and no mark. The phone pane shows the same tiles two to a row.

Key figures that never leave one alone

If that leaves one tile alone on the last row, it steps down to a count that does not; only when none exists (seven at 700 px, five at phone width) does the last tile span the whole row. Every tile in a row has the same height. Pick a number of tiles and a strip width: the line under the buttons says what attachKpiStrips() chose. Before the script runs, a strip keeps the package's auto-fit. The table under the phone pane is the spec's own list of cases.

A key figure with its 24-hour trend

Look at: .kp-kpi--trend, a key figure whose whole tile opens its chart (a stretched .kp-kpi__link, ↗ in the corner), with the 24-hour trend inside it as a second stop for the keyboard. The note avg 15 min sits in the label, in its capitals (.kp-kpi__label-note). The trend is the time chart's spark variant without its head line (data-kp-spark-head="none"), with the axis under it (data-kp-spark-axis="relative"): where it starts, as 14:40 yesterday, and now. Move the pointer over a trend: a crosshair and a chip with the moment and the value, as 04/10/2026 02:40 · 3.40 bar, kept inside the tile. Tab to a tile (the whole tile gets the ring), Tab again to its trend, then ← → (Shift: ten points), Home, End and Esc. A click on the trend opens the tile's link; a drag only reads it. Flow into the network stopped forty minutes ago, so its axis ends at that clock, not now; Reservoir South began this morning, so it says today. The second strip shows a tile loading, one with no trend and one with a single reading, each as tall as the filled one. In the phone pane the tiles are narrow: the value goes under the moment, and the corner shows only ↗. Ten minutes later is a live update: a reading on screen stays at its moment.

A state word that keeps its width

Look at: .kp-state-word is as wide as the widest word it can show, listed in data-kp-words, so the button beside it never moves when the state changes: a second click without moving the mouse hits the same button. Press Change the states: in the first list (plain words) the Stop and Start buttons jump left and right with each word; in the second (.kp-state-word, changed through setStateWord()) they stand still while the words change in front of them. The last line is centred (.kp-state-word--center): the word stays in the middle of its own width.

A month of nightly backups

Look at: .kp-calendar, a month as a grid of days, each a plate in the colour of how its night went, with how many of the nine services were backed up under the day's number. Green nights had every service, amber some, red none (17/09, the night of the power cut: only the red plate, its count reads like every other day's); the dashed days are still to come or from before the first backup (10/08). The days of September and November show their numbers, quiet and inert, so every week reads full. Today, 04/10, has a dark inner ring (today is the day in Brussels); the picked day an outer ring, and the detail beside it lists every service's own night. The grid is always six weeks tall: press ‹ Prev twice and back, and it keeps its height in every month. One tab stop: the arrows move a day or a week, Home and End go to the week's ends, Page Up and Page Down change the month (with Shift, the year), Enter picks. Try the states above the stage: Loading pulses every day in place, Nothing to check and Could not read stop the pulse and say why (the error after a red dot), and The late copies arrive fills in today's two missing services without moving the focus or the pick. In the phone pane the detail goes under the grid. The sample's clock stands at 04/10/2026 14:40, so the block reads the same on every day it is opened.
