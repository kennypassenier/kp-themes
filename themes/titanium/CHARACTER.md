# Titanium — character

The reference for how titanium looks and moves. Read the grammar before adding
or changing any titanium component; the inventory, families and outliers below
it record where the decided picks (2026-10-05 … 2026-10-06) stand against that
grammar, and the numbered proposal (proposal-1 …) is the plan to bring them in line.

Sources measured (2026-10-06): every `research/character-*/decided.json` pick
for titanium resolved to its CSS (shape, loading, arrival, tone, interaction,
live, phone …), `research/character-meter` (README, picks 1/1/3/3/3),
`css/titanium-register.css` (2632 lines), `css/themes.css` (titanium tokens),
`js/motion.js` (`themeMotion()`, reverse-close opening), `js/effects.js`
(TIMINGS), `research/THEME_PROFILES.md`. Every motion claim quotes its selector
or keyframe and its values. `research/titanium-loading` did not exist yet.

## 1. The titanium grammar

The theme is a **material**, not a place: bare machined titanium over a carbon
twill, finished with an anodised oxide film. Everything that happens to it is
something a machine shop does to titanium: it is **cut, milled, indexed,
stamped, heat-tinted**. Nothing breathes, bounces, swells or spins.

| #   | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1  | **Metal does not ease.** Every motion is `linear`; a discrete count may index with `steps()`; no `ease`, `ease-in/out`, `cubic-bezier` and never an overshoot (no value past 1 in a bezier, no keyframe past the rest pose).                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| G2  | **The tool feeds from inline-start to inline-end.** Arrivals, loading passes and heat-tint sweeps travel start→end in logical terms (`inset-inline-start`, `clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0)` mirrored by `dir`), never end→start and never physical `left`/`translateX` alone.                                                                                                                                               |
| G3  | **What drops from a trigger is cut top-down.** Menus, dialogs and popovers open by a `clip-path` cut from the top edge (the dialog's `kp-sig-titanium-ti-cut`) and close as that cut reversed; what lives at an edge (drawer, toast) slides in from its own edge, linear.                                                                                                                                                 |
| G4  | **Durations come from three bands.** Contact (hover, press, focus, toggle): 0–60 ms. Cuts (open, close, arrival, leave, update): 160–400 ms, longer only for a line drawn across a whole chart (≤ 900 ms at a constant feed). Loops (loading): one full-width pass per 1800 ms; the spinner's drill 480 ms.                                                                                                               |
| G5  | **Colour has a cause.** The only chroma is the oxide film, always in the heat order gold → violet → blue → cyan (`--warning-foreground`, `--chart-2`, `--chart-1`, `--chart-3`); bronze `--chart-4` only inside that ramp, never alone (it is the destructive hue, hsl 12 88% 62%).                                                                                                                                       |
| G6  | **Two accents, two meanings.** Bare bright metal (`--primary`, the tool edge) marks what the person did: focus, pick, press. Blue oxide (`--chart-1`) marks what the system says: today, events, a running process.                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| G7  | **Square corners, one chamfer diagonal.** `border-radius: 0`; a cut corner is the register's own pair (top-left and bottom-right, `--kp-chamfer`), on every plate, tag and badge; round only for parts that are turned on a lathe (dials, rivets, graph nodes, spinner).                                                                                                                                                  |
| G8  | **Surfaces are machined.** Brushed grain (`repeating-linear-gradient(90deg, … 0 1px, transparent 1px 3px)`), a bright tool-edge line along the top (`--kp-tool-edge`), engraved text (dark cut over a lit lip, `text-shadow: 0 -1px 0 background, 0 1px 0 foreground/0.12`), Martian Mono for figures, labels and counts.                                                                                                 |
| G9  | **Live change is heat, not light.** A value that updates in place gets the package's heat tint (`kp-sig-titanium-update-anodise`): the oxide ramp runs start→end across it through `mix-blend-mode: color`, linear, and cools away; no scale, no brightness flare, no glow ring.                                                                                                                                          |
| G10 | **Loading is the cutter leaving heat behind.** A loader is a bright tool head (`--primary`) running start→end, linear, with the oxide tint left in its track (the anodising bath is the reference): one picture, one period, in every component.                                                                                                                                                                          |
| G11 | **Leaving is the part cooling off.** What leaves runs the heat ramp start→end and drops away linear; what arrives plays that backwards (reverse-close), so arrival and leave share one curve and one direction axis.                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| G12 | **Every animation sits under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`;** the reduced pose is the finished part (the still oxide wash, the full cut), never a missing picture.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| G13 | **An element inside a composite is the theme's own element.** A button in a header, menu, drawer, tile or alert hovers, focuses, presses and is cut exactly like titanium's `.kp-button`; a link like its link; a menu entry like `.kp-menu__item`; a chip like `.kp-badge`. A composite may add its own accent around them, never replace their hover, focus ring, press, corner or font. The base set is listed in §4b. |

## 2. Inventory of the decided picks

Legend for easing: **L** linear, **St** steps, **E** eased (ease / ease-out /
ease-in-out / standard bezier), **Sp** springy (overshoot). Direction: S→E
inline-start to end, E→S end to start, T↓ top-down, C centre-out, ∅ in place.

## 6. What this means for the loading pictures

Kenny's reference is **the anodising bath** (kpi `r2-ti-load-1`): the oxide
ramp washed into the plate, drifting start→end, 2200 ms linear.

1. **Direction is already uniform** — every titanium loader runs inline-start →
   end (calendar, chart, columns, tiles, trend, kpi, menu, meter, skeleton,
   progress). Only busy (in place, outlier-5) breaks it.
2. **Easing:** all linear except busy (`ease-in-out`, outlier-5) and menu
   (`steps(5)`, outlier-11).
3. **Colour is the real split:** only the kpi bath (and the calendar cutter's
   blue glow) carries oxide; chart, columns, tiles, trend, menu, busy, skeleton
   and progress are grey/primary machining. The bath is colour without a tool;
   the others are a tool without colour.
4. **The fusion (proposal-12):** the cutter leaves heat behind it — physically what
   cutting does to titanium. A bright tool head (`--primary`, the `--kp-tool-edge`
   line) passes start→end; behind it the bath's ramp (gold → bronze → violet →
   blue → cyan, mixed ~32 % into the surface) is left in the track and fades as
   the head returns. Each component keeps its carrier: the whole tile (kpi,
   tiles), each day tile (calendar), the plot groove (chart), the number bezel
   (columns), the ridged band (trend), the menu's bottom edge (menu), the 2 px
   bar (busy), the ghost links (graph, as the signal).
5. **One period:** 1800 ms per full-width pass (the skeleton's and busy's
   current period, between the bath's 2200 and the cutters' 1400–1600), so two
