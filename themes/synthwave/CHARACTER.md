# Synthwave — character

The reference for how synthwave looks and moves. Read the grammar before
adding or changing any synthwave component; the inventory, families and
outliers below it record where the decided picks (2026-09-08 … 2026-10-07)
stand against that grammar, and the numbered proposals (proposal-1 …) are the
plan to bring them in line. Nothing here is applied yet: Kenny fixes the
grammar first, question by question, in `research/synthwave-character` (the
titanium way, Kenny 2026-10-07 02:54: "waar jij eerst uitzoekt wat bij mekaar
past, wat niet past en dan zo voorstellen doet"; synthwave fourth in the
series after cyberpunk, Kenny's order of 04:23: cyberpunk, synthwave,
solstice, brutalism, grotesk, blueprint).

Sources measured (2026-10-07): every `research/character-*/decided.json`
pick for synthwave resolved to its CSS (shape, loading, arrival, tone,
interaction, live, phone …) with a rule extractor over each demo's
stylesheets, and to the option's own words in the demo's `demo.js` and round
files; `css/synthwave-register.css` (2822 lines, layers `kp.register` and
`kp.signature`), `css/themes.css` (synthwave tokens, the VHS texture, every
theme's `--fx-ease` and `--radius`), `js/motion.js` (reverse-close opening),
`js/update.js` (every theme's `--kp-update`), `themes/synthwave/anatomy.md`,
`research/THEME_PROFILES.md`, `research/families/VERDICTS.md` (Kenny's
synthwave family picks) and `research/families-applied/pictures.js` (every
approved theme's family pictures, for §7), and the four character files
already written (`themes/titanium`, `forest`, `nostromo`, `cyberpunk`).
Contrast was computed from the tokens (WCAG 2.2) and the picks were read in
Firefox. Every motion claim quotes its selector or keyframe and its values.

## 1. The synthwave grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the drive into the sunset, 1984**: a neon horizon on the night,
the grid floor running to it, the striped sun sinking behind it, an arcade
marquee and a VCR's on-screen display. Everything that happens on screen is
something that happens on that horizon: it **rises over the horizon, sets
behind it, switches on like a neon tube, chases like marquee bulbs, is marked
by a laser**. Nothing tears, glitches, falls, bounces or runs diagonal; the
world is horizontal.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the floor is the ground, the
tubes are the light, the sun is the clock of coming and going**. A reporting
plate stands on the grid floor under its own horizon; every light in the
theme is a neon tube (a near-white core, the colour in its bloom: letters,
controls, lines, rings); what arrives or opens rises over its horizon and what
leaves sets behind it, cut by the sun's stripes. This is the reading of
Kenny's family picks taken together: chrome over the grid (shape), the marquee
chases (loading), over the horizon (arrival), the laser flares (live), the
marquee glows (hover), the neon spotlight ring (tone).

| #   | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1  | **Bodies glide on the sunrise curve; light switches.** Every one-shot motion of a part (rising, setting, opening, closing, a mark sliding) uses `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`: a slow start, a long glide, a slow landing, the sun clearing the horizon; never an overshoot. Light does not move: a bulb, a tube, a stripe switches in hard steps (`steps(1)`), a neon tube with at most two dips as it strikes (the anatomy's tube). A loop of a body (the road, the floor) runs at a constant speed (`linear`). Not the register's quick curve `(0.2, 0.9, 0.2, 1)`: that is cyberpunk's register exactly and nearly titanium's decided curve (§7).                     |
| G2  | **The world is horizontal.** What arrives or opens rises over its horizon; what leaves sets behind it; light runs along a line, start → end (the marquee, the road) or from its centre out both ways (the horizon, the laser); a line in time is drawn start → end. All in logical terms (mirrored by `dir`). Nothing falls from above, nothing slides in from a side, nothing tears sideways, nothing runs diagonal (cyberpunk is diagonal, §7).                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| G3  | **What opens rises over its horizon** (Kenny's arrival family). A menu, the header's menu, a popover, the dialog, the drawer, a toast and a tooltip open the same way: the panel's horizon, a pink line with its bloom along its lower edge, is drawn from its centre outward; the panel rises 0.5 rem from behind it, uncovered from that line up (`clip-path`), cut by the sun's stripes that close as it rises; its top stripe strikes on (the menu's _Neon strikes_). 2 beats (450 ms). Closing sets it behind the line, the same frames reversed. Never `scale`, never a drop from the top, never a slide from an edge.                                                 |
| G4  | **Durations keep the beat.** One beat is 225 ms: one step of Kenny's marquee (900 ms in four) and the register's own contact (220 ms). Contact 1 beat (225 ms). Opening and closing a panel 2 beats (450 ms). A live change 3 beats (675 ms). Arriving and leaving 4 beats (900 ms; the trend's 1000 and the leave's 750 meet there). A loop 4 beats (900 ms, the marquee); the spinner's sun 8 (1800 ms). Every duration a whole number of beats; the hero floor's drift (6 s) is scenery and stays.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| G5  | **Pink acts and signals, cyan reads, laser warns, red fails** (the anatomy, kept). `--primary` is what a person does (an action, a pick, a press, focus's lit tube) and what signals a change (the laser). `--accent` (cyan) is what the system reads out: identifiers, timestamps, a label's `▶`, a chart's ticks, the horizon's far glow. Laser yellow (`--warning-foreground`, 11.5:1 on the tape) only for a warning and inside the sun's ramp. `--destructive` only for a failure. A neon core is `--kp-core`, never `white`; a tone line is its ink, never the tone plates (`--warning` 1.3:1, `--success` 1.2:1 on the tape, measured).                               |
| G6  | **Square panels, 2 px controls, a horizontal cut, never a notch or a pill.** Plates and panels (card, tile, key figure, menu, popover, dialog, drawer, toast) are square and carry the stripe; controls and small parts (button, field, tag, badge, chip, tooltip, legend key, a day) take `--radius` (2 px). Round only for the sun, the neon ring, a dot and the radio. The theme's cut is horizontal: the sun's stripes, the button's sun cut; never a notch (cyberpunk's) or a chamfer (titanium's).                                                                                                                                                                     |
| G7  | **A reporting plate is chrome over the floor** (Kenny's shape family, carried to the horizon). The tape (`--card`) with the pink → cyan stripe along its top and a chrome bevel (a lit line under the stripe, a shaded foot, in `--kp-core` and `--sidebar-background`); in its lower part the grid floor, lines closing toward a pink horizon and lines converging on its centre, at most 25 %, faded before the words. A panel that opens (menu, popover, dialog, drawer) is the tape with the stripe, without the floor. A plate never glows on its rim (that is cyberpunk's holo card, §7): the glow belongs to tubes. The VHS screen-door stays the still page texture. |
| G8  | **Pointing turns the tube up** (Kenny's hover family, _The marquee glows_). The part pointed at brightens in one step: a control's bloom widens (inset 0.7 em, out 1.6 em, the register's), a plate's title and stripe light; a framed button's sun cut crosses it once (two slits in the ground's colour, 3 steps, the anatomy's); a link's stripe thickens to 3 px. Nothing lifts or moves, siblings are untouched (dark dims them, §7).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| G9  | **A change draws the laser** (Kenny's live family, _The laser flares_, carried to a line). When a value changes in place, a laser line (2 px, a near-white core, a pink bloom) is drawn under it from its centre outward in one beat, flares, and burns down: 3 beats (675 ms), once. The value never moves, flips or glows. On a carrier without text (a state light, a trend line, a tile) the laser is drawn under its own box. Declared as `--kp-update: laser`. Not the glow on the digits: that is dark's live family (§7).                                                                                                                                            |
| G10 | **Loading is the marquee** (Kenny's loading family, §6). On every waiting surface a row of bulbs runs along its top edge, one in four lit, the light chasing one bulb a step, start → end, four hard steps per 900 ms. Nothing sweeps, rolls, scans, drives or slides back and forth.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| G11 | **The spinner is the sun setting** (the signature): the striped sun sinks behind its cyan horizon and rises again, one cycle in 8 beats (1800 ms) on the sunrise curve. The busy progress bar keeps the road, the lane dashes racing start → end (transfer, G16).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| G12 | **Leaving is a sunset, arriving a sunrise.** What leaves sinks 0.5 rem behind its own horizon line while the sun's stripes cut it, the gaps widening toward the line, until it is gone: 4 beats (900 ms); the horizon glows and goes out. What arrives plays that backwards (reverse-close), which is G3's rise, so opening, arriving and leaving are one picture. Not a circle closing over it (solstice's eclipse) and not a collapse to a line (nostromo's tube switched off), §7.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| G13 | **A warning is a neon ring** (Kenny's tone family). A warning or failed part carries a hollow neon ring before its title or figure: a near-white core, the bloom in the tone's ink (`--warning-foreground`, `--destructive`, `--success-foreground`); its title or figure glows in that ink. The ring strikes on once with the tube's dips, then holds; it never flashes, jolts or frames the whole part. The change sits on a 2 px chip with the package's arrows.                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| G14 | **A press dips the tube.** The pressed part's bloom drops to its bare core and its ground takes the `-active` token, for as long as it is held; the part does not move (titanium's press drops 1 px, §7). Focus is DI2's two-channel ring, inset inside a framed control (the register's button) and outside a plate, with the tube lit as under the pointer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| G15 | **The OSD names, the chrome counts.** Labels, tags, table heads, identifiers and timestamps: VT323 (the theme's mono, a VCR's on-screen display), capitals, tracked 0.12 em, after a cyan `▶`; labels in the pink that reads (`--kp-pink-text`, 6.9:1 on the tape), identifiers and timestamps in cyan. Figures and titles: KP Outrun Display, a near-white core with the pink tube. Prose and buttons: Rajdhani. KP Tech Mono, which the register uses for its data (`--kp-mono`), is cyberpunk's label face (§7) and leaves synthwave.                                                                                                                                     |
| G16 | **Every motif means one thing.** The horizon: where things come and go (open, arrive, leave, the divider, a floor's edge). The striped sun: the sun itself (spinner, empty state, the current step, the meter's mark) and the cut it leaves on what crosses it (the button's sun cut, a part setting). The grid floor: the ground under a reporting plate and the hero. The road: transfer (the busy bar). The marquee bulbs: waiting. The laser: a change, and a heading's rule. The neon ring: a tone. The stripe: the top of a panel, a link. VHS: the still texture and the OSD voice, never a tracking glitch (cyberpunk's glitch, §7).                                 |
| G17 | **An element inside a composite is the theme's own element.** A button in a header, menu, drawer, tile or alert hovers, focuses and presses exactly like synthwave's `.kp-button` (the tube turned up, the sun cut, the inset ring, the dip); a link like its link; a menu entry like `.kp-menu__item`; a tag like the badge. A composite may add its own accent around them, never replace their hover, focus ring, press, corner or font (§4b).                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| G18 | **Every animation sits under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`;** the reduced pose is the finished picture (the marquee standing with one bulb in four lit, the panel risen, the laser standing under the value for a moment), never a missing one. **The network graph is never a target** (Kenny, 2026-10-07 02:54): its shape (the grid-floor constellation), loading (the outrun pulse), arrival (stars come out), focus (the neon spotlight) and live update (the arcade pop) stay as decided, in synthwave and every theme.                                                                                                                                      |

## 6. The loading picture: the marquee

Decided by Kenny on 2026-10-07 01:32 (research/families: loading = the busy
table): the busy overlay's _The marquee chases_ (loading 3,
research/character-busy) is synthwave's loading picture. What it draws, and
how each part of it carries to every other loading element:

1. **The bulbs.** A row of bulbs along the top edge of the waiting surface:
   each bulb 0.5 rem wide and 3 px tall, 0.35 rem apart (one every 0.85 rem),
   in the primary at 25 % when dark; the lit bulb in the primary with a 3 px
   bloom (a tube, G2's light). As the families page describes it; the picked
   CSS builds one bulb (§0).
2. **The chase.** One bulb in four is lit; the light steps one bulb a step,
   start → end, in four hard steps per 900 ms (`steps(4)`): one step per beat
   (225 ms). Logical: in a right-to-left page it chases from the right.
3. **Light, not a body.** The bulbs never move: the light jumps from bulb to
   bulb. No sweep, no glide, no fade between steps.
4. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a key figure, the busy panel, a
   table, a menu entry, the chart's plot, the meter while it measures): the
   row runs along the surface's own top edge, inside its stripe; the content
   under it stays as it is, its placeholders faint.
5. **Skeleton lines**: tape grooves, each with the bulbs chasing along its
   top, the lines a beat apart, so the light runs down the block line after
   line.
6. **Where there is no room for a row** (a day in the month): each day
   carries one bulb at its top edge that lights on its beat, the days of a
   week a beat apart, so the week itself chases.
7. **The spinner** is the sun (G11), not a bulb: the one loading part that
   is a picture of its own (the signature, Kenny 2026-10-03).
8. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the row stands with one bulb in four
   lit.
9. **Exceptions.** The busy progress bar keeps its road (transfer, G16). The
   network graph keeps its outrun pulse (G18). Nothing else.
