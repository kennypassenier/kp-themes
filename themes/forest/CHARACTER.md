# Forest — character

The reference for how forest looks and moves. Read the grammar before adding
or changing any forest component; the inventory, families and outliers below
it record where the decided picks (2026-10-05 … 2026-10-07) stand against that
grammar, and the numbered proposals (proposal-1 …) are the plan to bring them
in line. Kenny fixed the grammar question by question in
`research/forest-character` (the titanium way, Kenny 2026-10-07 02:54: "waar
jij eerst uitzoekt wat bij mekaar past, wat niet past en dan zo voorstellen
doet") and approved it on 2026-10-07 17:47 (§0); the package applies it (see
the "Applied 2026-10-07" notes under §5). The inventory below describes the
state before that.

Sources measured (2026-10-07): every `research/character-*/decided.json` pick
for forest resolved to its CSS (shape, loading, arrival, tone, interaction,
live, phone …) and to the option's own words in the demo's `demo.js`,
`css/forest-register.css` (2227 lines, layers `kp.register` and
`kp.signature`), `css/themes.css` (forest tokens, the contour texture and its
40 s drift), `js/motion.js` (`themeMotion()`, reverse-close opening),
`research/families/VERDICTS.md` (Kenny's family picks for forest),
`research/progress-signature` (the tree bar, scope-140),
`research/families-applied/forest.css` (the family pictures as translated, not
approved as a way to choose), `themes/forest/anatomy.md` and
`research/THEME_PROFILES.md`. Every motion claim quotes its selector or
keyframe and its values.

## 1. The forest grammar (proposed)

The theme is a **ranger's field kit**: the ground is a map on kraft paper
(contour lines, the trail with its blazes and trig points), and what stands on
it is the wood the map describes. Everything that happens on screen is
something that happens in a wood: it is **planted, grows, is walked, is
blazed, sways in the wind, withers**. Nothing is cut, stamped, glitched,
flashed or bounced, and nothing falls.

The two worlds have a fixed division of labour: **the map is the ground**
(paper, contour texture, the trail's marks: blazes, pins, trig points, dashes),
**the wood is what lives and is built on it** (trees, growth, wood grain on the
instruments, rings). This is the reading of Kenny's family picks taken
together: a wooden gauge with a trig point (shape), the logbook's field note
(tone), the trail blaze (hover), trees planted (loading), growing (arrival), a
growth ring (live).

| G1  | **Forest grows like a tree** (amended 2026-10-07 15:15, Kenny: "iets dat van beneden naar boven groeit, met een bepaalde feeling, het moet niet te snel zijn, ik wil het element zien groeien"). Every one-shot motion uses forest's growth curve, the Gompertz curve of plant growth (`exp(-5.57 e^(-5.05 t))` normalised), written as `linear(0, 0.01 5%, 0.032 10%, 0.072 15%, 0.133 20%, 0.211 25%, 0.302 30%, 0.398 35%, 0.493 40%, 0.582 45%, 0.662 50%, 0.732 55%, 0.791 60%, 0.84 65%, 0.881 70%, 0.913 75%, 0.939 80%, 0.96 85%, 0.977 90%, 0.99 95%, 1)`: a moment under the ground (3 % at a tenth of the time), a long even growth (half at 40 %), a soft settle (90 % at 73 %); a part is between 10 % and 90 % for 55 % of the run (measured per frame in Firefox, 2026-10-07). A close, a shrink, a leave is the same motion played backwards (same keyframes, curve and time, `reverse`; measured frame for frame identical). A there-and-back (the sapling's sway) is `ease-in-out`; a loop keeps a constant walk (`linear`); `steps()` only where whole things are counted. Never an overshoot, never a spring. Not the register's `cubic-bezier(0.33, 0, 0.15, 1)` (half at 30 %, 90 % at 60 %: it reads as opening, not growing), not titanium's quick curve (§7). Two other growth feelings stay on the page as options: the push that unfurls and the three spurts. |
| G2  | **Growth goes up, walking goes start → end.** What arrives grows up from under its own line; what is walked (a loading pass, a line drawn across a chart, the planting front) travels inline-start → end, in logical terms (`inset-inline-start`, `clip-path` mirrored by `dir`). What stands free (a dialog, a tile, every arrival) grows up out of its own base, its top first (`translate 0 100%` → 0, clipped at its base line). Never `scale` (the words are never squashed). It closes as the growth reversed. What lives at an edge (drawer, toast) grows out of that edge. The map's fold (the first proposal) stays an option.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

## 6. The loading picture: planting

Decided by Kenny on 2026-10-07 02:54: the signature progress bar with the
planted trees (`css/forest-register.css`, `kp.signature`, scope-140) is
forest's loading picture. What the bar draws, and how each part of its idea
carries to every other loading element:

1. **The ground.** Cleared ground: a 1 px `--border-strong` line along the
   foot of a `--muted` strip, seedlings (`--kp-fo-seedling`) in a row on it,
   one per planting slot. A slot is half the strip's height
   (`--kp-fo-pitch`), so a tree keeps its shape at any size.
2. **Whole trees only.** The row holds whole slots, centred
   (`round(down, 100cqi, var(--kp-fo-pitch))`, a margin of plain ground at
   both ends); the grown edge always stands on a slot boundary. No tree or
   seedling is ever cut by an edge. Carried: every planting strip, every
   skeleton line and the spinner count whole trees; a stage is a step, never a
   tween.
3. **The forest.** Seven trees per tile (`--kp-fo-trees`: pines, spruces, two
   broadleaves, seven heights) in forest ink, so the skyline does not read as a
   pattern.
4. **The newest tree.** Removed on 2026-10-07 at Kenny's request: the
   sapling head and its sway are gone; the planted row alone shows the share.
5. **Busy, no share known.** Changed on 2026-10-07 at Kenny's request: the
   same planted row breathes. Whole trees fill the bar start → end in
   3000 ms, it holds full 600 ms, and the trees leave end → start in 3000 ms,
   the arrival's frames reversed (`--kp-fo-breath`, symmetric ease-in-out,
   `kp-progressbar-forest-breath`). Reduced motion shows the pose at half
   breath. The compass stays the spinner's loop (`--kp-sig-fo-loop`).
6. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a panel, a table row, a menu
   entry, a busy button or card, a chart's plot): the planting strip at the
   surface's foot, the bar itself at `--kp-progressbar-scale: 0.8` (a 1 rem
   strip, a 0.5 rem slot), the content above it standing faint as it does
   today. Skeleton lines are three such rows, 400 ms apart. While the meter
   measures, the strip takes its groove's place.
7. **Where there is no room for a row** (a day in the month, the spinner):
   one planting slot, the bar's own seedling → sapling → tree in three whole
   stages (three fixed drawings, one shown at a time, `steps`), on its mound,
   then the next; 1600 ms a tree, the days of a week 150 ms apart
   (proposal-4).
8. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the planted row with its grove
   standing at 35 %–65 % of the row on slot boundaries, exactly as the bar's
   own reduced pose; a single slot shows the grown tree.
9. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps its fireflies (G16). Nothing else.

**Measured, 2026-10-07 (Firefox, research/forest-character question 9):**
