# Pastel — character

The reference for how pastel looks and moves. Read the grammar before adding
or changing any pastel component; the inventory, families and outliers below
it record where the decided picks (2026-09-08 … 2026-10-13) stand against that
grammar, and the numbered proposals (proposal-1 …) are the plan to bring them
in line. Written the forest way (Kenny, 2026-10-07 02:54: "waar jij eerst
uitzoekt wat bij mekaar past, wat niet past en dan zo voorstellen doet"):
nothing here is applied before Kenny's verdicts in
`research/pastel-character`, which turns every open rule into one question
with the recommendation first. Pastel was the last theme without a character
file; it has no anchor round of its own, so the anchor is the first question
of that demo (§0a).

Sources read (2026-10-14): every `research/character-*/decided.json` pick for
pastel resolved to its option's own words in the demo's `demo.js` (and, where
the CSS was read, to its values), `css/pastel-register.css` (2691 lines, layers
`kp.register` and `kp.signature`), `themes/pastel/tokens.json`,
`themes/pastel/anatomy.md`, `themes/pastel/signature.json`,
`research/families/VERDICTS.md` (Kenny's family picks for pastel),
`docs/SCOPE.md` (scope-4, scope-23, scope-24, scope-93, scope-95, scope-97,
scope-98), `research/size-motion` (the leave), `research/open-reverse` (the

## 0a. The signature anchor (proposed)

Every other theme that has decided its character has one element every
decision departs from: synthwave's page horizon, nostromo's raster, grotesk's
red plate out of register, blueprint's tracing pen, phantom's thrown screen.
Pastel's only opening today is the sheet, which reads plain (lesson 52): a
move, a turn and a fade, which every theme could do. Its own material is
already in the register, scattered: the headline's second pass that lands off
register, the progress bar's sky offset under its dotted fill, the dialog's
second sheet, the sticker's flat peel, and the families' washi tape and
_Stuck on_. The anchor gathers them into one picture.

**Recommended: Printed twice, stuck on.** A part is printed in two passes and
stuck onto the page. First the **second pass** lands: a flat silhouette of the
part in the second ink (`--fx-overprint`, sky; no words, no outline, only the
shape and its radius) springs from a wide misregistration into its rest,
**6 px off register, down and toward the end**, where it stays as the part's
flat shadow. Then the **ink pass**, the part itself, is stuck on: it swings
down from its top-start corner (the corner the washi tape holds), lands on the
same soft overshoot and settles, a hair late against its pass. The close is
exactly that backwards: the part peels off at its corner, then the pass slides
off register and is gone. Measured as a picture it is two layers in two inks
on two clocks, which no other theme draws: grotesk's red plate travels a
closing spiral at an even pace, stops dead and _hides in register_; phantom's
part resolves from halftone dots on a static deep-red plate; brutalism's slab
casts one still black shadow. Pastel's pass never registers and never hides:
it springs into an offset that stays, and it is the sticker's shadow.

| #   | Option                                    | What happens                                                                                                                                                                         | Overlap, and why it is or is not recommended                                                                                                                                  |
| --- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Printed twice, stuck on** (recommended) | the sky pass springs from wide misregistration into its 6 px off-register rest; 120 ms later the part swings down from its top-start corner onto it; the close is that backwards | none: the pass that lands off register and stays as a flat shadow is pastel's (the headline's, since 2026-09-08); the swing from the corner is Kenny's arrival family        |
| 2   | Into its off-register rest                | the sky pass and the ink pass come from opposite sides of the misregistration line and spring together to rest 6 px apart; no turn                                                  | the headline's own move, carried; two plates closing on one another is near grotesk's plate falling into register, told apart only by the spring and the offset that stays |
| 3   | Popped (the picks)                        | the part scales from a quarter of its size with the overshoot (the busy table's and the trend's _Popped_, the header menu's), its pass a moment after                                | a scale squashes the words while it plays, and a pop from a point is light's and the tooltip's bubble                                                                         |
| 4   | The sheet (today)                         | up from 18 px below, turned -2.5°, out of nothing, part and pass together (`kp-sig-pastel-pastel-sheet`)                                                                            | reads PLAIN (lesson 52): rising is light's, solstice's, synthwave's and forest's direction, and nothing in it is pastel's but the curve                                      |

**How the grammar derives from it.** The pass is the theme's one second
layer: it is the plate's flat shadow at rest (G7), the thing that jolts on a
live change (G9, as an option), the strip's misregistered colour in loading
(G10); the swing from the taped corner is every arrival and opening (G2, G3);
the peel off that corner is every leave (G12) and the hover (G14); a press
sticks the part flat onto its pass (G16). The words never double (the anatomy's
rule): the pass is a silhouette, not a copy of the text.

## 1. The pastel grammar (proposed)

The theme is a **risograph-printed sticker sheet in a planner**: a lavender
milk page with a faint halftone, plum ink, a second ink that never lines up,
and what stands on the page is a sticker or a taped-in note. Everything that
happens on screen happens to paper in a planner: it is **printed in two
passes, stuck on, taped down, peeled, pressed flat, wobbled**. Nothing is cut,
glitched, scanned, grown, thrown or burnt; nothing lands with a dead stop.

| #   | Rule |
| --- | ---- |
| G1  | **A soft landing with one overshoot.** Every one-shot motion uses pastel's landing curve, a smooth approach that passes its rest by 8 % and settles back once: `linear(0, 0.014 5%, 0.052 10%, 0.112 15%, 0.189 20%, 0.28 25%, 0.38 30%, 0.486 35%, 0.594 40%, 0.7 45%, 0.8 50%, 0.891 55%, 0.968 60%, 1.028 65%, 1.066 70%, 1.08 75%, 1.072 80%, 1.052 85%, 1.028 90%, 1.008 95%, 1)` (half at 36 % of the time, 90 % at 56 %, the peak 1.08 at 75 %; computed from the curve). One overshoot, never a rebound: scope-4's "a settle rather than a bounce". The register's `--fx-ease`, `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`, is the same family but reaches 90 % at 30 % of its time and peaks at 1.098 at 57 % (computed from the bezier): the landing is over before it is seen, and what remains reads as a bounce. A close is the same keyframes on the same curve played `reverse` (lesson 8): the part first dips toward its rest, then lifts away. A loop's there-and-back (the flutter) is `ease-in-out`; a drift is `linear`. |
| G2  | **Along the misregistration line, in reading order.** The second pass rests down and toward the end (`translate: 6px 6px`, mirrored by `dir`); it arrives from further along that line. The ink pass swings down from its top-start corner (`transform-origin: 0 0`, the inline-start corner in RTL). A group comes in reading order, start → end, top → bottom, 60 ms apart. Nothing rises from below (the sheet's rise is light's, solstice's, synthwave's and forest's), nothing falls from above, nothing grows from its centre. |
| G3  | **What opens is stuck on beside its trigger.** A menu, a date picker, a combobox list or a tooltip under its trigger swings down from the corner nearest the trigger, so its top edge never leaves it; a free part (a dialog, the alarm's panel, the tour's card, a toast) swings from its own top-start corner; an edge part (the drawer, the side navigation) from the corner at its edge. Every one lands on its pass first. Never `scale` (the words are never squashed). The close is the open backwards. |
| G4  | **Durations: one picture of 560 ms.** Contact 220 ms (`--fx-duration`). A pass 440 ms; the ink pass starts 120 ms after the second pass, so one landing is 560 ms, the dialog and the menu alike. A group 60 ms apart. The wobble 800 ms. A loop 2400 ms. A leave is the landing backwards, 560 ms. |
| G5  | **Three inks: plum acts, sky is the second pass, mint fills.** `--primary` (rose plum) and its darker peel are what a person does and the text; `--fx-overprint` (sky) is the second pass and nothing else (no text, no action, no fill); `--accent` (mint) is the plate behind a marked word, the washi tape and a hovered entry. Violet (`--info`) stays the info status only. The status colours show a state on the sticker (G8), never as an edge or a glow. No rainbow, no black outline. |
| G6  | **Round, two radii and the pill.** Plates (cards, panels, dialogs, menus, toasts) `calc(var(--radius) * 1.2)` (1.2 rem); controls (buttons, fields, the sticker of a change) 0.85 rem; chips, tags, the tooltip and the bar pills. Round for what is round (a day, a dot, a pearl). Never square, never a scallop or a blob. |
| G7  | **Paper with its pass, taped where it is stuck.** A plate is white paper (`--card`) on the milk ground, its second pass a flat sky shape 6 px off register beside it; a control is a sticker with its flat plum peel 3 px under it (scope-24). No blur, no soft shadow anywhere. A plate that is stuck to the page (a card, a tile, a panel, the dialog, the drawer) carries one strip of washi tape across its top-start corner (Kenny's shape family); what is held in the hand (a menu, a tooltip, a toast, a button) carries none. The halftone dots are the ground's only texture. |
| G8  | **A tone is a sticker.** The change on a key figure, a tile or the trend is a small sticker on the tone's own plate and ink (`--success` / `--success-foreground`, `--warning` / `--warning-foreground`, `--destructive`), with its own flat peel (Kenny's tone family); a warning or failed figure carries the note in its label on the tone's plate. Never a heart, never askew, never a tape label. |
| G9  | **A live change wobbles.** What changed (a figure, a word, a line, a tile's value) wobbles once like jelly round its foot: `rotate` 10° → -7° → 3° → 0, 800 ms `ease-out` (the graph's _The wobble_, Kenny's live family). Its second pass stays where it is, so the wobble shows against it. No swell, no wiggle of a whole tile, no colour flash. |
| G10 | **Loading is washi tape.** A strip of striped washi tape (mint, its stripes in `--accent` and `--accent-foreground` at a quarter) lies across the top of every waiting surface and flutters gently round its middle (±1.5°, 2400 ms `ease-in-out`, alternate), its stripes drifting start → end; where there is no room for a strip (the spinner) the roll of the same tape turns (§6). |
| G11 | **The spinner is the tape roll.** A ring of the striped tape turns in three hops a lap on the landing curve, 1200 ms a lap, its sky pass beside it. |
| G12 | **Leaving is peeling off.** What leaves is its landing played backwards: it peels off at its top-start corner, then its pass slides off register; what arrives is stuck on. One picture, one curve, 560 ms. The balloon (Kenny's leave of 2026-10-04) is an option: with it, an arrival comes down from above while an opening comes from its corner, two directions for one gesture. |
| G13 | **An element inside a composite is the theme's own element.** A button in a header, menu, drawer, tile or alert is pastel's `.kp-button` in every state; a menu entry `.kp-menu__item`; a tag the sticker. A composite may add its own accent around them, never replace their hover, focus ring, press, corner or font (§4b). |
| G14 | **Pointing peels the corner.** Hover lifts the sticker 2 px on its peel (the peel grows from 3 to 5 px, still flat) and its top-end corner curls up into a small fold in the paper's back (`--muted`), on the landing curve in 220 ms; the words stand upright and the siblings are untouched. Kenny's hover family (_The tape peel_) drawn so it shows a peel, and scope-24's lift without the turn. |
| G15 | **Focus is the two rings, the outer one dashed.** DI2's two channels stay (a box-shadow ring in `--focus-ring` composed with the peel, lesson 41) and the outline becomes a 2 px dashed `--primary` line 3 px out (the header family's "focus dashes a line round it"). |
| G16 | **A press sticks it down.** The part drops onto its peel (3 px down, peel 0) and the fold lies flat, 220 ms; it does not shrink, squash or turn (scope-24). |
| G17 | **One face; weight speaks.** Instrument Sans for everything; titles and figures 700, labels 600 in sentence case, figures tabular; the microlabel's spaced capitals stay the one exception; monospace for identifiers, timestamps and log lines only. No face outside the tokens. |
| G18 | **Motifs only from the sheet and the press.** The second pass, the sticker and its fold, the washi tape, the pearls on a thread, the halftone dots. Not candy: no sprinkles, hearts, clouds, macarons, gummies, bubblegum outlines or rainbows. |
| G19 | **Every animation sits under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`;** the reduced pose is the finished picture: the part stuck on with its pass 6 px off register, the tape still on its surface, the roll still. |
| G20 | **The network graph is never a target** (Kenny, 2026-10-07 02:54). Its shape (candy beads and licorice), loading (the sprinkle drift), arrival (beads dropped in), focus (the package's dimming) and live update (the wobble, the source of G9) stay exactly as decided. |

Decided by Kenny on 2026-10-07 01:32 as the loading family: the busy table's

turning ±1° in 2000 ms `ease-in-out`). What the picture is, and how it carries
to every other loading element:

1. **The tape.** A strip of washi tape: mint (`--accent`) with stripes of
   `--accent-foreground` mixed in at a quarter, 6 px wide and 6 px apart, its
   two ends cut straight (no torn edge, G6), 0.6 rem high on a surface, its
   corners 2 px soft. Its sky pass sits 2 px off register under it (the
   anchor's second ink, at a third of its strength so the tape stays mint).

   90 % of its width, half on the surface and half off its top edge, as tape
   holds a note in a planner. On a surface narrower than 6 rem it is a short
   tab across the top-start corner at 35° (the busy shape's own corner).
3. **The flutter.** The strip turns round its middle from -1.5° to 1.5° and
   back, 2400 ms a cycle, `ease-in-out` (a there-and-back), and its stripes
   drift start → end one stripe pitch (12 px) per cycle, `linear`: the tape
   is being pulled off its roll while it hangs.
4. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a panel, a table row, a menu entry,
   a busy button or card, a chart's plot, the month's days): the strip on its
   top edge, the content under it standing faint (`opacity: 0.55`), the
   surface's own pass and peel kept.
5. **Skeleton lines** are three strips laid one under the other, 400 ms out
   of phase, each as long as its line.
6. **The busy progress bar** is the tape laid along the whole track, its
   stripes drifting start → end at the same pitch and period; the share bar
   keeps its dotted fill and its sticker head.
7. **Where there is no room for a strip** (the spinner, a day of the month):
   the roll of the same tape, a ring of the stripes round a hole, turning
   clockwise in three hops a lap on the landing curve, 1200 ms a lap, its sky
   pass 1.5 px beside it (G11).
8. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the strip lying level on its
   surface, the roll at rest.
9. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps its sprinkle drift (G20). Nothing
