adding or changing any brutalism component; the inventory, families and
outliers below it record where the decided picks (2026-09-08 … 2026-10-07)
stand against that grammar, and the numbered proposals (proposal-1 …) are the
plan to bring them in line. Kenny fixed the grammar question by question in

## 1. The brutalism grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the slab**: raw construction, shown as it is. A box is
drawn in one ink line and stands on its own hard shadow, which is where it
would fall if you let go of it: its footprint on the page. Nothing is
disguised: the line is the structure, the shadow is the weight, the colour is
paint, a control names what it is. So everything that happens is something
weight does: what arrives **is dropped onto its footprint** and stops dead;
what is pointed at **is lifted off it**; what is pressed **is driven into
it**; while something waits, **its reading is poured** in its own place; a
new value **is slammed onto its offset**, as the headline's words are; a
warning **is taped off**; what leaves **is lifted off and taken away**.
Nothing eases into place, nothing bounces, nothing fades, nothing glows.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the shadow is the ground, the
line is the structure, yellow is the hand, the slab is the body**. The hard
shadow never moves, never blurs and never takes a colour; everything that
moves, moves the box against it. The line is one ink in two weights. Yellow
marks where a person's hand is (the ordinary button, the pointed, the
picked, the current). Archivo Black shouts the titles and figures; Space
Grotesk speaks everything else in bold capitals.

| #   | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1  | **Gravity: what comes falls, and stops dead.** What arrives, opens or lands accelerates all the way down and stops in one frame, on the meter's own _Thrown on_ curve, `cubic-bezier(0.6, 0, 0.9, 0.5)` (it leaves at rest and lands at full speed, a final slope of 5). What goes is lifted on the same curve played back, `cubic-bezier(0.1, 0.5, 0.4, 1)`, quick off the ground and slowing at the top. Whole things counted (the lifts of a pour, a row of blocks) step with `steps()`. Never an ease-out settle, never a spring or a bounce, never a fade. Not hard steps alone: those are terminal's register, nostromo's frame clock, cyberpunk's ticks and retro's (§7). |
| G2  | **Depth is the shadow's diagonal.** The hard shadow falls down and to the right; so _up off the page_ is up and to the left, physically, the light's direction, not mirrored by `dir`. What arrives or opens drops onto its footprint from up-left; what is pointed at lifts toward up-left; what is pressed sinks down-right into its footprint; what leaves is lifted off toward up-left. What is laid in a row (a line in a chart, the days of a week, a pour along a well) goes start → end, in logical terms. Nothing slides in from a side edge, nothing falls straight down the page, nothing scales, nothing tilts in motion.                                            |
| G3  | **What opens is dropped onto its footprint.** A menu, the header's menu, a popover, the dialog, the drawer, a toast and a tooltip: their hard shadow is on the page from the first frame, where the panel will stand; the panel falls onto it from 1.5 rem up-left on G1's curve and stops dead, 3 units (300 ms); the dialog, the big slab, keeps Kenny's quarter-speed drop (15 units, 1500 ms, from 2.5 rem). Closing lifts it off (G12). No clip, no fade, no scale, no tilt.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| G4  | **Durations count the register's 100 ms.** Contact 1 unit (100 ms, the register's `--fx-duration`). A fall (an opening, an arrival, a live slam) and a leave 3 units (300 ms). The dialog's drop 15 units (1500 ms). A loop 12 units (1200 ms; the picks loop between 480 ms and 2200 ms). Every duration a whole number of units; the marquee's 42 s and the band's 20 s are scenery and stay.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| G5  | **Ink builds, yellow is the hand, lavender is the second sheet, the statuses are paint.** The one ink (`--foreground` = `--primary` = `--border-strong`) is every line, every shadow, every word and the primary action. Yellow (`--secondary`) is where a hand is: the ordinary button and every touched state (pointed, focused well, picked, current, the table's head). Lavender (`--accent`) is the second plate (a share's fill, every other column, the dialog's backdrop). The status plates are paint under black ink (paper on red, DI4), never a line, a shadow or a glow; paint is flat and full: no tint mixed from a plate.                                        |

Siblings untouched. | G9  | **A new value is slammed onto its offset.** When a value changes in place, the new value appears 6 px up-left of its place and falls onto it on G1's curve, 3 units, landing on a yellow copy of itself 3 px down-right: the headline's slam (2026-09-08) as the live update. ## 2. Inventory of the decided picks

As built on 2026-10-07. Legend for easing: **S** the register's and the
signature's `--fx-ease` = `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` (formal's, light's,
grotesk's and nostromo's too), **G** a gravity curve (an ease-in that lands
at speed: the meter's `(0.6, 0, 0.9, 0.5)`, the chart's `(0.5, 0, 1, 0.5)`),
**St** `steps(n)`, **IO** `ease-in-out`, **L** linear, **Sp** a spring (a
bezier past 1). Direction: ↖ up-left (off the page), ↘ down-right (onto the
shadow), ↓ straight down from above, ← → sideways, ↑ up, ∅ in place.
Verdict: **fits** the grammar, or the outlier it is (§4).

## 6. The loading picture: the pour

Not decided by a family pick (brutalism has none); proposed from the trend's
_The pour_ (Kenny's correction of 2026-10-07, research/character-trend), its
_Cast_ arrival ("there the instant the pour sets solid"), the meter's blocks
_Thrown on_, the drawer's _It is cast in one pour_ by name and the graph's
_Re-cast_, which already make casting the theme's way of making a reading.
What it draws, and how each part of it carries to every other loading
element:

1. **The well.** The place the reading will stand (a figure's line, a
   tile's body line, a table's rows, a chart's plot, a meter's track, a menu
   entry's row) is a well in the 2 px ink line, as the trend's plot is "a
   plain cast well", white inside.
2. **The pour.** Ink fills the well from its floor in four hard lifts, a
   quarter of its height each (`steps(4)`), the way concrete is placed in
   lifts. The ink is the theme's one ink, unblurred, no gradient.
3. **The strike.** Full, the well holds for one lift and is struck clean in
   one cut (the formwork stripped), and the next pour starts: one cycle in
   12 units (1200 ms): four lifts, one hold, the strike.
4. **In place.** The pour does not travel: it fills where the value will
   stand; nothing runs along the foot, nothing goes back and forth, nothing
   hammers or crawls.
5. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a key figure, the busy panel, a
   table, a menu entry, the chart's plot, the meter while it measures): the
   labels and titles stand above the well, readable; only the reading's
   place is poured.
6. **Skeleton lines**: each block (the signature's line and footprint) is a
   well, poured in its own lifts, the blocks two units apart, so the pour
   moves down the block like courses of a wall.
7. **Where there is no room for a well** (a day in the month): the day's
   figure place is the well, the days of a week one unit apart.
8. **Arrival is the strike** (the trend's _Cast_): when the reading comes,
   the well is struck and the value is there; under G9 a value that then
   changes is slammed onto its offset.
9. **The spinner** is the block tipped over (G11); the busy bar keeps its
   tape (the signature).
10. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the well stands half poured.
11. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps the site taped off (G18).
    Nothing else.
