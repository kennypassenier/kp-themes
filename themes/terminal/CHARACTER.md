**What the picks and the families keep, and what they add.** The anatomy is a
screen: a raster that never moves, cells, one hue, one face, "the only loops
are the sweep and the caret". Kenny's family verdict turns one part of that
screen into its actor: **the block cursor**. It is the headline's caret
(2026-09-08), the cursor in the box (R6-Q7), the cursor that arrives under the
hand (gap-4), the empty prompt's cursor (the signature) and the cursor that
deletes what leaves (round seven); the families add it as the loading picture
(the busy table's cursor blinks where the words will be written), the live
update (the graph's changed part blinks like a cursor) and the hover family's
_cursor ring_. Everything else the families decided is what a text screen is
made of: a line is typed out at the line's rate (_Typed out_), a plate is a
line of htop between square brackets (_The htop meter_), a tone is printed in
reverse video on the tone's plate (_The dumb-terminal plot_), a press enters
the part in reverse video. One idea holds them: **the cursor writes the
screen**. What arrives is typed by it, cell by cell, the cursor at the head of
the line; what opens is printed by it, line after line; while something
waits, the cursor waits where the reading will be written; what changes
blinks, as the cursor does; what is pointed at takes the cursor; what leaves
is deleted by it. Nothing on a text screen eases, glides, scales, rounds or
glows a rim: a cell is lit or it is not.

## 1. The terminal grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the text screen**: a VT-class terminal whose phosphor shows
character cells and nothing else. The ground is the dark glass, the phosphor
is the one ink at three brightnesses, the block cursor is the one actor. So
everything that happens is something a terminal does: what arrives **is typed
out**, the cursor at the head of the line; what opens **is printed**, a line
a line at a time; while something waits, **the cursor waits** where the reading
will be written; a new value **blinks**; what is pointed at **takes the
cursor**; what is pressed **is entered in reverse video**; a tone **is printed
in reverse in its colour and says its level**; what leaves **is deleted by the
cursor**. Nothing eases, glides (but the leave Kenny asked for), fades (but
the phosphor's own decay), scales, turns, rises, rounds or glows a rim.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the screen is the ground, the
cursor writes, the brackets hold a line, reverse video emphasises**. A plate
on the screen is a line of htop between square brackets; what floats over the
screen is a curses window in one box-drawing line. The bright phosphor is
what is written now and what a person does; the phosphor is the text; the dim
phosphor is a frame, a bracket, an unlit cell. Red is danger, the yellow-green
a mark and a warning, never a frame.

## 6. The loading picture: the cursor waits

Kenny picked it as terminal's loading family on 2026-10-07 01:32: the busy
table's _The cursor blinks_, "a block cursor at the end of the words appears
and disappears on a hard beat, no fade (1 s)". It is the one loading picture
that is already terminal's own in five other places: the field's cursor in
the box (R6-Q7), the button's cursor (gap-4), the empty prompt's cursor (the
signature), the progress bar's blinking head and the trend's caret (a pick).
What it draws (`busy-b.css`): a block (`--bo-pic`, the primary, 0.6 by
1.1 rem) placed at the end of the busy panel's words, `bo-terminal-cursor`
1 s `steps(2, jump-none)`: lit 0–49 %, dark 50–100 % (measured on for 500 ms,
off for 500). A terminal shows that block where its next character will be
written: the cursor waiting is the screen saying "the answer goes here". How
each part of it carries to every other loading element:

1. **Where.** On every waiting surface one block cursor stands where the
   reading will be written: after the label's colon on the reading's line
   when the line holds words, at the line's start when it is empty (a key
   figure's figure, a tile's body, a table's first empty row, a chart's plot,
   a meter's track, a menu entry's row, a day's number).
2. **What.** One character cell: `1ch` wide and one line tall (the busy
   table's 0.6 × 1.1 rem is that cell in its type), the bright phosphor
   (`--primary`) with the bloom, square.
3. **When.** The slow blink (G4): lit for 500 ms, dark for 500 ms, hard, for as
   long as the part waits; never a fade, never a pulse in brightness.
4. **One.** One cursor per surface, whatever its size: a table waits with one
   cursor at its first row, not one per row; a week waits with one cursor in
   the first day that has none.
5. **In place.** The cursor does not travel and nothing runs along the foot: a
   terminal's cursor stands still while the line is not yet written.
6. **Labels stand.** Titles, labels, heads and units stand above or before
   the cursor, readable; only the reading is missing.
7. **The spinner** is the braille cell (approved): two dots walk a 2 × 4 cell,
   the phosphor decaying behind them; it is the signature's sign of work
   going on, the cursor the sign of a reading not yet written.
8. **The busy progress bar** keeps `<=>` walking its cells (approved): a
   transfer, not a wait for one value.
9. **Arrival is the cursor starting to type.** When the reading comes, the
   cursor stops blinking and types it (G2): the waiting block becomes the
   block at the head of the line, so waiting and arriving are one cursor.
10. **A change** blinks rapidly (G9), three times: the fast blink after a
    write, the slow one before it.
11. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the cursor stands lit, a solid block
    where the reading will be.
12. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps _The packet stream_ exactly (G18).
    Nothing else.
