### 0a. The anchor, and how the grammar derives from it (07/10/2026 21:23)

Kenny approved **Out of register** as grotesk's anchor (`research/grotesk-anchor`, round two): a second, red plate arrives offset,
travels a closing spiral at an even angular pace, falls into register in the seventh of eight units, dwells and leaves as the same
fall played backwards; two plates of one print, the red under the black ink, the skeleton text as the hero; no crosshairs, no
registration mark, no rings. The clock is the anchor's: gap 2, fall 8, dwell 4, release 8 units of 120 ms (22 units, 2640 ms).
`research/grotesk-character` was built before this and has been reworked (round `2026-10-07-r2`). The reading it now proposes: **the
red plate is the one thing that moves, and where it stands says what the part is. Slipped: arriving, waiting, pointed at, changing.
In register (hidden behind the black): there, held.** What changed in the options (the numbers are the demo's questions):

- **Anchor recommended, the earlier recommendation kept as an option:** G2 arrival and G3 opening (a part is printed in black and its
  plate falls in; the set along the line and the colour bands are options 2), G9 a live update (re-registered; the train is option 2),
  G10 loading and its slip (the closing spiral; the graph's constant circle is option 2; since update 2 it turns clockwise in and out,
  §0b), G12 leaving (released: the arrival played backwards; the bands, Kenny's leave of 2026-10-04, are option 2; since update 2 the
  leave turns clockwise and is not the arrival reversed, §0b), G8 pointing and G14 focus and press (the plate falls in on a touch,
  the ring stays DI2's two channels, the press lands in the seventh unit when the plate is in register and the face goes grey with the
  black label; the baseline and the thickened rule, scope-12, are option 2).
- **Re-derived in their numbers:** G1 (the plate's even pace, dead stop and dwell; the alternatives are the same eight points held or
  eased) and G4 (120 contact, 960 a fall, 2640 a loop).
- **Dropped:** the registration mark and every cross (G11); the spinner is the plate.
- **Unchanged:** G5 colour (the red is named the second plate), G6, G7 surface, G13 warning (a standing out-of-register tone would be
  cyberpunk's standing split), G15 voice, G16 motifs, G17 composites (their states are now the plate's), G18.
- **Distinct from the others:** brutalism's offset is one still hard shadow and cyberpunk's glitch jitters cyan and red copies home in
  ticks; here one red copy travels a closing spiral at an even pace, stops dead and dwells. Under reduced motion the plate is in

**What the picks overrule, and what they keep.** The anatomy promises a
theme that is simply there: "Quiet, no arrival; the page is simply there",
"nothing loops", "no skew, no clip-path, no bevel" (those it gives to phantom
and retro). Kenny's picks set it in motion all the same: the signature cuts
the dialog, the toast and the tooltip in with `clip-path`, the menus are _Set
in type_ by a clip from the start, every loading picture loops, the leave
sweeps colour bands across. And they walk off the poster into the station:
the transit board four times (the tiles, the trend, the menu, the trend's
loading), the transit map, the flap board, _Timetable_ and _Express_ (the
meter), _The ticker_, _The train passes_. The two worlds meet in one idea,
which this grammar takes as the theme: **information set on a grid, running
on time**. Swiss typography was made for exactly this (the timetable, the
station sign, the map): a part is _set_ on its line and stops flush on its
column, it runs at an even pace and stops dead at its station, and what is
printed is printed in register. The picks keep the anatomy's load-bearing
parts (one red, black structure, the twelve columns, Archivo heavy and tight,
Inter, radius 0, no shadow, asymmetric columns) and break its promise of one
red that never decorates in places (outlier-7). The "no clip-path" line is
overruled by Kenny's own signature; the grammar keeps the cut where a thing
is cut (the resize, the redaction) and moves what arrives along its line.

### 0b. Clockwise always: the opposites rule is broken here, on purpose (Kenny, update 2, 07/10/2026)

Kenny's verdict on round 2 of `research/grotesk-character`, on the loading slip: "I like a closing spiral, but I feel like I only like
it going clockwise. So I know there's a rule of opposites, but maybe we can break that for this theme. Make the clockwise spiral loop,
give a couple of variations on this so I can decide"; and on the leave: "I feel like I mostly like it going clockwise. So come up with
ten variations, base them on the other step so they can be in line." So **grotesk deliberately breaks the rule that a close is its open
played backwards, for the red plate's waiting loop and for a part's leave**:

- **The red plate turns clockwise only.** In the waiting loop it turns clockwise while it closes in, dwells in register, and turns
  clockwise again while it opens out; it never turns back. A part still arrives as the anchor has it (its plate closes in clockwise), but
  it **leaves with the plate opening out clockwise**, not with the arrival played backwards, so the plate keeps one direction through a
  part's whole life.
- **How it is guaranteed.** The plate's polar position is two registered custom properties, `--gk-a` (the angle, only ever growing; screen
  y points down, so growing is clockwise) and `--gk-q` (the distance, 0 = in register), read with `cos()` and `sin()` by the plate's
  `translate` or `drop-shadow`. No keyframe lowers `--gk-a`. `research/_review/measure-motion.mjs` reports the measured turn in degrees,
  positive clockwise, and says "clockwise both ways, not mirrored on purpose" instead of flagging the leave as a mirror fault.
- **What stays mirrored.** Everything else: opening and closing a menu or dialog, an arrival and its close, hover, focus and the press
  release stay the open played backwards (the standing rule, 2026-10-05). The approved scenes of update 1 keep the old spiral (`gk-loop`,
  whose way out is its way in backwards) until Kenny's pick on the slip question is applied.
- **Rows it changes, once he picks.** G10 (the plate turns clockwise in and out), G11 (the spinner turns clockwise only; the plate
  stays the reference, five other candidates are asked), G12 (the leave turns clockwise and is not the arrival reversed; ten candidates
  are asked). The press (G14) no longer borrows the loading spiral: it looked like a loading animation.

## 1. The grotesk grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the timetable**: information set on a grid, running on time.
White paper, black type and one red; every part stands in its column, flush
left on its line, under a rule. Nothing is decorated: the grid is the
structure, the rule is the edge, the red is the signal, the type is the
picture. So everything that happens is something a typesetter or a timetable
does: what arrives **is set** along its line from the start and stops flush
on its column; what is pointed at **gets its baseline**; what is pressed
**thickens its rule**; while something waits, **its proof is out of
register**, the red plate travelling round the black until it registers;
a new value **is passed by the train**, a red mark running along its
baseline; a warning **is indexed**; what opens and what leaves **is printed
and taken off by the colour bands**. It runs at an even pace and stops dead,
the way the station clock's hand does. Nothing eases, bounces, swells,
glows, tilts or fades.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the grid is the ground, the rule
is the edge, black is the structure, red is the signal**. The twelve columns
lie under the page and under data, never under prose. A plate has no box: a
heavy ink rule along its top says where it starts. Black is every word, rule
and line. Red marks one thing at a time: what is current, what the hand is
on, what waits, the primary action. Archivo sets the headings and the
figures heavy and tight; Inter reads everything else, in sentence case.

## 6. The loading picture: out of register

Decided for the graph by Kenny on 2026-10-06 21:37 (`r5-gk-load-3`), who asked
for a grotesk-only demo of every loading element from it; grotesk has no
family pick. What the graph draws (`research/character-graph/round4-b.css`): the
ghost network in full ink, a `drop-shadow` of itself in the red 16 px off;
`r5-gk-load-3-plate` moves that red copy round the ink through eight
positions (right, down-right, down, down-left, left, up-left, up, up-right) at
an even pace (`linear`) for 78 % of 2600 ms, snaps it under the ink by 84 %,
holds it in register to 92 % while the stations press (`scale: 1.25`, 85 %),
and slips it off again; standing still, the plates stand 5 px and 4 px apart.
It is print, which is where the Swiss style was made: two plates on one
sheet, the second one not yet in register, until it is. What it draws, and
how each part of it carries to every other loading element:

1. **The proof.** The place the reading will stand (a figure's line, a
   tile's body lines, a table's rows, a chart's plot line, a meter's rule, a
   menu entry's row, a day's figure) is printed as a proof: ink bars the size
   of what will stand there, heavy and square, flush left on the column.
2. **The two plates.** Under the ink lies the same proof in the red, the
   second plate. Only the red moves; the ink stands where the reading will.
3. **The slip.** The red plate travels round the ink at an even pace, as on
   the graph, its distance scaled to the part: 3 px on a small part (a day,
   an entry, a skeleton line), 5 px on a plate's reading (a figure, rows, a
   meter), 8 px on a plot; the graph's 16 px on its own canvas.
4. **The register, and the dwell.** Once a cycle the red snaps into register
   behind the ink (the red is gone, the proof stands black and true) and
   dwells there for 4 units: the station clock's stop-to-go (G1). One cycle
   is 22 units (2640 ms), 18 travelling, 4 in register; the graph's 2600 ms
   kept within its 2 %.
5. **In place.** The proof does not travel and nothing runs along its foot:
   the slip is round the reading, not along the part.
6. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a key figure, the busy panel, a
   table, a menu entry, the chart's plot, the meter while it measures, a day):
   labels and titles stand above the proof, readable; only the reading's
   place is printed in two plates.
7. **Several proofs** (the rows of a table, the lines of a skeleton, the days
   of a week) slip one column of time apart (2 units), so the misregistration
   moves down the page like sheets through the press.
8. **The spinner** is the register mark: the printer's mark itself (a ring
   crossed by two hairlines) printed in two plates on the same clock.
9. **The busy progress bar** is its ruled track printed over its red fill,
   the fill out of register round the track on the same clock.
10. **Arrival is the last registration.** When the reading comes, the plates
    register for good and the value is printed in their place (set along its
    line, G2); a value that then changes is passed by the train (G9).
11. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the proof stands with its red plate
    a little out of register (the graph's own still pose).
12. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps its own _Out of register_ exactly
    (G18). Nothing else.
