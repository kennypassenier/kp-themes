# Cyberpunk — character

The reference for how cyberpunk looks and moves. Read the grammar before
adding or changing any cyberpunk component; the inventory, families and
outliers below it record where the decided picks (2026-09-08 … 2026-10-07)
stand against that grammar, and the numbered proposals (proposal-1 …) are the
plan to bring them in line. Nothing here is applied yet: Kenny fixes the
grammar first, question by question, in `research/cyberpunk-character` (the
titanium way, Kenny 2026-10-07 02:54: "waar jij eerst uitzoekt wat bij mekaar
past, wat niet past en dan zo voorstellen doet"; cyberpunk third in the
series after nostromo, 04:23).

Sources measured (2026-10-07): every `research/character-*/decided.json`
pick for cyberpunk resolved to its CSS (shape, loading, arrival, tone,
interaction, live, phone …) with a rule extractor over each demo's
stylesheets, and to the option's own words in the demo's `demo.js` and round
files; `css/cyberpunk-register.css` (3147 lines, layers `kp.register` and
`kp.signature`), `css/themes.css` (cyberpunk tokens, the scanline texture,
every theme's `--fx-ease`), `js/motion.js` (`themeMotion()`, reverse-close

## 1. The cyberpunk grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the netrunner's HUD over Night City**: signal yellow on the
void, cyan read-outs, blood red where something is wrong. Everything that
happens on screen is something a hacked signal does: it is **glitched in,
decoded, hunted and locked on, lit like a circuit, stuttered home, torn
out**. Nothing eases, glides, floats, grows, falls or bounces; a signal is
acquired in hard ticks, and when it is off it is off to the side.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the void is the ground, the
holo is what stands on it, the HUD is what the system draws over it**. The
ground is the near-black plate with its scanlines; every plate on it is a
holo card (a yellow neon rim, diagonal scan lines, a cut corner); the HUD is
the system's own marks laid over a part: the reticle that locks on, the mono
read-outs with their prefixes, the RGB copies of a signal that changes. This
is the reading of Kenny's family picks taken together: the holo card (shape),
the lock-on (loading), Target locked (tone), the circuit lights (hover),
Glitch in (arrival), the count-down stutter (live).

| #   | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1  | **A signal is acquired, not eased.** Every one-shot motion is a run of held poses (`steps(1, end)`), one pose per **tick of 60 ms**. What moves to a place does not travel: it appears there displaced sideways and **jitters home**, each tick's offset smaller than the last and on the other side (the stutter's 6, 4, 2, 1 px; the glitch-in's −18, 12, −8, 5, −3, 4, −1, 0 px). A change of state (a lit circuit, a colour) is one tick. A loop advances in whole ticks. No tween, no ease, no spring. Not the register's smooth curve: that is synthwave's exactly and nearly titanium's (§7).        |
| G2  | **The tear is sideways; reading runs start → end.** A glitch displaces along the inline axis only (`translate` on x, horizontal bands by `clip-path: inset(top 0 bottom 0)`); text and lines are decoded start → end; the reticle hunts from the inline end toward the start; all in logical terms (mirrored by `dir`). Nothing rises, falls, drops from the top, rains or slides in whole.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| G3  | **What opens glitches in** (Kenny's arrival family). A menu, the header's menu, the dialog, the drawer, a popover, a toast and a tooltip are torn into place: horizontal bands of the panel flash in sideways with a cyan and red split (`--accent`, `--destructive` as offset copies), then the panel locks clean, 6 ticks (360 ms). Closing tears it out, the same frames reversed. By `clip-path` bands and `translate`: never `scale`, never a slide from an edge, never a fade.                                                                                                                        |
| G4  | **Durations count ticks of 60 ms.** Contact 3 ticks (180 ms, the register's `--fx-duration`). A glitch (open, close, arrive, leave) 6 ticks (360 ms). A live change 8 ticks (480 ms, the stutter as decided). One loop 30 ticks (1800 ms, the lock-on as picked). Every duration a whole number of ticks.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| G5  | **Yellow acts and frames, cyan reads, red alarms, violet glitches** (the anatomy, kept). `--primary` is the colour of what a person does (an action, a pick, the lit circuit, focus) and of the holo's rim. `--accent` (cyan) is what the system reads out: labels, the reticle, the tooltip, a link at rest. `--destructive` only where something is wrong. `--chart-4` (violet) only as a glitch copy and in charts. A warning's line is its ink `--warning-foreground` (11.0:1 on a card), never the `--warning` plate (1.6:1). Green is a success state only. No `black` or `white` mixed into a token. |
| G6  | **Square or notched, never rounded.** `border-radius: 0`; a corner is square or cut by the notch, in two sizes: `--fx-notch` (14 px) on plates, `--fx-notch-sm` (8 px) on controls and tags. A control is cut at its end corner (bottom-end, the way it points; a back button at bottom-start), a card at its dossier corner (top-end), the three large panels (dialog, spec sheet, empty state) on the pair top-end and bottom-start. Round only for the radio. The focus ring follows the cut.                                                                                                            |
| G7  | **Every plate is a holo card** (Kenny's shape family). A plate (card, tile, key figure, panel, menu, drawer, the busy panel) is the void plate (`--card`) with a 1 px rim in the primary glowing 10 px inward and outward, and scan lines at 135° (2 px every 6 px at 6 %), cut by the notch. Controls keep the register's frame and slit. The glow belongs to rims and lines; text at rest never glows. The page keeps its static scanlines (`--fx-texture`).                                                                                                                                              |
| G8  | **Pointing lights the circuit** (Kenny's hover family). The part pointed at lights a 14 px circuit grid on its face in its own ink at 30 % (yellow on the void, ink on a yellow face), in one tick; a link gains the neon underline, drawn start → end; the button's charge runs across it once. Nothing lifts, nothing moves, siblings are untouched.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| G9  | **A change stutters home** (decided 2026-10-07 02:49). When a value changes in place, two neon copies of it, yellow right and up, cyan right and down, come home 6, 4, 2, 1 px in four ticks of 120 ms; the value stays sharp on top and never moves (`--kp-update: stutter`). On a carrier without text (a state dot, a line, a tile) copies of its own shape do the same.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| G10 | **Loading is the lock-on** (Kenny's loading family, §6). On every waiting surface a cyan reticle of four corner brackets hunts across it from the inline end in hard jumps, snaps tight around it, blinks its lock (cyan, yellow, cyan) and hunts again: 1800 ms, `steps(1)`. The content stands faint under it. No sweep, no rain, no glow running.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| G11 | **The spinner is the reticle locking on its core**: four corner brackets close on the cyan core in hard ticks, blink the lock and open again, one cycle in 1800 ms. The progress bar and the divider keep their data stream: a stream is data moving through a wire, the reticle is the system looking.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| G12 | **Leaving is torn out, arriving is glitched in.** What leaves is torn into horizontal bands that jump sideways with the red and cyan copies, smaller each tick, and is gone on the sixth (360 ms, `steps(1)`); what arrives plays that backwards (reverse-close), which is G3's glitch-in, so opening, arriving and leaving are one picture.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| G13 | **A warning is locked as a target** (Kenny's tone family). The reticle's corner brackets, 2 px, in the tone's ink (`--destructive`, `--warning-foreground`) frame a warning or failed part, with a mono tag at its end (TARGET, CAUTION); they twitch twice as they lock and then hold. The change sits on a square mono chip with the package's arrows. Never a full frame, never a hazard band, never a jolt of the part.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| G14 | **A press closes the circuit.** The pressed part's grid lights full (60 %) and its ground takes the `-active` token, for as long as it is held; the part does not move (titanium's press drops 1 px, §7). Focus is DI2's two-channel ring drawn inset, so the notch can never cut it, with the circuit lit as under the pointer.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| G15 | **The machine talks in prefixed mono capitals.** Labels, tags, table heads, timestamps and tooltips: KP Tech Mono, capitals, tracked 0.12–0.14 em, with the diegetic prefix (`///`, `>`, `//`, `!!`). Figures and titles: Big Shoulders Display, condensed and upright. Prose and buttons: Rajdhani. No other face.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| G16 | **Every motif means one thing.** The reticle: the system targeting (loading, a warning, a chart's lock-on marker, the graph's lock). The RGB split: a signal changing (arrival, leave, update), never at rest. Hazard stripes: something armed (the switch's on track, the alarm's bars). Scan lines: the holo plate and the page. Tripwire dashes: a group's rule. The data stream: transfer (the bar, the divider). The notch: every part.                                                                                                                                                                |
| G17 | **An element inside a composite is the theme's own element.** A button in a header, menu, drawer, tile or alert hovers, focuses and presses exactly like cyberpunk's `.kp-button` (the circuit, the charge, the inset ring, the closed circuit); a link like its link; a menu entry like `.kp-menu__item`; a tag like the badge. A composite may add its own accent around them, never replace their hover, focus ring, press, corner or font (§4b).                                                                                                                                                        |
| G18 | **Every animation sits under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`;** the reduced pose is the finished picture (the reticle standing locked, the panel in place, the copies standing 2 px out for 1.2 s as decided), never a missing one. **The network graph is never a target** (Kenny, 2026-10-07 02:54): its shape (the holo HUD), loading (the packet race), arrival (jacked in), focus (target lock) and live update (the neon surge) stay as decided, in cyberpunk and every theme.                                                                                                                |

`--fx-ease` = `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.2, 1)` (synthwave's too), **D** the
dialog's `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.2, 1)`, **F** formal's

**O** `ease-out`, **L** linear (a tween between keyframes), **St** steps, **Sp**
a spring or a keyframe past the rest pose. Direction: S→E inline-start to
end, E→S end to start, ↔ sideways jitter, ↓ top to bottom, ↑ up, ↕ from the
centre line, ∅ in place. Verdict: **fits** the grammar, or the outlier it is
(§4).

## 6. The loading picture: the lock-on

Decided by Kenny on 2026-10-07 01:32 (research/families: loading = the menu
button): the menu's _The lock-on_ (`r3-cy-load-3`, research/character-menu
round 3) is cyberpunk's loading picture. What it draws, and how each part of
it carries to every other loading element:

1. **The reticle.** Four corner brackets, 2 px, 9 px arms, in the accent
   (cyan: the system reading), cut from one frame by four mask tiles
   (`mask-size: 9px 9px` at the four corners).
2. **Hunting.** The reticle starts narrow (3 rem) at the inline end of the
   surface and jumps toward the start in hard ticks (62 %, 40 %, 20 %, 3 %),
   widening as it goes, overshoots to the full width, then snaps tight round
   the waiting part: `mb-r3-cy-lock`, 0 % → 48 %. Logical: in a
   right-to-left page it hunts from the left.
3. **The lock.** Locked, it blinks its colour cyan, yellow, cyan, yellow …
   (54 %, 59 %, 64 % … 84 %), each held 5 % of the cycle (90 ms), never a
   luminance flash of the content: under DI5 the blink is the brackets'
   colour only.
4. **The word.** Where a line of text has room (a menu entry, the busy
   panel, a table row), a mono word at the end reads SCANNING while it hunts
   and TARGET ACQUIRED when it locks (`mb-r3-cy-lock-word`); never on a
   figure or a day.
5. **One period**: 1800 ms, `steps(1)`, 30 ticks of 60 ms; the theme's
   one loop period.
6. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a panel, a table, a menu entry, a
   key figure, the chart's plot, the meter while it measures): the reticle
   hunts across the surface's own box; the content under it stands faint
   (45 %). Skeleton lines: void grooves the reticle locks on one after
   another, 6 ticks apart.
7. **Where there is no room to hunt** (a day in the month): the reticle
   locks on the day without hunting, the days of a week one tick apart.
8. **The spinner** is the reticle at its own size, closing on its cyan core
   (G11).
9. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the reticle stands locked round the
   part, cyan, and the word reads SCANNING.
10. **Exceptions.** The progress bar and the divider keep their data stream
    (transfer, G16). The network graph keeps its packet race (G18). Nothing
    else.
