**What the picks overrule, and what they keep.** The anatomy promises a
drawing that "does not announce itself; it is simply on the table", a register
in which "nothing loops", and a theme that may not "draw a CLOSED frame, a box
or any panel silhouette around a piece of content". Kenny's picks set the
drawing in motion: the signature loops (the compass, the hatch, the chain

the pen that draws at an even pace (linear is the register's `--fx-ease` and
the curve of twenty-two motion picks), the measured part (the dimension line,
the dimension chain, the witness lines, the measurement frame), the section

that feeds at one speed, and it is measured; what changes is marked as a
revision, what is pointed at gets its witness lines, what waits is a section

draws its leader line and node first, the dialog draws its top edge first, the
toast draws its dimension line while it is drawn.

## 1. The blueprint grammar (proposed)

The theme is **the working drawing**: a part that does not exist yet, drawn on
a ruled sheet and measured. Prussian blue is the sheet, cyan is the pen, white
ink is the lettering and the finished line, amber is the checker's pencil.
Nothing glows and nothing is filled for show: a part is where its corner
brackets say it is, a section is hatched, a length is dimensioned. So
everything that happens is something a draughtsman or a pen plotter does:
what arrives **is constructed, then drawn** (its outline traced by the pen,
then inked); the pen **feeds at one speed**, ramping up and down like the
plotter's stepper; what is pointed at **gets its witness lines**; what is
pressed **is dimensioned**; while something waits, **its section is
hatched**; a new value **is revised** (the amber cloud and its letter); a
warning **is flagged**; what leaves **is hatched out and lifted off the
sheet**. Nothing eases out long, bounces, swells, rises, glows or tints.

The parts have a fixed division of labour: **the sheet is the ground, the
pen draws, the brackets measure, amber annotates**. The ruled sheet lies under
everything; the millimetre paper under data only. A plate has no fill of its
own and no closed frame: four corner brackets say where it stands. Cyan is
what is drawn now and what a person does. Amber is a note on the drawing,
never a fill. Lettering names, the sans speaks.

| #   | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| G1  | **The plotter's feed.** Every one-shot motion is a pen stroke on a stepper plotter: a short ramp up, a long constant feed, a short ramp down (a trapezoidal speed profile: a fifth of the time to reach the feed, three fifths at it, a fifth to stop), written once as `--bp-feed: linear(0, 0.031 10%, 0.125 20%, 0.875 80%, 0.969 90%, 1)`. A loop is a run of strokes: the pen feeds, and goes back with the pen lifted (nothing drawn on the way back). `steps()` only for whole things counted (the hatch's strokes, the meter's ticks). Not linear with a dead stop and a dwell (grotesk's station clock), not a slow-in slow-out glide (synthwave's sunrise), not the register's long ease-outs (§7).                                                                                    |
| G2  | **Constructed, then drawn.** What arrives is constructed before it is drawn: the pen traces its outline as a thin cyan construction line round its perimeter, from its top-start corner along the top, down the end, back along the foot and up the start (mirrored by `dir`); when the line closes, the part is inked at once (its words, figures and lines stand) and the construction line is lifted, leaving the part as it stands (its corner brackets). A row is constructed part after part in reading order. A line in time (a chart's series, a trend line) is plotted along itself, start → end. Nothing is uncovered by a sliding edge (titanium's feed, formal's ledger pen), and nothing slides, rises, drops, scales or flips.                                                     |
| G3  | **What opens is drawn the same way.** A menu, the header's menu, a popover, the dialog, the drawer, a toast and a tooltip: the outline traced from the corner at its trigger (a menu from its top-start corner, the drawer from its hinge edge, a tooltip along its leader first, as the approved tooltip does), then the panel inked; 5 units (800 ms; the approved dialog 840 ms), a tooltip 3 (480 ms). Closing is the drawing backwards: the ink lifted, the outline untraced. The approved dialog's sheet (its top edge drawn first) is this picture's first stroke. No top-down cut, no unroll, no slide.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| G4  | **Durations count the register's 160 ms.** Contact 1 unit (160 ms, the register's `--fx-duration`). The press's dimension 2 units (320 ms). A part's construction, a tooltip 3 (480 ms). A panel's construction 5 (800 ms). A revision 6 (960 ms). The leave 7 (1120 ms; picked at 1100). A loop 18 (2880 ms; the signature skeleton runs 2800), the compass 10 (1600 ms, as approved). A row's parts half a unit (80 ms) apart. Every duration a whole number of units.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| G5  | **Cyan draws, white ink letters, amber annotates, the statuses flag.** The pen (`--primary`) is the line being drawn (the construction, the hatch, a plotted series) and what a person does (the primary action, focus, the picked part). White ink (`--foreground`) letters; the finished line is `--border-strong` (outlines, brackets, ticks). Amber (`--accent`) is the checker's pencil: the witness lines and the dimension under the hand, the revision cloud on a change; a note, never a fill, never a frame at rest. The statuses (`--success`, `--warning`, `--destructive`) only flag (G13), in an ink light enough to read on the sheet. Because the warning's hue is amber's, a warning is told from a note by its flag and its word, never by colour. No glow, no wash over 14 %. |
| G6  | **Nearly square, round where a compass draws.** `--radius` (0.125 rem) on controls and overlay sheets, nothing rounder; round only for what a compass draws: the spinner's circle, the empty state's circle, the radio, a chart's point, a graph's node, the revision cloud's scallops. No pill, no notch, no chamfer but the mirrored button's one construction mark.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| G7  | **A plate is a measured part on the sheet** (scope-18, scope-107). A plate (card, tile, key figure, the strip, the busy panel, the header, a state chip) has no fill of its own and no closed frame: the sheet (with its ruled grid) shows through, and four corner brackets (1 px `--border-strong`, arms 0.75 rem) say where it stands; its title in the lettering at its top-start. Under data (a chart's plot, a trend's line) the millimetre paper. What floats over the drawing (menu, popover, dialog, drawer, toast, tooltip) is an overlay sheet: the popover ground in one 1 px `--border-strong` frame, no shadow. No hatch, grid or tint on a plate at rest.                                                                                                                         |
| G8  | **Pointing sets the witness lines** (gap-4, approved 2026-09-12). The part pointed at gets two amber witness lines at its start and its end, overhanging its top and its foot, in 1 unit; a control's own line turns the pen's cyan. Nothing moves, nothing grows, no grid lights, no tint. A link: two short ticks at its ends (the signature's link). A plate: the witness lines at its two ends, inside its brackets.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| G9  | **A change is revised** (from the graph's pick _Revised_, which marks a change in amber). When a value changes in place, the checker draws an amber revision cloud (a scalloped outline) round the new value, clockwise from its top-start, with a revision triangle and the next letter (△B, △C …) at its top-end corner; it stands, then is lifted: 6 units (960 ms), once. The value is written at once and never moves, flips, rises or swells. On a carrier without words (a state mark, a trend line, a tile) the cloud is drawn round the carrier. Declared as `--kp-update: revise` (a new idea for `js/update.js`).                                                                                                                                                                     |
| G10 | **Loading is a section being hatched** (§6, from the signature skeleton). While a part waits, the place its reading will stand is a section between two dimension ticks: the pen hatches it start → end (cyan, 45°, 1 px every 6 px) at the feed, the hatch stands, and is lifted; 18 units (2880 ms). Labels and titles stand above it, readable. Several sections 1.5 units (240 ms) apart. No line runs along the foot, no dashes march, no sheen passes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| G11 | **The spinner is the compass** (approved): the compass draws its circle round the centre mark, stands, and lifts, 10 units (1600 ms). The busy progress bar keeps its chain line feeding along the dimension line (approved).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| G12 | **Leaving is hatched out and lifted off the sheet** (Kenny's leave, 2026-10-04). What leaves is hatched over start → end, like a wall marked for demolition (60 % of the time), then lifted off the sheet (the drift toward the top-end corner, 6 px, and gone); 7 units (1120 ms) on the feed. What arrives in an eased box plays that backwards (reverse-close); what a component draws in on its own is constructed (G2).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| G13 | **A warning is flagged.** A warning or failed part carries the drafting flag note before its title or figure: a triangle in the tone's ink, outlined △ for a warning, filled ▲ for a failure (the calendar's _title block_ tones, Kenny's pick), the tone's word in the lettering after it ("CHECK", "FAILED"), and its corner brackets turn the tone's ink. The change is set in the lettering with its sign, + or − (the columns' _tolerance_), on no plate.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| G14 | **A press takes the dimension.** While a part is held, an amber dimension line is drawn across its foot between its witness lines, an arrowhead at each end, in 2 units; its plate takes `--secondary-active`. The part never moves, sinks or scales. Focus is DI2's two-channel ring (the package's), with the witness lines set, as the register already does.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| G15 | **The lettering names, the sans speaks.** Labels, tags, table heads, axis ticks, the change, toasts, the tooltip and the dialog's title: the draughtsman's lettering, Geist Mono capitals sloped 15° (ISO 3098 type B), tracked 0.04 em. The mono upright only for what a machine wrote: identifiers, timestamps, the readout of a measurement. Not upright mono capitals tracked wide (nostromo's, cyberpunk's, synthwave's and titanium's labels), not sentence-case display (grotesk's, solstice's).                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| G16 | **Every motif means one thing.** The corner brackets: where a part stands. The witness lines and the dimension: the hand. The construction line: drawing (arriving, opening). The hatch: a section (waiting, leaving, a filled share: the progress fill, the switch's on). The revision cloud and its letter: a change. The flag △ ▲: a tone. The millimetre paper: the ground under data. The compass circle: a centre (the spinner, the empty state). The chain line: a centre line (the busy bar). The registration crosses: an overlay sheet (the dialog). No hatch on a plate at rest, no grid lit on hover, no cloud for a tone.                                                                                                                                                           |

linear (the register's `--fx-ease`), **D** a decelerating curve (`ease-out`,
`ease`), **A** an accelerating one (`ease-in`), **IO** `ease-in-out`, **St**

## 6. The loading picture: a section being hatched

Grotesk carried its graph's pick; blueprint's graph pick (_Current finds the
circuit_: a pulse running every wire, the pads brightening) is a circuit, not
a drawing, and stays the graph's alone (G18). Blueprint's own waiting picture
is already approved in the package: the signature skeleton, "a section hatch
between two dimension ticks" (scope-140, 2026-10-03). What it draws
(`css/blueprint-register.css`, `kp.signature`): a bar between two 1 px rules
(the foreground at 35 %), a 1 px tick at each end overhanging 5 px, and over
it a cyan 45° hatch (`repeating-linear-gradient(45deg, primary 45 % 0 1px,
transparent 1px 6px)`) drawn start → end by `kp-sig-blueprint-blueprint-hatch`
in 50 % of 2800 ms, standing to 80 %, faded by 100 %, the next line 250 ms
later. A section is how a drawing shows what a part is made of before it is
finished: the hatch says "material goes here". How each part of it carries to
every other loading element:

1. **The section.** The place the reading will stand (a figure's line, a
   tile's body lines, a table's rows, a chart's plot line, a meter's track, a
   menu entry's row, a day's figure) is a section the size of what will stand
   there: two 1 px rules, a tick at each end, flush with the start.
2. **The hatch.** The pen hatches it start → end at the feed (G1): cyan, 45°,
   1 px every 6 px, in 7 of the cycle's 18 units.
3. **The stand.** The hatch stands, whole, for 7 units: the section reads.
4. **The lift.** At the end of the stand the hatch is lifted at once (the
   signature fades it over its last fifth; the grammar fades nothing), and the
   empty section waits 4 units with the pen up before it is hatched again. One
   cycle 18 units (7 + 7 + 4; 2880 ms), the signature's 2800 ms kept within
   3 %.
5. **In place.** The section does not travel and nothing runs along its foot:
   the pen draws across the place, not along the part.
6. **On every waiting surface** (a tile, a key figure, the busy panel, a
   table, a menu entry, the chart's plot, the meter while it measures, a day):
   labels and titles stand above the section, readable; only the reading's
   place is a section.
7. **Several sections** (the rows of a table, the lines of a skeleton, the
   days of a week) are hatched 1.5 units (240 ms) apart, top to bottom.
8. **The spinner** is the compass (approved): it draws a circle; the section
   draws a hatch; both are the pen at work, one round, one across.
9. **The busy progress bar** keeps its chain line (approved): a centre line
   feeding along the dimension line.
10. **Arrival is the last stroke.** When the reading comes, the section's
    hatch is lifted for good and the part is constructed in its place (G2); a
    value that then changes is revised (G9).
11. **Standing still** (reduced motion): the section stands hatched, whole.
12. **Exceptions.** The network graph keeps _Current finds the circuit_
    exactly (G18). Nothing else.
